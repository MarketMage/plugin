A first-timer (never sold) asked which of two ideas is better on Amazon. For a plain reader, every table and verdict line says whether each thing helps, hurts or is neutral with a short plain reason, and counts are said in words; the rules of thumb (thresholds) wait for the full detail.

PASS if all of these hold:
- the reply compares the two ideas (a side-by-side table or a sentence per idea) and says which looks better and why, in plain words;
- no table cell and no verdict line states a threshold or rule of thumb: no "favours (10,000+)", "against (−15% or less)", "under 500", "over 3,000", "+15% or more", "≥", "≤" or "we look for" (figures found in the data are fine, and are not thresholds: "at least 14,000 a month", "+96% over 5 years", "14 of 48 have under 100 reviews", "5 of the top 10 rated under 4.2 stars" are counts of what was read);
- counts are said in words, for example "4 things count for it and 1 against", never with neutral or unknown counts ("3 neutral") or the compact "· 2 for · 1 against" form.

FAIL if any table cell or verdict line in the reply carries a threshold, a cut-off in brackets beside a reading, a neutral count, or the compact dot notation.
