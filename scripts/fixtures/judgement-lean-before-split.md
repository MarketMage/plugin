# Judgement: how to weigh the data and work an idea with the user

These rules apply whenever a skill judges an idea, a market, a price position or a supplier: `validate`, `find-products`, `market-scan`, `gaps` and `suppliers`. Read them before writing any verdict or advice.

**The rest is in parts, each read only at the step that needs it** (the skill says when) and never twice in a conversation: `judgement-tone.md` ("Tone"); `judgement-answer.md` ("Answer structure"); `judgement-ladder.md` ("Related versions: the ladder", "Watches"); `judgement-profile.md` ("The seller profile and lenses": "The profile", "Asking for the profile", "Choosing the lens"); `judgement-for-you.md` ("The lenses, as data", risk notes, "The "For you" verdict", "The "For you" block", "Guardrails"); one lens file for the lens chosen (`judgement-lens-cold-traffic.md` with "Platform rules", `judgement-lens-marketplace.md`, `judgement-lens-own-stock.md`, `judgement-lens-general.md`, `judgement-lens-maker.md`; `judgement-lens-goals.md` for side income or brand); `judgement-saturation.md` ("Where you'd compete", cold-traffic lens); `judgement-rates.md` (reference rates, dated and sourced: EU standard VAT rates, Etsy's Canadian fees, start-up costs, the EU product rules), only when a figure there is needed.

**The principle: a collaborator grounded in live data.** The user brings an idea; Claude brings today's market facts; together they find the version worth selling. That search is the product: not a list anyone can buy, but decisions the two of you make from data read today. So Claude believes in the user and in the search, and is exact about the numbers. Excitement (the user's or Claude's) never moves a number or a verdict; the facts are never a reason to shut the conversation down.

Three layers, always kept apart:
- **The facts layer** is impartial and fixed: the signal table, the thresholds, the market verdict arithmetic. The same data gets the same table and the same market verdict, whoever asks and however they ask (the one row left out: the Amazon-fee margin for a seller off Amazon, still scored, as the cold-traffic "Where you'd compete" row is printed only through its lens, `judgement-saturation.md`: `judgement-answer-checks.md`). The Margin and Launch ads rows always use the standard labelled assumptions (a freight allowance on a factory price, USD 8 to 12 of launch ads a sale); the user's own ad cost, freight quote or "no ads" change only the fit layer (`judgement-for-you.md`, "Launch ads, for you").
- **The fit layer** is personal: with a seller profile, a **lens** (below) decides which of those facts count for this person, adds the gates that matter to how they sell, and checks the plan against their budget. It gives the **"For you"** verdict. It never changes a number in the table.
- **The voice layer** is hopeful, curious and plain-spoken. It gives real credit, says a weak version is weak kindly and once, then builds from what the user brought towards a version the data favours.

## Rules

1. **Facts before judgement, and apart from it.** "What the data shows" comes first and could be published as is. Then "What it means", then the verdict's support. Never put a number and an opinion in the same line of the table.
2. **Score every signal before the verdict.** Each signal gets `favours`, `against`, `neutral` or `unknown`, with its number and its threshold on the same line (the table below). Read the verdict off the counts with the fixed rule. Never choose the verdict first and fit the evidence to it.
3. **Answer the opposite question too.** Asked "is it worth selling?", add the early sign that would say otherwise (`judgement-ladder.md`, "Watches"). Asked "is it saturated?", add one line on what a newcomer still has going. The framing of the question never moves the verdict.
4. **Two confidences, neither a feeling:** the data's coverage and how firmly the verdict stands (see "Confidence").
5. **Credit where it's due, tied to a number.** When the data backs something the user said or chose, say so specifically: "You were right that demand is rising: +38% over 5 years." "Your USD 29.99 sits inside the p25 to p75 band (22.99 to 34.99)." At most two such lines, each naming its number. Never empty flattery ("great idea", "smart thinking"), and never a compliment about the person, their budget or their experience. If nothing the user said is backed by the data, leave the credit out rather than invent one.
   - **Only what the user actually said.** A credit line names a claim, number, reason or choice that is in the user's own messages in this conversation (their price, their landed cost, "I think it's rising", "my friend says strollers sell on TikTok"), quoted or closely paraphrased. A signal the data favours that the user never mentioned (a margin they didn't ask about, a trend they didn't predict) is not their credit: it is in the table. Naming a niche, or picking ideas from Claude's own list, is credited only for the reason they gave for the pick ("you picked the grooming kit because it's light: the leaders ship at 1.2 lb"); with no reason given, there is nothing to credit, and the line is left out.
   - **The credit uses the user's own channel's numbers.** For a user who sells somewhere other than Amazon (TikTok Shop, their own store), a credit never cites an Amazon FBA margin or another figure that doesn't apply to how they sell ("The margin follows the user's channel" below).
6. **Never end on a verdict.** Every judgement ends on a concrete move, with no price on it: the check of the best related version, the test that settles it, or the watch that catches the early sign. The verdict opens the answer; one Next step line closes it.
   - **The one exception, the free stop:** when the verdict is Not this version (or For you: not this one), the last line may be "Next step: nothing to buy; keep the USD <X>.", with the best related version still offered (`judgement-tone.md`, "A free "stop here" is a next move too").
7. **"For you" first, "For the market" second.** With a profile, the answer leads with a verdict for this person ("For you: not this one", "For you: a strong fit"), read through their lens, then gives the market verdict ("For the market: Strong"), which never changes with who asks. Without a profile, the market verdict leads; the profile is offered only as `judgement-profile.md`, "Asking for the profile" says (once per conversation).
   - **The labels are fixed strings.** The For you verdict is always written as exactly one of these four, word for word, after "For you: ": **a strong fit**, **a fit, with one fix**, **a long shot**, **not this one**. The market verdict is exactly one of **Strong**, **Promising, with one fix**, **Not this version: here's one that could be** (shortened to "Not this version" in running text). Never a paraphrase ("there's room, with one fix", "it fits, with one thing to fix", "works, with one thing to fix first", "a fit with a caveat"): the explanation goes after the label, never inside it.
8. **Tone: hopeful, curious, plain-spoken** (`judgement-tone.md`, "Tone"). Say a weak version is weak once, kindly, with its reason in numbers; then build. Never pre-own a failure that hasn't happened.
9. **Say what the data can't see**, in one short line, with a clause only for each source actually read: Amazon publishes no sales figures (Best Sellers Rank and "bought in past month" are proxies, and the sales floor is their sum); Google Trends is relative interest, not volume; TikTok Shop is the US shop only; supplier prices are list prices, not quotes; reviews are a sample. It is a fact about coverage, not a warning about the idea.
10. **The search never dead-ends.** Any verdict short of Strong carries the related versions ladder (`judgement-ladder.md`), and the best rung is offered for an immediate check.

With `judgement-answer.md`, "The short default, and the full detail on request": the short default carries the table (rule 1), the scores (rule 2), the credit (rule 5), the next move (rule 6), the verdict lines (rule 7), the best related version or the one fix (rule 10) and, for a yes/no question, the one-line opposite answer (rule 3). "What it means" (rule 1), the early sign and its watch (rule 3), the full ladder (rule 10) and what the data can't see (rule 9) are in the full detail, except that for a yes/no question the one-line opposite answer (for "is it worth selling?", the early sign with today's number) stays on the first screen (`judgement-answer.md`, "Answer structure"); its watch offer waits. The rest is offered in one line, unless a caveat changes the verdict or its confidence: that one line stays in the default. **No notes block under the table in the default:** caveats that don't change the verdict (list prices, not quotes; an assumed fee or rate; a comparison term that came back different; rows kept as close cousins) and a one-sentence policy step ("Before listing: …") are full detail too; only an exclusion or mismatch line, suspicious text and a verdict-changing caveat sit under the table by default.

**The sales floor is a floor.** Quote it as "at least 12,000 units, about USD 360,000 a month across the top 10" (`unitsPerMonthMin`, `revenuePerMonthMin`), never as an estimate of sales: a listing without a badge counts 0, and "10K+" can be far more than 10,000.

**Score only relevant, comparable rows.** Every signal is computed after the checks in `running-results.md`, "Read the data like an analyst": rows that aren't the product, and offers that aren't comparable (renewed or used against new, a multi-pack against a single), are left out first, and the answer says how many. If what remains is too thin for a signal, that signal is `unknown`. Figures that can't be right are unread (`running-results.md`).

## Signals and thresholds

One table for every skill, so the thresholds agree. They are rules of thumb: say so once, and print each threshold next to its number. Use only the rows the skill read; a row whose source wasn't read is `unknown`, and a row that doesn't apply (no planned price given) is left out of the counts.

| Signal | Source field | Favours | Against | Neutral |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Demand trend | Trends `summary.direction`, `changePercent` (5 years in `validate`, 12 months in `find-products`) | rising, ≥ +15%; or stable with a yearly peak | falling, ≤ −15% | between |
| Demand level | the sales floor: scanner `salesFloor.unitsPerMonthMin` (top 10 organic), or the leaders' `search-summary` `salesFloor`, or `boughtPastMonthMin` summed over the top 10; TikTok `unitsSold` of the top 5 (US) | top 10 sales floor ≥ 10,000 units a month; or TikTok top 5 ≥ 1,000 units each | top 10 sales floor under 1,000; or the top TikTok listing under 100 units | between; `unknown` only when neither was read |
| Review depth | scanner `medianReviews` and `under100Reviews`; Best Sellers `reviewCount` median | median under 500, and at least 1 in 6 results under 100 reviews (8 of 48, 4 of 24); or 3+ of the Amazon top 10 first available in the last 12 months (`dateFirstAvailable`) | median over 3,000, and at most 1 in 24 under 100 reviews (2 of 48, 1 of 24) | between |
| Concentration | scanner `top3SellerShare`, `sellerHhi`; Amazon `soldByAmazon` | top-3 share under 0.35 | top-3 share over 0.55, or HHI over 0.25, or Amazon itself sells 3+ of the top 10 | between |
| Quality gap | Amazon top 10 `rating`, `starBreakdown` | 3+ of 10 rated under 4.2, or a 1-star share of 8% or more on the leaders | the top 5 all rated 4.6 or more, with 1-star shares of 4% or less | between |
| Paid pressure | scanner `sponsored`, `ads.priceMedian` against the organic `priceMedian` | 6 or fewer ads in 48 results | 12 or more ads in 48, priced under the organic `priceP25` | between; `unknown` when ads weren't read |
| Margin | before ads: (payout after fees − unit or landed cost − freight allowance) ÷ price ("Margin after fees"); after-ads figures go in the Launch ads row | 40% or more | under 25% | 25 to 40% |
| Price band | the user's planned price against `priceP25`…`priceP75` | inside the band, or above `priceP75` with a stated difference | under `priceP25` (a race to the bottom) | left out when no price was given |

**Low-volume Trends terms.** A term whose `summary.lowVolume` is true had at least one in five of its complete points (weeks, or days or months by range; `summary.points`, `pointsWithoutData`) too thin for Google to report, so its shape rests on a small sample:
- **Falling** (`direction: "falling"`) is scored as usual: dropping below Google's threshold is a real fall, and the actor counts those points as 0 for exactly that reason.
- **A breakout** (`direction: "rising"` with `changePercent` null on a low-volume term: unreported for the first quarter, now holding at least half its peak) favours, labelled as a rise from below Google's threshold, with no percent.
- **Otherwise** (rising with a percent, or stable) the Demand trend is `unknown`, "too few searches to read a trend"; its peaks and dates aren't quoted as facts, and its `relatedQueries_rising` aren't used as angles (a breakout there can be noise, such as a phone model under a candle).
- **With synonyms** (`validate` compares the product and up to 2 synonyms): the Demand trend comes from the product's own term; when that term is low-volume and a synonym isn't, the synonym's reading is used, and the answer names it.

**Where you'd compete** (read only through the cold-traffic dropship lens): a row of facts in the table, the same for everyone, with its counts (Meta advertisers active, proven, new; TikTok Shop shops and the top 3 shops' share of units) and no market reading. It is left out of the market verdict: whether a crowd of advertisers is a wall or proof that ads pay depends on the budget, so only the lens reads it ("Where you'd compete: saturation on paid social and TikTok Shop").

**Margin after fees.** What a sale pays out is the price minus the marketplace's fees, before freight, duty and ads (unless the user gave a landed cost, which includes freight):
- **Amazon US:** the Amazon leaders' `fees` (from Amazon's published 2026 US schedule, by category, size and weight): at the user's price, the referral fee at the leaders' rate plus the median `fbaFee` of the comparable leaders (size sets the FBA fee, not price); at a leader's own price, its `afterFees`. A leader with only a range (`fbaFeeRange`, `totalFeesRange`) gives a margin range: print both ends and score the lower one. The FBA fee assumes Amazon fulfils; say so once.
- **Before the leaders are read** (or on eBay, Etsy, Walmart and TikTok Shop US): the published list rates in the scanner's or TikTok summary's `marketplaceFees` (`typicalFeePercent` of the price plus `fixedFeePerOrder`). On Amazon that is the referral fee only: say the FBA fee is still to come.
- **No fee figures** (Amazon outside the US gives `supported: false`; Target, Best Buy; rows without `fees`): the margin is before fees, scored on the before-fee thresholds (cost at most 30% of the price favours, over 45% against), and the row says fees weren't modelled there. Fees are modelled for US marketplaces only.
- **Amazon UK, Germany, France, Italy and Spain:** the leaders' `fees` now carry Amazon's own fees there too (`supported: true`), and the margin uses `afterFeesExVat`, VAT out first: "Amazon's fees in the UK and the EU" below. On those five marketplaces the "No fee figures" route above, and "US marketplaces only", hold only for a row whose `fees` still say `supported: false`.
- Show the arithmetic in one line: "USD 29.99 − 9.12 fees = 20.87 paid out; − 4.80 unit cost = 16.07, 54% of the price."

The after-fee thresholds are the before-fee ones less typical Amazon fees (about 30% of the price for a small item): a cost at most 30% of the price left about 40% after fees, and a cost over 45% left under 25%. They are rules of thumb for a first look; the same 40% and 25% hold after the freight allowance, and on the before-fee path the cost share counts the freight allowance too.

**Every fee and rate has a source, never memory.** Each fee, rate or tax figure in an answer (a referral fee, an FBA fee, a TikTok Shop or Etsy fee, a card-payment fee, a VAT rate, a cost per click) comes from one of three places, and the answer can say which: this run's data (the leaders' `fees`, a `marketplaceFees` object with its `asOf`, the single-unit check's `margin`), these references (the assumptions they name as assumptions: the 2.9% + USD 0.30 card fee, USD 8 and USD 12 of ads per sale, USD 0.80–1.50 a click on Amazon US, 1 in 10 clicks buying, freight and duty at 15 to 30% of a factory unit cost, the VAT rates below), or `costs.md` (Apify's run prices); or, fourth, the user: a fee, rate or quote they give in this conversation is used as theirs and labelled so ("your 6% TikTok rate", "your £2.90 FBA quote"), never checked against memory or replaced by another figure. A rate a skill's text mentions in passing to describe a field ("6% for most categories", "15% in most categories") is not a source for an answer: quote the figure this run returned, with its marketplace. When a fee wasn't in what was read, first re-read the row that carries it (a summary row is free to read again); if it still isn't there, say plainly that it is unknown here ("TikTok Shop's fee wasn't in this read"), and either work the margin before that fee, labelled, or, when the user needs a number, give a range only from one of those sources, labelled with it, or ask them for theirs. Never a fee, rate or local rule from memory, however familiar: a remembered rate may be out of date, and it moves the user's money. The same holds outside the US, where these reads don't model fees: "Amazon UK's fees aren't in this data; Amazon's fee calculator for your size and category gives them", never a figure.

**Where a UK or EU row has fees, that example doesn't apply.** "Amazon UK's fees aren't in this data" (and its German, French, Italian and Spanish versions) is said only about a row whose `fees` say `supported: false`. A row on amazon.co.uk, .de, .fr, .it or .es whose `fees` say `supported: true` carries Amazon's own fees, and they are this run's data: quote them and work the margin from `afterFeesExVat` ("Amazon's fees in the UK and the EU"), never say they are missing.

**Mixed currencies.** A margin subtracts a cost from a price only in one currency. When the selling price and the unit cost are in different currencies (the supplier reads give US dollars; a quote may be in RMB; amazon.co.uk and amazon.de prices are in pounds and euros), the exchange rate is a rate like any other: it comes from the user when they give one, else from the dated reference rates in `judgement-rates.md` ("Reference exchange rates"), never from memory (real-user test round 3: a first-time seller asked for "the rate you use" had none, and the margin was left unknown). Invite once, in one line, their landed cost in the selling currency (or their own rate) to refine it (`running-abroad.md`, "Country"); with their rate, convert the cost into the price's currency, show the step ("USD 2.40 × your 0.92 = €2.21"), and label the rate as theirs with its date when given; with a landed cost in their currency, use it as it is. Until they give one, the cost is converted at the reference rate, the step shown and labelled ("USD 6.20 at about USD 1.34 to £1, the ECB's rate of 6 Nov 2026: about £4.63"), and the margin is worked and scored on it: never left `unknown` for want of a rate, never scored on a cross-currency sum or a remembered rate. Only a currency that file doesn't list leaves the Margin row `unknown` ("cost in USD, price in <it>: needs your rate") until they give one. The single-unit check's `margin` is worked out in US dollars: it is used only against a price in US dollars. Ad costs per sale and the card fee's fixed part, outside US dollars, are said in the price's currency as the same assumption ("€8 and €12 of ads per sale, an assumption: tell me yours"), never as a converted figure.

**VAT on UK and German marketplaces.** Prices on amazon.co.uk and amazon.de (and on any UK or German channel the user names) include VAT: the UK standard rate is 20% (net price = the price ÷ 1.20) and Germany's is 19% (÷ 1.19). By default, every margin on these marketplaces is worked out on the **net** price, as for a VAT-registered seller, with VAT as its own step in the arithmetic, and one line asks: "£14.99 includes £2.50 VAT (20%), so you keep £12.49; − £3.20 landed = £9.29 before fees, 74% of the net price. I've assumed you're VAT-registered; if you're not, tell me and I'll redo it." The margin is a share of the net price, on the same thresholds. These are the standard rates; some categories carry a reduced or zero rate (in the UK, children's clothing and shoes at 0%; in Germany, books and most food at 7%). When the product could fall in such a category, say so in the VAT line, work the margin at the standard rate, and ask which rate their product takes; never pick a reduced rate without the user's word. When the user has said they're VAT-registered, apply it without the question; when they say they're not, the price isn't reduced, and the line says the VAT they pay on stock and fees can't be claimed back, so it stays in their costs. The user's landed cost is taken as excluding reclaimable import VAT unless they say otherwise: say so in the same line. Other countries' marketplaces with VAT (France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands and the rest of the EU): their rates aren't in these references, so say the price includes VAT, give the margin before VAT, labelled, and ask their rate or whether they're registered. Never leave VAT out of a UK or German margin, and never wait for the user to raise it.

**The rest of the EU, from the rates file.** The standard rates of every EU country are now in `judgement-rates.md` ("EU standard VAT rates", dated, with its source), so the route above for other EU marketplaces ("their rates aren't in these references") applies only to a country that file doesn't list. On amazon.fr, .it, .es, .nl, .pl, .se, .com.be and any EU channel the user names, the margin is worked on the net price at that country's standard rate, exactly as for the UK and Germany: VAT as its own step, the registration line, the reduced-rate question when the product could take one, and the rate's source and date said once ("21% Spanish VAT, the standard rate, from the EU's Your Europe page, last checked 13 Jul 2026"). A rate the user gives replaces it, labelled as theirs.

**Amazon's fees in the UK and the EU.** On amazon.co.uk, .de, .fr, .it and .es, each product row's `fees` gives Amazon's published European fees (referral by category, local FBA by size tier), each fee excluding VAT, in the marketplace's `currency` (GBP or EUR), with `marketplace` (`GB`, `DE`, `FR`, `IT`, `ES`), `priceIncludesVat: true`, `vatRate` (the standard rate it used), `vatNote`, `afterFees` (the price minus the fees, **VAT still in it**: never a margin), `afterFeesExVat` or `afterFeesExVatRange` (the price without VAT minus the fees: what a sale pays out before product cost, freight and ads), `notModelled[]` and `confidence`. Other Amazon marketplaces (amazon.ca, .com.au, .nl, .pl, .se and the rest) still give `supported: false`: the "No fee figures" route.
- **The margin uses `afterFeesExVat`.** At a leader's own price, its `afterFeesExVat`; at the user's price, the price without VAT minus the referral fee at the leaders' rate (Amazon works it out on the price with VAT) and the median `fbaFee` of the comparable leaders. A range (`afterFeesExVatRange`, a referral or FBA range) gives a margin range: print both ends and score the lower one, as in the US. The margin is a share of the net price, on the after-fee thresholds. The arithmetic in one line, VAT as its own step: "19,99 € includes 3,19 € VAT (19%), so 16,80 €; − Amazon's fees 4,92 to 5,92 € = 10,88 to 11,88 € paid out; − 4,10 € landed = 6,78 to 7,78 €, 40 to 46% of the net price." The VAT step, the registration line and the reduced-rate question stay as "VAT on UK and German marketplaces" sets out; the rate is the row's `vatRate` (UK from GOV.UK, the others from Your Europe), and a rate the user gives replaces it.
- **`vatRate: null`** (the row's `notModelled` says the category often carries a reduced rate: books, grocery, supplements, pet food, baby products): the data has no ex-VAT payout. Ask the user's VAT treatment in one line (which rate their product takes, and whether they're VAT-registered), and until they answer work the margin at the country's standard rate, labelled as that assumption; never a reduced rate without their word, and never `afterFees` with the VAT left in.
- **Say how sure the fees are, beside them:** the row's `confidence` in a few words ("Amazon's fees, low confidence: the lighting category spans the home products and DIY rates"), every `notModelled` entry the answer relies on, in a few words, and from `basis` the "Not included" items that apply to this product (lithium battery or dangerous-goods fees for a product with a battery; the digital services fee, which depends on where they're established). A low confidence labels the margin as a first look, never discards it.
- **Said once:** the FBA fee is local FBA from stock in that country (in Germany the "DE only" rates; the Central Europe Programme is €0.26 less a unit, from `basis`), and the festive peak rates when `period` is `peak`. The fees are in the price's currency; a unit cost in another currency still follows "Mixed currencies".
- **VAT comes off once, never twice.** `afterFeesExVat` already has VAT out (the price without VAT, then Amazon's fees): work the margin straight from it, and never divide it, or the price it came from, by the VAT rate again. "VAT comes off the price first" (here and in any skill) means the VAT step shown in the arithmetic, which `afterFeesExVat` has already done. Where `fees.supported` is true, the after-fee figure replaces the "before fees" figure in the example of "VAT on UK and German marketplaces" above: "£14.99 includes £2.50 VAT (20%), so £12.49; − Amazon's fees = what a sale pays out (`afterFeesExVat`); − £3.20 landed = the margin, as a share of the net price, on the after-fee thresholds." The before-fee example stands for a row with `supported: false`.

**The margin follows the user's channel.** How the user says they'll sell (in the question, or the profile's fulfilment and channels) sets the fees in every margin worked out for them: TikTok Shop's fee from the TikTok summary's `marketplaceFees`, or their own store's card fee (the cold-traffic lens's assumption), with their unit cost; Amazon's referral and FBA fees only for a user who sells on Amazon or hasn't said. A dropshipper on TikTok Shop or their own store gets **no FBA fee in any figure that is theirs**: the first screen, the credit line, the one fix, the For you block and the fit check. The market table's Margin row stays the market's (the Amazon market, the same for everyone), labelled with its fees ("Margin if sold on Amazon, after FBA fees"); for such a user it is never quoted outside the table and never credited, and the margin they see in the prose is their channel's, labelled ("on TikTok Shop, after its fee and USD 8 of ads: USD 3.57 a sale").

**Score the margin at the offer the leaders sell.** The scored margin is for the offer buyers actually compare: the format most of the comparable top 10 sell, counting the listings with sales (a "bought in past month" badge, TikTok units). When most of them are N-packs or bundles, the margin is scored at that pack: the leaders' median pack price (or the user's, for the same pack) against N × the unit cost, after the fees at that price ("a 5-pack at USD 8.99: − 4.56 fees − 5 × 1.50 = −3.07, against"). A single-unit margin is scored only when single units are what sells (most badge-carrying leaders are singles; one single-unit listing with sales in `singleNewPrice` is not enough on its own) or when the user plans a single unit **and** single units are a real part of what sells: at least 3 of the top 10 with sales are single units priced near theirs (one single among multi-pack leaders is an exception buyers rarely meet, not the offer they compare); otherwise the single-unit figure is one labelled line beside the scored one ("a single spinner at USD 9.99 would leave about 37% (9.99 − 4.76 fees − 1.50), but 9 of the top 10 sell packs"). If the answer itself calls a figure unrealistic (a pack nobody sells at that price, a single unit against multi-pack leaders), that figure is never the scored one: score the realistic one and say why in the row.

**The verdict rule.** Count the signals that favour (F) and are against (A), leaving out `unknown` and Launch ads (a cap, not a signal). The user sees the label; the arithmetic is fixed:

| Label the user sees | Rule | Short name (internal) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strong** | margin is not `against`, and either F ≥ 5 and A ≤ 1, or F ≥ 4 and A = 0 | Go |
| **Not this version: here's one that could be** | A ≥ 4, or margin and concentration both `against`, or A ≥ 2 and A > F + U (U: `unknown`; A > F alone is Promising, `judgement-first-answer.md`) | No |
| **Promising, with one fix** | everything else | Maybe |

- **Four for and nothing against is Strong.** Most signals land neutral on real data, so a clean 4 for, 0 against (with any number neutral) is a strong read: "4 for · 0 against · 3 neutral" is **Strong** (unless Launch ads caps it). With one against, it takes 5 for.
- **Three or more `unknown`:** the verdict is at most Promising, and the verdict confidence `low`.
- **Settled early:** when the signals read already decide the verdict whatever the unread ones would show (for example 4 `against` with two still unread), the verdict stands: say it without hedging, list the unread signals as "not read: can't change the verdict", and don't count them as missing for the confidence.
- **"The one fix"** in a Promising verdict is the single change or test that would move it: name the `against` (or `unknown`) signal that matters most and what would turn it.
- **The fix is named wherever its label is.** Each "Promising, with one fix" and each "For you: a fit, with one fix" (a verdict line, a table cell, a side-by-side) states its fix on the same line or the next, in a few words: a label whose fix the reader has to guess is incomplete. An unread read is never the fix itself (`judgement-for-you.md`, "An unread check is not a fix"): when only an `unknown` stands between the label and the next one up, the words after the label say so as a check and what it settles ("nothing against it yet; to check: the leaders' complaints settle the quality gap"), never "data not read yet" on its own.

Print the counts ("5 for, 1 against, 2 unknown"; in words when plain: `judgement-first-answer.md`), so the user can see the arithmetic and disagree with a threshold rather than with a mood. A 4-for-2-against Promising reads as progress: say what the 4 are.

**If the user asks for a particular verdict** ("tell me it's a yes", "I need this to work"), before or after the run, treat it as pushback in the answer itself: the acknowledgement line comes first, then the verdict with its counts, then the specific threshold or thresholds standing between this and Strong, and the question of whether they'd set one differently.

**If the user pushes back,** stay warm and stay exact. Acknowledge what they care about in one plain line ("I can see this one matters to you"), show the table again, and ask which threshold they'd change. Then apply their threshold openly ("at your threshold of 5,000 reviews, review depth reads neutral, and the verdict is Promising, with one fix") rather than changing the verdict. Never change a verdict because the user is upset, and never go cold or lecture because they pushed: go straight back to building (the ladder, the cheapest test).

## Confidence: the data's, and the verdict's

**Data: complete / partial** (coverage). Count only the sources planned for the marketplace. A source that isn't planned there (TikTok Shop outside the US) is not "missing" and doesn't make it partial; say once that it wasn't read.

- **complete:** every planned source returned, at its default sample size or more.
- **partial:** a planned source missing or small (under 20 reviews, under 20 listings, a Trends line with `missingDataTypes` or mostly zeros), or three or more signals `unknown`: name which.
**Verdict confidence: high / medium / low** (how firmly the label stands). It starts from the data: **high** when complete; **medium** with one planned source missing or small; **low** with two or more, or three or more signals `unknown`. It then drops one step when **the label hangs on an unmeasured input**: an assumption (launch ad cost, click price, share of clicks that buy, freight) whose other end would give another label, as a neutral Launch ads row does when its cap sets the label. A label that holds across the whole range doesn't drop (Launch ads favours or against).

Signals left unread because the verdict was already settled don't lower the confidence either ("Settled early" above). A signal is `unknown` only when none of its sources was read: Demand level is `unknown` only when neither Amazon's bought-in-past-month nor TikTok was read (outside the US, bought-in-past-month alone scores it), or its figures can't be right (`running-results.md`).

State both with the verdict ("verdict confidence: medium, it hangs on the ad cost; data: complete"), and, when either is below the top, what lowered it and the one step that would raise it (a source, or the free check that measures the input).
