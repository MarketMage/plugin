# Judgement: the cold-traffic dropship lens

Part of the judgement rules (`judgement.md` is the core). Read with `judgement-for-you.md` when the lens is cold-traffic dropship. The saturation reading ("Where you'd compete") is in `judgement-saturation.md`: read it when the Where you'd compete read is made or offered.

#### Platform rules and risk notes

**The policy gate fails only when a named platform prohibits the product for the user's channel,** and the answer names the platform and its rule ("Meta's Advertising Standards prohibit ads for vapes"). These are the platform rules, as published (read 2026-10-24):
- **Meta ads (Facebook, Instagram):** Advertising Standards, https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/ : weapons, ammunition or explosives; tobacco and related products (e-cigarettes and vapes included); illicit or recreational drugs and other unsafe substances; adult nudity and sexual products; hazardous goods and materials; sale of human body parts; endangered species; prohibited documents and financial services. Health and wellness, alcohol, pharmaceuticals and some others are restricted (allowed with targeting and claim limits), not prohibited.
- **TikTok ads:** Dangerous Products or Services (updated April 2025), https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-dangerous-products-or-services : weapons, ammunition and explosives (blades, fireworks, self-defence weapons; toys may be allowed); police or military gear; tobacco and nicotine (e-cigarettes, vape pens, nicotine pouches, smoking paraphernalia); illegal, controlled and prescription drugs and drug paraphernalia. Index: https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-advertising-policies-industry-entry (updated August 2025).
- **Counterfeits, on every ad channel** (read 2026-11-03): Meta's Advertising Standards, Copyrights and Trademarks, https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/intellectual-property-infringement/copyright-and-trademarks/ : ads can't "promote or sell counterfeits, knockoffs, or replicas of another company's products"; TikTok's ad policy on Intellectual Property Infringement, https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-intellectual-property-infringement : no counterfeit goods. The gate fails only when the listing text or the user says the product is a copy of another company's branded product: "replica", "fake", "1:1", "AAA", "mirror quality", or another brand's name or logo on a product that brand didn't make, presented as that brand's own product. "Dupe", "inspired by" or "alternative to <brand>" alone is a risk note (`judgement-for-you.md`, "Platform rules and risk notes"), not a gate: an unbranded product of the same type is a different product. A brand named only as what the product fits or works with ("for iPhone 15", "fits Stanley 40 oz", "compatible with AirPods", "works with Dyson V8", "replacement for <brand> filters") is an accessory or part, not a copy: no gate and no risk note for it.

A product passes the policy gate when its listing text names none of these for the user's channel; **unclear** only when a title names something close and the rule's wording doesn't settle it (say which rule, and that the platform's review decides). A rule not listed here is not a gate: never fail a product on a remembered or assumed policy.

The risk notes ("Everything else is a risk note") are in `judgement-for-you.md`, "Platform rules and risk notes".

**Cold-traffic dropship** (ads to their own store or TikTok Shop; supplier ships). This lens is built to let products through: a product is "not this one" only when it **cannot work at all** for this way of selling, and every other finding is information the user weighs.

| Row | For you | Reads from |

| Search-led demand | **added**: a top-10 Amazon sales floor of 10,000+ units a month is **one caution line** ("those buyers search Amazon and can buy it there"), never `against` on its own: it reads neutral in the count, and when Felt want favours the line says the feed demand is there too. 1,000 to 10,000 is neutral. **Margin after ad cost, as a range.** (price − unit cost − channel fee − ad cost per sale) ÷ price, at **USD 8 and USD 12 of ads per sale** (an assumption, a common cold-traffic range: say so, "tell me yours and I'll redo it"). The unit cost is the AliExpress read's landed cost (`landedPrice`, only when `shippingCost` is above 0: `running-runs.md`, "AliExpress prices"), else its cheapest one-unit `price`, labelled **"plus shipping, still to add"** (shipping not established: a margin from it is a best case, said so; never "free shipping"), or a landed cost read elsewhere (a DHgate page's estimate, or the user's quote). Show both ends, said with the shipping still to add when the cost is before shipping. **The break-even ad cost per sale** (the price − the channel fee − the unit cost: the profit before ads, the same deciding number as for every seller who pays for traffic, `judgement-answer-checks.md`, "The margin and the launch ad cost") is said beside the range. The verdict confidence drops one step only when the For you label itself would change within the range (`judgement.md`, "Confidence": a label that holds across the whole range doesn't drop); a loss at USD 12 only, with gate 3 passing at USD 8, is a note and changes no label, so it doesn't drop it. **Gate 3 (below) alone decides pass or fail**, from the break-even price and the proven price ceiling, never from the sign of the margin at one price; negative only at USD 12 (with gate 3 passing) is a note (the fix: the ad cost or the price that would turn it).

**The price being judged is the user's, not Amazon's.** A cold-traffic seller sets their own price in their own store or TikTok Shop listing, so the price in the sum is the user's planned price when they gave one, otherwise the Amazon median (`priceMedian`) or the TikTok listings' typical price, labelled as such ("at Amazon's median of USD 12.99"). It is a starting point, never the only price that could work.

**The channel fee in these sums:** on TikTok Shop, the TikTok summary's `marketplaceFees` (`typicalFeePercent` plus `fixedFeePerOrder`); in their own store, a card-payment fee of 2.9% + USD 0.30 (an assumption, a common Shopify Payments rate: say so once). Never leave the fee out.

**A price in another currency** (a UK or EU store, TikTok Shop outside the US): the unit cost (AliExpress's US dollars) is never subtracted from it across currencies; it is converted at the user's rate or the dated reference rate, with their landed cost in their currency invited once (`judgement-abroad.md`, "Mixed currencies"), the ad cost per sale and the card fee's fixed part are said in the price's currency as the same assumptions (€8 and €12, € 0.30), and the sums are worked in the price's currency. Only for a currency the reference rates don't list, until the rate or landed cost comes, gate 3 is unclear as a note (its step 3: "profit: needs your rate or landed cost in euros"), never a fail.

**The break-even price at USD 8 of ads:** (unit cost + USD 8 + the fixed fee per order) ÷ (1 − the fee rate), compared with the ceiling **exactly** (USD 15.69, not 15.99), and quoted to the user rounded up to a shop price ending in .99, unless that rounded price is above the ceiling: then quote the exact break-even (never a price above what buyers are shown paying). **The proven price ceiling** is the highest price with sales behind it in this run: a top-10 Amazon leader's price with a sales floor of 100+ units a month, or a TikTok Shop listing's price with 100+ units sold, whichever is highest (name which one). Price statistics with no sales attached (`priceMedian`, `priceP75`) are never the ceiling. The user's own price ceiling, if given, caps it.

**No margin from the actor.** The AliExpress read gives prices, never a worked margin: work every sum above out yourself, for their own store and for TikTok Shop alike. Round the break-even price and the cost-free floor up to the cent (the lowest price that doesn't lose money), the channel fee and the profit to the nearest cent and the margin percentage to the nearest tenth, an exact half going against the seller; the .99 quoting rule for the break-even price above still applies on top (USD 15.69 compared with the ceiling, quoted as USD 15.99). An ad cost or fee the user gives after the run (the "tell me yours" redo): work the sums out with theirs. Gate 3's steps and their order are unchanged: these figures only feed them.

Gates (each pass / fail / unclear, with its fact). **Only these three can make "For you: not this one":**
1. **Platform policy:** a named platform rule prohibits the product for the channel they named ("Platform rules and risk notes" above: Meta for Facebook or Instagram ads, TikTok for TikTok ads or TikTok Shop).
2. **Single units ship:** read from the single-unit check, the AliExpress read (`cleanpull/aliexpress-scraper`, `running-runs.md`, "AliExpress prices"): AliExpress sells by the piece, so a `product` row matching the idea passes (so does a supplier `lowestMoqOffer` with `moq: 1`). It fails **only** when AliExpress answered with product rows and none sells the product as one unit (every row a lot or a bulk pack of it). Every other reading is **a note, never a fail, and never caps the For you verdict**:
   - **No matching listing** (the idea's `search-summary` with no `product` row: `listingsDelivered: 0`): "AliExpress showed no matching listing under this name; try another name", with one rerun of the check under a synonym offered.
   - **No answer**, in any form (an `unreadable` row for the idea, a run that failed or was stopped, or no row for the idea at all): "single units: not confirmed yet (AliExpress didn't answer in time; most consumer goods sell single units there)", with one rerun of the check offered.
   - **Not read yet** (the check hasn't run): "single units: not read yet", with the single-unit check as the next step.
   - **Still reading** (the read is still running after its wait: its product pages can take several minutes): "single units: AliExpress is slow on this one (its product pages can take a few minutes)", read exactly as not read yet, and completed in the follow-up when the run lands ("Still reading AliExpress" below).
   Print on demand: does not apply (made to order).
3. **Profit at some price buyers pay:** the margin after ad cost at the **low** ad cost (USD 8 per sale), after the channel fee, with the same unit cost as the margin range above: a landed cost when one was read (the AliExpress read's `landedPrice` with `shippingCost` above 0, a DHgate page's estimate, or the user's quote), else the cheapest one-unit `price` (plus shipping, still to add; or a supplier `lowestMoqOffer` with `moq: 1`), else the user's unit cost. The gate is read in this order, **first match wins** (the break-even price and the proven price ceiling are defined above; the user's planned price, when given, is their call). Without a read unit cost, the break-even used is the **cost-free floor**, (USD 8 + the fixed fee) ÷ (1 − the fee rate), the break-even at a unit cost of zero:
   1. **The user's planned price at or above the break-even** (the cost-free floor when the unit cost isn't read) → no fail. With a read unit cost it **passes**; when that price is above the ceiling, or no ceiling was read, add the note "buyers are shown paying up to USD <ceiling> (<which listing>); at your USD <price> the listing has to show why it's worth more" (or "no price with sales behind it was read"), never a fail or a cap. Without a read unit cost it is the unread note in 3.
   1b. **Unit cost not read, a ceiling read, and the user's planned price below the cost-free floor, with the floor at or under the ceiling** → passes, with **the price as the fix** (as in 6, so it caps at "a fit, with one fix"): "at USD 7.99 even a free product can't cover USD 8 of ads plus fees; from USD 8.99 it can, and buyers pay up to USD 19.99", with the unit-cost read still to come. (With no ceiling read, this case is step 3's note, naming the price that the floor needs.)
   2. **The break-even above the ceiling** (the cost-free floor when the unit cost isn't read) → **fails**: no price buyers are shown paying leaves profit after USD 8 of ads. Say both numbers, the break-even exact ("profitable only from USD 24.37; the highest price buyers pay here is USD 15.99", or "even a free product can't cover USD 8 of ads at the USD 7.99 buyers pay"), adding "it can only get worse" when the unit cost is before shipping; the related version is a bundle or premium version priced where USD 8 of ads leaves profit.
   3. **Unit cost not read** → unclear **as a note**, with the read that settles it as the next step (and, when no ceiling was read either, the read that shows a price buyers pay, both named) (when the AliExpress read gave no `price` and no `priceNote` range, in any of gate 2's no-answer or no-listing forms, and the user gave no unit cost: "profit: not read yet, AliExpress gave no price").
   4. **No ceiling read** (no price with sales behind it in this run) → unclear **as a note**: give the break-even price, and the next step is the read that would show whether buyers pay it.
   5. **The price being judged at or above the break-even** → **passes**.
   6. **The price being judged below the break-even** (which is at or under the ceiling) → passes, with **the price as the fix**: "loses USD 2.62 a sale at Amazon's USD 12.99; profitable from USD 15.99, and buyers already pay USD 19.99 for the top TikTok listing (870 units)". This caps "For you" at **a fit, with one fix**, the fix being that price (an unclear gate 1 outranks it).

   Step 2 is the one way a gate 3 without a read unit cost fails: the cost-free floor above the ceiling is a fact, not a missing read. Unclear (3 or 4) never caps and never pushes an idea below "a fit, with one fix"; only a pass (1 with a read unit cost, or 5) lets an idea reach "a strong fit". Print on demand: the unit cost is the print-on-demand base cost, used only when the user or a read gives it; otherwise the cost-free floor (step 2 can still fail it), else unclear as a note, and asking for the base cost is the next step.

**Unclear gates in this lens:** an unanswered or unread gate 2 or 3 is a note that never caps (as above) and counts as not failed: it never pushes an idea below "a fit, with one fix" (gate 3's steps 1b and 2 are the exceptions, under their conditions). The one thing an unread gate 3 holds back is "a strong fit", which needs it passed (step 1 with a read unit cost, or step 5: the count, below). Only an unclear gate 1 (a title close to a rule whose wording doesn't settle it) caps at "a fit, with one fix", the fix being the platform's review of that rule. Gate 3's pass **with the price as the fix** also caps there (it is a pass, not an unclear gate).

**Still reading AliExpress.** The single-unit check returns within its wait only when its product pages are quick (it can take several minutes) and is then read like any read that is in, with none of what follows; only when it is still running after that wait (AliExpress is slow on this one: its product pages can take a few minutes) does the first answer go without it (`running.md`, "Timing"). Until it lands, gates 2 and 3 are read exactly as unread: gate 2 is the still-reading note above, and gate 3 goes through its ordered steps without a read unit cost (the user's unit cost, when they gave one, counts as usual), its step 3 said as "profit: AliExpress is slow on this one (its product pages can take a few minutes)". So each is a note that never caps, and steps 1b and 2 still apply under their own conditions. While the check is still running, and only then, the verdict can't be "a strong fit", even when gate 3 passes on the user's own unit cost (gate 2 is still unread and could fail): a count that reads strong is "a fit, with one fix (provisional)", the fix being the AliExpress price, already being read (no cost to offer). Once the check has returned or missed, the count below applies unchanged. The For you verdict is marked "(provisional)" on those gates. When the run lands, the short follow-up reads gates 2 and 3 from it through the same ordered steps, with the actor's numbers (the one-unit cost, landed or plus shipping still to add, the break-even, the margin at USD 8 and 12 of ads), and gives the final For you verdict, saying plainly when the label changed ("now: a strong fit", "now: a fit, with one fix") or that it stands.

Notes (information, never a gate; they cap at "a fit, with one fix" only when three or more point the same way, each naming what it rests on):
- **Demo-able:** a visible change within 3 seconds with the sound off (a judgement from the titles).
- **Felt want:** an impulse a buyer feels on seeing it, beyond the TikTok units (a judgement).
- **Someone cares:** not a utility nobody stops scrolling for (a judgement).
- **Taste-led:** cosmetics, fashion, décor: buyers choose on taste, so a winner is harder to call from data.
- **Light and sturdy:** the Amazon leaders' `fees.shippingWeightLb` or dimensions, when read, labelled as **the leaders' weight** ("the leaders ship at 0.6 lb"); over about 2 lb, bulky or fragile (glass, ceramics in the titles) is a note on shipping cost and returns. Not read → "weight not read"; never invent a weight or freight.
- **Thin margin at the higher ad cost:** the margin after ad cost negative at USD 12 but not at USD 8.
- **Price room (guidance in the fit check, never a gate, a cap or a fix):** a price at least about 3× the landed cost leaves extra room for returns and shipping surprises; under it, say the room is thinner, with the numbers, beside gate 3's break-even (which alone decides whether it can work). It needs a landed cost (a DHgate page's `landedCost`, or the user's quote); with AliExpress's before-shipping price only, say the landed cost is still to read. The user's own price range, if given, caps gate 3's proven price ceiling; never a fixed number.

**Against the amount, for a dropshipper** (the fit check's "Against the amount they gave" line, when the user named an exact amount in this chat; `judgement-for-you.md`): there is no first order of stock, so the cash read against the amount is the sample order (the one-unit cost × 1 to 3 samples, landed when shipping was read, else plus shipping) plus `launch-plan`'s test spend for their band (its "Ads to their own store or TikTok Shop" column, or the Amazon column when they sell there; with an exact amount, capped at about a quarter of it, as `launch-plan` says). Read privately, with the same three outcomes as `judgement-lens-own-stock.md`, "Against the amount you gave me": at or under about half the amount (a rule of thumb) fits with room; over half, up to the whole amount, fits with little left (one line of information, never a cap); over the whole amount doesn't fit, with the way in (fewer samples, a smaller test spend). The amount is never said or worked back from.

Risk notes as above (lithium battery, health claims, children's products, ingestibles): they never move the verdict, except a risk the user asked to avoid (it caps at "a fit, with one fix" on its own).

**The count, through this lens** (first match wins):
- **not this one:** a gate fails (1, 2 or 3), naming the fact read in this run and the related version that would pass it. Nothing else makes it;
- **a long shot:** no gate fails, but demand on the channel they'd sell through is thin: **Felt want is against** (the top TikTok Shop listing under 100 units, for example 94 units ever across 11 shops), or, where TikTok Shop isn't read (outside the US), Search-led demand is against for thinness (an Amazon floor under 1,000 units a month). **Thin demand stays thin:** say plainly that the feed shows little demand, with its numbers (and the Amazon floor beside it), and never call low Amazon demand an opening. Hopeful and honest: it can still work, so the answer says **what would have to be true** (in one line, from the data: "buyers who haven't found it on TikTok yet would have to stop for it in a feed") and **the cheapest test** that would show it (the best ladder rung's check, for example `validate` on a version buyers already want, or a small ad test the user runs themselves, priced from their band). **When search demand is real but the feed's is thin** (Felt want against and an Amazon floor of 1,000+ units a month), the first related version is **the channel switch**, with both numbers: "buyers search for it (Amazon floor 6,550 a month) but don't impulse-buy it on TikTok (best listing 51 units): sell it where people search", meaning search ads (Google Shopping) to their own store, or a marketplace listing (Amazon, eBay, Walmart, or Etsy for a handmade or personalised version), each with what changes (search ads are paid per click rather than per view; Amazon needs stock or a seller-of-record setup with a supplier who ships under their name). This switch comes before the cheapest-test line, and the ladder's channel rung (`judgement-ladder.md`) is this one;
- **a strong fit:** Felt want favours, no counted row is against, no gate fails and gate 1 is not unclear, gate 3 **passed** (its step 1 with a read unit cost, or step 5; not unclear, not with the price as the fix), **Where you'd compete was read** (not `unknown`), and no three notes point against. "Strong" is a claim the data has to back, so an unclear gate 3 or an unread Where you'd compete never reaches it: the idea is **a fit, with one fix**, the fix being the missing read, named from the skill running it (the single-unit check, the read that shows a price buyers pay, or the ad library read), never lower for being unread. Gate 2 unanswered (AliExpress didn't answer) doesn't block it on its own;
- **a fit, with one fix:** everything else. The fix is the one that matters most: an unclear policy gate's rule (gate 1), the price from gate 3 when it passed with the price as the fix, the read that would confirm a strong fit (what gate 3 is missing, Where you'd compete) when that is all that stands between it and strong, the TikTok read when Felt want is `unknown`, an against row (Amazon sells it; Where you'd compete, whose fix is an unserved angle), or the three notes that cap it.

A provisional verdict while a slow read is still running (`running.md`, "Never hold the whole answer for a slow read") uses the same labels, marked "(provisional)" after the label, never a fifth label.

Felt want is this lens's deciding row for a strong fit, so with a cold-traffic profile in the US the TikTok read is part of the check: never left unread as "settled early" because the market verdict is settled.

**Felt want outside the US.** TikTok Shop is read only in the US (`running.md`, "Country"), so for a seller in another country Felt want is read from a stand-in instead of staying `unknown`, and the row names it as one. In this order:
1. **The feed's ads in their own country:** the Where you'd compete read (part of the check through this lens), with TikTok's ads library added for the EU, the UK and Switzerland. At the Several proven threshold (**3 or more proven advertisers**, running 35 days or more; `judgement-saturation.md`, rules of thumb, said as such) in one library's read, Felt want favours ("Felt want, from ads: 4 advertisers have kept Meta ads running 35+ days in the UK; ads that last are selling"); active advertisers with fewer proven is neutral; no active advertiser in any library read is `unknown` (no ads is not thin demand). The same read feeds Where you'd compete: say so once, so the two rows aren't read as two separate facts.
2. **The US TikTok Shop feed, as a labelled proxy**, when the ads read leaves Felt want `unknown` or the user asks for it: the TikTok read in the US, scored on the row's thresholds and labelled "US TikTok Shop, a proxy: your country's feed may differ". A proxy that favours or reads neutral counts like the real row; one that reads against is a line, neutral in the count, and never makes "a long shot" (outside the US that still comes from Search-led demand, as the count says).

With a stand-in that favours, "a strong fit" is reachable outside the US on the same terms as in the US. **The one fix is never a read that can't run for them:** outside the US, when Felt want is `unknown`, the fix (or the check named after "a fit, with one fix") is the stand-in read that settles it (the ads read with TikTok's library for an EU, UK or Swiss market, or the US TikTok proxy), never "the TikTok read" or TikTok Shop data for their country.

**The stand-in runs with the check, not after it.** Outside the US, the ads read in step 1 is how this lens reads Felt want at all, so it is in the first estimate and runs with the first yes, on the top ideas as soon as the chart and Trends name them, in the same turn and before the answer (the skill running the check says how). It is never left as an optional add-on to ask about later; only the user declining it leaves Felt want `unknown`, and then the fix is that read.

**Proven advertisers checked on TikTok, beside the stand-in (EU, UK, Switzerland).** TikTok's library can't be searched for long-running ads by keyword (a keyword search returns the newest ads first, with no date filter, and gives no account ids to size from), so the TikTok side of the stand-in is read by name: the proven advertisers the Meta read found, or brands the user names, checked on TikTok with `cleanpull/ad-library-monitor`, `advertisers: [<their names>]` (at most 10), `platforms: ["tiktok"]`, `activeOnly: true`, `oneRowPerAdvertiser: true`, `maxAdsPerTarget: 12` (no `tiktokAccountSize`: the named advertiser's own read fills the account size when TikTok gives it, free). Each advertiser row then carries `daysRunning` (at least: the oldest of the newest page), `maxReach` (the widest reach band among its kept ads) and, when present, `accountAdsInLibrary` (how many ads TikTok's library lists for the account). It costs up to about **USD 0.033 a name** (1 check, up to 4 library requests at USD 0.0035, up to 12 rows at USD 0.0015; about USD 0.006 when a name matches one account; about USD 0.047 a name at most on the Free plan from 2026-11-07): offered after the Meta read with that cost, its own part of the yes, never folded into the stand-in's figure. When someone asks for TikTok's proven advertisers on a product, say plainly the library can't be searched that way and offer this check by name; the labelled US TikTok Shop proxy (step 2 above) stays as it is. Reading it:
- **Proven** is an ad running 35 days or more; **small** is about **50 or fewer ads** in TikTok's library, an order of magnitude (TikTok's count varies between reads), each said as a rule of thumb; `accountAdsInLibrary` missing or null is "size not read", never small. Sorted by `maxReach`, widest first. There is no store step: TikTok rows have no landing page.
- **It counts the same:** advertisers proven on TikTok count toward the Several proven threshold as one library's read, exactly as in step 1 above; the sizes add one line of facts, never a different count: "2 of the Meta advertisers also keep TikTok ads running 35+ days with a few dozen ads or fewer: small sellers are making TikTok ads pay here." Big advertisers are information, never against.

**Outside the US, "a long shot" comes only from thin Search-led demand** (an Amazon floor under 1,000 units a month, with Felt want's stand-in against or `unknown`: a stand-in that favours or reads neutral rules it out). Large review counts on the leaders, a seasonal fall in Trends or a high price are never thin demand: they are the market's rows (review depth, the trend), read in the count as they are, or the one fix ("you need a better product than the leader": an angle), never "a long shot" on their own.

**Worked examples** (cold-traffic dropship, under $1k unless said):
- Heated eye mask at USD 29.99, Strong for the market: TikTok top 5 at 1,200 to 3,400 units (Felt want favours); a 14,000-unit Amazon floor (one caution line, neutral beside that felt want); no Amazon-sold leaders; the AliExpress read's one-unit offer at USD 6.20, shipping still to add (a `product` row, `price` 6.20, `shippingCost` 0, so not landed: gate 2 passes); break-even at USD 8 of ads well under the ceiling (TikTok listings at USD 29.99 with 1,200+ units) and under the USD 29.99 price (gate 3 passes, shipping still to add); "rechargeable" and "for dry eyes" in the titles (risk notes); Where you'd compete: 14 active Meta advertisers in 30 ads (crowded at 10+), 5 running 35+ days, 3 new in the last 14 days (no late wave), the top 3 TikTok shops 42% of units. → Where you'd compete is **against** for a first-timer under $1k (you'd be outbid while testing), so **For you: a fit, with one fix**: an angle no current advertiser targets, such as the Bluetooth sleep-sound version (+400% in rising searches), checked against their ads. Not "not this one": every gate passes.
