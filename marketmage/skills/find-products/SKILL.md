# Find products

Turn the user's niche into a short, scored map of product ideas, and find the ones worth checking together. Read these first:
- `references/judgement.md`: the core: the rules and the signal thresholds. **Follow it, and each part below.** Its other parts are read only at the step that needs them: `references/judgement-answer.md` (the tone, the answer structure, and asking for the profile) before writing the answer, or with the first batch when the profile is to be offered; `references/judgement-ladder.md` (the related versions ladder, the watches) once the verdict is known; `references/judgement-profile.md` (the profile, choosing the lens) when there is a profile (asking for one is in `references/judgement-answer.md`); with a profile, once the lens is chosen, `references/judgement-for-you.md` and the chosen lens's file only (`references/judgement-lens-cold-traffic.md` with `references/judgement-lens-cold-traffic-count.md`, `references/judgement-lens-marketplace.md`, `references/judgement-lens-own-stock.md`, `references/judgement-lens-maker.md` or `references/judgement-lens-general.md`; plus `references/judgement-lens-goals.md` when the goal is side income or brand); `references/judgement-abroad.md` (margins outside the US or across currencies, EU risk notes) before working such a margin; `references/judgement-saturation.md` (Where you'd compete) only through the cold-traffic lens, when that read is made or offered. Never read a lens file for a lens the user isn't on. **When the first message already gives the profile** (or the chat holds one), read the core and every part this job will use (the answer file, ladder, profile, for-you, the lens's file or files, the goals file when the goal is set, saturation through the cold-traffic lens, and `references/judgement-abroad.md` outside the US) in that one first batch, with `running.md` and `costs.md`.
- `references/running.md`: costs, caps, tools, secrets, and the runs already made in this conversation.
- `references/running.md` is the core; its parts are read only at their step (its first lines say when): `references/running-results.md` once results are in, before using them; `references/running-runs.md` before a data-provider read, when a run doesn't succeed, or when this chat already holds a run; `references/running-abroad.md` outside the US; `references/running-asks.md` when the user asks who runs this, offers an allowance, pastes a secret or asks for something MarketMage won't do.
- `references/notebook.md`: the opt-in notebook, when the user asks to be remembered or keeps one (then `references/notebook-use.md` before any notebook call).
- `references/costs.md`: the prices.

**Read once, together:** a reference already read in this conversation (by this skill or another MarketMage skill: their copies are the same text) is not read again. Read the references a step needs in one batch of parallel reads, never one after another.

**Reading a run's record** (`RUN_SUMMARY`, `DIGEST.md`): `get-key-value-store-record` with `keyValueStoreId` set to the run's `defaultKeyValueStoreId` (from `call-actor` or `get-actor-run`) and `recordKey` set to the key; never without `keyValueStoreId` (`references/running.md`, "Running").

## 1. Ask only for

- **The niche or category:** "kitchen", "dog accessories", "skincare".
- **The marketplace:** from the user's words (`references/running.md`, "Country"); US by default; GB, DE, FR and others for Amazon.
- **Optional:** the price range they want to sell in.
- **The profile, if there isn't one and it hasn't been offered in this chat:** its five quick bands, in the same message as the estimate, skippable, once per conversation (`references/judgement-answer.md`, "Asking for the profile"). The lens changes what is searched, so ask before the runs when it costs nothing to wait; if they skip, search for any seller and don't offer it again. **Except a quick one-off read** the user asked for directly (one TikTok list, one trends check: under about USD 0.10): don't put the five questions before it. Over USD 0.05, give its estimate and wait for the yes; under it, just run it, with no cost said; and offer the profile in one line after the answer (`references/running.md`, "Before any run", item 1: the one exception to "Asking for the profile").

## 1b. Search through the lens

With a profile, the lens shapes the search itself, not only the labels on one list (`references/judgement-for-you.md`, "The lenses, as data"):

**With a profile, read `references/find-products-lenses.md` once the lens is chosen** (in the batch with the lens's file): its "Step 1b, the lens table" says, lens by lens, the price range, the sources, the candidates kept and the suppliers, and the rest of that file holds the lens's offers (step 2) and its For you reading (step 4). ## 2. Plan the runs (cheapest first)

- **Run cost:** the standard set is about **USD 0.15** in the US (about USD 0.09 elsewhere, without TikTok), and up to USD 0.10 more for each chart this account has read before (products that left the chart since are billed as changes): the one estimate counts them ("up to about USD 0.25 (capped)", `references/running.md`, "Before any run"), and the cap comes from that figure. Say it once, with how long it usually takes (about 1 to 2 minutes), and get a yes, then, with the one-line note before the calls (`references/running.md`, "Running"), run Best Sellers and TikTok Shop together, and Trends on the product types they surface. With every offer taken (the competition check USD 0.025, one scale for 8 ideas USD 0.08, the AliExpress single-unit check on the top 3 USD 0.08, the Where you'd compete read on the top 3 USD 0.15, the wave read on the strongest USD 0.12), about **USD 0.61** at most in the US (through the Marketplace lens on Etsy or eBay: the competition check on their marketplace, about USD 0.28, and no Where you'd compete read, so about **USD 0.59** at most; about USD 0.82 on Apify's free plan from 2026-11-07); the sub-category charts for a narrow niche add up to USD 0.32 more.
- **Cold-traffic dropship** (a profile, or a request that says they'll dropship with TikTok or Meta ads): the Where you'd compete read (outside the US, part of the check, in the first estimate), the wave read and proven small advertisers, with the figures for their estimates: `references/find-products-lenses.md`, "Step 2, cold-traffic offers", read before the estimate.
- **Competition check:** offer it with the shortlist. Demand is half of a pick; the other half is whether a newcomer can get onto page 1. Ads are off here: "can I get on the page" doesn't need them.
- **More than 5 ideas:** Google compares 5 terms at a time, so offer the calibrated comparison to rank all of them on one 0 to 100 scale.
- **Outside the US:** skip TikTok Shop and say why: the actor covers the US shop. For a cold-traffic dropship seller, the ads read on the top 3 takes its place as part of the check ("Where you'd compete" above), not as an add-on.
- **Narrow niches:** Best Sellers takes a department, not only an exact chart. For a niche inside a department ("dog grooming" is in `pet-supplies`), read the department's chart and keep the rows whose titles fit the niche, and say you filtered. If few rows fit, offer the department's sub-category charts: `subcategoryDepth: 1`, `maxCategories: 10`, `maxItemsPerChart: 20` (at most 10 × 20 × USD 0.0015 + 10 summaries × USD 0.002 ≈ USD 0.32, and up to 10 × 20 × USD 0.002 = USD 0.40 more if these charts were read on this account before), with a yes. Skip Best Sellers only when no department fits, and say so. A slug is the part of the chart URL after `/zgbs/`, for example `kitchen`, `pet-supplies` or `beauty`.
- **"Under USD 100 to start"** (the user says they have under about USD 100, asks for "cheap to start", or the profile is under USD 250 and they ask what they can start with): a filter on the ideas, said as one: each idea's **start cost** is the smallest real way in, read from supplier rows (the AliExpress single-unit check's MOQ-1 price × 3 samples, before shipping; or a lower-MOQ offer's MOQ × its price), and the filter keeps the ideas whose start cost is USD 100 or less, adding a "Start cost" column. It prefers, as notes from the titles and the leaders' rows (never a fact invented): a single variant (no sizes, few colours), light (the leaders' weight under about 1 lb when their `fees` give it), and no risk note that needs testing or certification first (children's products, batteries, ingestibles, CE categories on an EU marketplace). Until the supplier rows are read, the column says "start cost: not read yet", with the single-unit check on the top 3 offered as the read that fills it (its estimate at the yes; usually under a minute). Pass a `maxPrice` to TikTok only when the user gave a price range. Ideas over USD 100 to start are named under the table as "over USD 100 to start", with their start cost, never hidden. Never repeat an exact amount of theirs (a band or "under USD 100", as they put it).
- **Price range:** pass the user's range to TikTok Shop as `minPrice` and `maxPrice`. Best Sellers has no price filter: drop the chart rows outside the range when you read them, and say so.

## 3. Read the results

- **Best Sellers:** `rank`, `title`, `price`, `rating`, `reviewCount`, `daysOnChart` (check each title belongs to the niche; a department chart holds much else), and the `chart-summary` row (median price, brands, `newEntries`). A product new to the top 50 (`daysOnChart` low) is a sign of a chart that moves; offer the chart watch (step 5) to see new entries as they happen.
- **TikTok Shop:** `title`, `price`, `unitsSold` (lifetime), `shopName`, `brand`, and each keyword source's `relevantToKeyword` and `keywordWordsMissing`. **Check every row is the niche's product** (`references/running-results.md`, "Read the data like an analyst"): a shop that ranks for the keyword often brings its other products (a "lip gloss" search can return the same brand's deodorant and serum). `relevantToKeyword` is the actor's title check (every word of the keyword in the title), a clue, not a verdict: confirm it against the title, and keep a `false` row that is clearly the product under another name ("lip oil" for a gloss search, if the user would count it) and drop a `true` row that isn't. Use the keyword summary's `relevantToKeyword` totals (`products`, `unitsSoldTotal`, `unitsPerDayTotal`…, with its `relevanceBasis`) rather than the totals over every product, adjusted for any row you judged differently, and say how many were left out. If fewer than 5 relevant rows remain, say the TikTok signal is thin and offer a narrower keyword or more products.
  - **Units per day need two reads a few days apart:** this run gives lifetime `unitsSold`. Say so, and offer the TikTok watch (step 5), which reports units per day from its second run.
- **Trends:** the 12-month `summary.direction` and `changePercent` per product type, seasonal peaks, and **`relatedQueries_rising`**: rising searches are where angles and sub-niches show first, so they get their own column.
- **Competition check** (the `keyword-summary` rows): `medianReviews`, `under100Reviews`, `top3SellerShare`, `sellerHhi`, the `priceP25`…`priceP75` band, and `salesFloor` (the top 10 organic results' "bought in past month" floor: `unitsPerMonthMin`, `revenuePerMonthMin`). Quote the floor as "at least 8,000 units, about USD 160,000 a month across the top 10", never as an estimate of sales (`references/judgement.md`, "The sales floor is a floor"). `marketplaceFees` gives Amazon US's referral rate (quote it from that row, never from this text) when the user asks what a sale pays out; the FBA fee comes with `validate`. **On Etsy or eBay** (the Marketplace lens's own read): no `salesFloor` (neither shows monthly sales: Demand level is `unknown` for them, with the Amazon and TikTok figures as labelled context); Concentration for them is the top 3 sellers' share of the listing rows that are the product (checked against the title; 10 or more such rows, else `unknown`, "sample too thin to score"; top-3 share thresholds only), never the summary's `top3SellerShare` or `sellerHhi`; the price band, and `marketplaceFees` for that marketplace; its `medianReviews` are the shop's or the seller's feedback, not the product's, so it isn't scored as review depth.

## 4. Score, then present

Score each idea on up to four signals, with the thresholds in `references/judgement.md`:
- **Chart:** Best Sellers review depth (`reviewCount` median of its rows under 500 favours, over 3,000 against) and recent entries;
- **Demand level:** TikTok `unitsSold` of the idea's top listings (1,000+ favours, under 100 against), or, when competition was checked, the Amazon sales floor of its top 10 (10,000+ units a month favours, under 1,000 against); `unknown` when neither was read (outside the US, only the sales floor can read it);
- **Demand trend:** its Trends direction over 12 months (+15% or more favours, −15% or less against);
- **Competition,** if checked: review depth and concentration from its keyword summary. For an Etsy or eBay seller, on their marketplace: review depth is not counted (the summary's `medianReviews` are the shop's or the seller's), and concentration comes from the listing rows that are the product (10 or more), never from the summary's `medianReviews`, `top3SellerShare` or `sellerHhi`; `unknown` if not checked.

Give each idea its `for` and `against` counts. Then:
- **A question in the user's message is answered first,** in plain words, before the opening line and the table (`references/judgement-answer.md`, "Answer the user's question first"); the table follows. The reply is sized to what was asked ("Size the reply to the question"), and a newcomer gets plain words for each term the first time ("Plain words for a newcomer").
- **Open on the best opening found** (`references/judgement-answer.md`, "Open on the best opening"): the first sentence names the strongest lead (for them, through the lens, when there's a profile; through the cold-traffic lens, the table's top row) and its number ("The strongest lead is a 40-piece meal-prep set: 34,876 units sold on TikTok Shop at USD 30.99."), even when no idea came out clearly ahead; then say in a line why it leads. Never open with a count, or with what didn't come out ahead. If the user named a hunch about the niche, give credit where a number backs it.
- **The visual moment, between the opening and the table** (`references/running-results.md`, "Explaining results": "One visual, where it helps"; only where you can draw one, and otherwise nothing about it is said): **the ideas side by side**, one horizontal bar per idea in the table, in the table's order, all on one measure: the TikTok `unitsSold` (lifetime) of the idea's top relevant listing when TikTok was read for the ideas, else the Amazon `salesFloor.unitsPerMonthMin` when competition was checked; an idea without that number is named under the chart as "not read", never drawn as zero. Each bar is labelled with the idea and its number, and coloured by its for/against count (favours green, mixed grey, against red), with the word beside the number as well. Caption: the measure in words ("TikTok Shop US, lifetime units of the top listing" or "Amazon US, at least this many a month across the top 10") and the date read.
- **A table of 5 to 8 ideas** (under 10 rows): idea, typical price, for/against, the number that most favours it, the number that most goes against it, a rising search for it, and whether competition was checked. **With a profile, the table also carries a "For you" column, right after for/against:** one of the four fixed labels, written exactly (**a strong fit**, **a fit, with one fix**, **a long shot**, **not this one**: `references/judgement.md`, rule 7), after the idea's rank for them ("#1 for you": `references/judgement-for-you.md`, "Several ideas side by side: rank the fits"), plus the deciding fact or the fix in a few words, specific to that idea ("#2 for you · a fit, with one fix: bulky at 21 × 13 × 21 in, price the shipping in", "#1 for you · a fit, with one fix: nothing against it yet; to check: single-unit price and the ad crowd" (its 6,400 TikTok units go in the number-for-it column, not the fix slot), "#6 for you · a long shot: 60 TikTok units", "#7 for you · not this one: margin −USD 2 at USD 8 ads"). An unread check is never written as the fix ("a fit, with one fix: MOQ-1 unread" is not a fix), and neither is a strength ("most TikTok sales" says why it ranks high, not what to fix): when nothing read holds an idea back, the cell says "nothing against it yet" and names the check; when the same check holds back every idea, it is said once above the table in plain words, and the cells carry what tells the ideas apart, with no "Fix:" pointing back to that check ("Fix: the two checks above" is the same non-fix again); a cell names a fix only when that idea has one of its own (bulky, a low price, a crowd) (`references/judgement-for-you.md`, "What follows "a fit, with one fix" is a fix, named"); thin demand on their channel (for cold traffic, the top TikTok listing under 100 units) is "a long shot", never "a fit, with one fix"; through the cold-traffic lens, with the Where you'd compete reading when it was read ("a fit, with one fix: 14 Meta advertisers, crowded at 10+", "a strong fit: 3 advertisers, 2 proven") and, when it wasn't read, the check named as a check, never as the fix ("nothing against it yet; to check: the ad crowd on Meta, about USD 0.05", or said once above the table when it applies to every idea). Every row gets one; never leave the column out, and never fold it into prose instead.
- **Ranks and counts from this table:** any rank or count in the prose ("#6", "5 of the top 20", "the only two in the top 7") is counted again from the table's rows before it is written (`references/running-results.md`, "Read the data like an analyst": "Ranks and counts come from the table you show").
- **After the table, the count,** in one line: how many ideas the data favours, how many are mixed, and how many it is against ("2 of 8 the data favours, 5 mixed, 1 against"), counted again from the table's rows, one by one, before it is written (`references/running-results.md`, "Ranks and counts come from the table you show"); the three numbers add up to the rows shown.
- **Order** by (for − against), then by the demand number. The ideas the data favours lead. Through the cold-traffic lens with a profile, order by the For you label instead (below).
- **Through the cold-traffic lens:** the For you reading uses the lens's count, its reasons, opening, order and each label against its explanation: `references/find-products-lenses.md`, "Step 4, through the cold-traffic lens".
- **What to avoid, and why, briefly:** ideas the data is against stay in the table, so it is a map rather than a pitch; below it, one line names them with the number that sets them back ("set aside for now: fidget spinners, interest down 85% over 12 months"). Where an against idea has a rising search pointing to a better version, name it in the same line: that version can join the shortlist.
- **Say what each idea did, never what it is:** "entered the top 50 on 3 October; 4,100 units on TikTok at USD 18", not "hot" or "winning". Warm words are fine when a number backs them (`references/judgement-answer.md`, "Tone").
- **For you, through the lens:** only with a profile: `references/find-products-lenses.md`, "Step 4, the For you block".
- **Without a profile,** one friendly line before the next step offers it only if it wasn't offered earlier in this chat (`references/judgement-answer.md`, "Asking for the profile"); never a second time.

**The short default** (`references/judgement-answer.md`, "The short default, and the full detail on request"): the opening, the visual, the table, the count line and, with a profile, the For you block, with the lines that always reach the user (an exclusion line such as "3 of 10 results weren't lip gloss and were left out", suspicious text, a caveat that changes a reading; no other notes block: thin samples, dates and ranking notes wait for the full detail), the profile's one offer, then one line offering the rest ("Want the full detail: the ideas set aside and why, the unchecked signals, what this can't see?") and the Next step last. **The full detail,** when the user says yes or asks anything that needs it (then only that part): the "What to avoid" line, the unchecked signals, what this can't see and the follow-ups (a watch, the wave read), each as set out here, from the rows already read, ending on the Next step again. A shape the user asks for wins: "everything" up front gets it all at once, with no offer line.

End with (the first two in the full detail; the Next step in both):
- the unchecked signals ("competition not checked on 5 of 8");
- what this can't see, in one line, only for the sources read (`references/judgement.md`, rule 9);
- **Next step, last:** which two ideas `validate` should take, and why those two by the counts, with no price (each reuses these rows: `references/running-runs.md`, "Runs in this conversation"). If the user had a favourite that the data is against, the next step can take its best related version instead, said in one line.

## 5. Ongoing: the weekly opportunity digest, sales per day and chart moves (watchlists)

Offer these with the answer, with no price on the offer, when the user wants to keep an eye on the niche (the weekly opportunity digest, TikTok Shop sales per day, chart moves): what each watches, its input, its cost per run and its cap are in `references/find-products-watches.md`, read when the user wants one or asks what it would cost, before quoting it.

## 6. Reading an opportunity, TikTok or chart digest later

- **Opportunities this week** (`DIGEST.md` opens with it; in the dataset, changes carrying `opportunity`: `searches`, `charts`, `tiktok`): lead with the new rising searches and breakouts (the term, its rise), then products new or back on the chart (title, rank, price), then TikTok products selling faster (units a day, from and to). For the strongest one or two, say what it points to, and end on the next move, with no price: a `market-scan` summary of the new search or `validate`.

- **TikTok:** lead with the fastest sellers by units per day and the ones speeding up, then products new to the keyword, then price moves. Before the second run there is no units per day yet: say the next run gives it.
- **Charts:** lead with new entries and re-entries (title, rank, price), then the biggest climbers, then brands entering if product pages were read. Say what it suggests for the niche: new products getting in quickly is a chart a newcomer can enter.
