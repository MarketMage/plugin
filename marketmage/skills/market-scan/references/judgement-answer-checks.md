# Judgement: the checks before an answer is written

Part of the judgement rules (`judgement.md` is the core). Read in the same batch as `judgement-answer.md` and `judgement-first-answer.md`, before writing any answer that gives a verdict or a label. From the real-user test on claude.ai of 8 Nov 2026 (round 3b); where an older line elsewhere says otherwise, this file wins.

## The answer-time reads are required

(R3-18: "shud i sell those led face mask thingys on amazon? … just tell me yes or no" read only `judgement.md` and `known-limits.md`, skipped the answer files, and so gave a full table to someone who asked for a yes or no, and left out the regulated-product line an LED therapy mask needs.)
- **Before the first word of an answer that gives a verdict or a label,** the answer-time parts the skill names must have been read in this conversation: for `validate`, `find-products` and `market-scan`, `judgement-answer.md`, `judgement-first-answer.md`, `judgement-ladder.md` and this file (with `judgement-timing.md` on a timing question); for `suppliers` and `gaps`, `judgement-answer.md`, this file and `running-results.md`. Other skills that read `judgement.md` (`briefing`, `launch-plan`, `start`, `complaint-brief`) read only the parts their own steps name.
- **Any not yet read are read then,** in one batch of parallel reads, before writing. The answer's shape, its required lines and its labels are in them: an answer written without them is wrong, however right its numbers. A part already read in this chat is not read again.

## Where these parts sit in the answer

With `judgement-answer.md`, "The short default, and the full detail on request": the short default carries the table (rule 1), the scores (rule 2), the credit (rule 5), the next move (rule 6), the verdict lines (rule 7), the best related version or the one fix (rule 10) and, for a yes/no question, the one-line opposite answer (rule 3). "What it means" (rule 1), the early sign and its watch (rule 3), the full ladder (rule 10) and what the data can't see (rule 9) are in the full detail, except that for a yes/no question the one-line opposite answer (for "is it worth selling?", the early sign with today's number) stays on the first screen (`judgement-answer.md`, "Answer structure"); its watch offer waits. The rest is offered in one line, unless a caveat changes the verdict or its confidence: that one line stays in the default. **No notes block under the table in the default:** caveats that don't change the verdict (list prices, not quotes; an assumed fee or rate; a comparison term that came back different; rows kept as close cousins) and a one-sentence policy step ("Before listing: …") are full detail too; only an exclusion or mismatch line, suspicious text and a verdict-changing caveat sit under the table by default.

Also under the table by default: a cross-check that fires and a regulated product's one line (`judgement-first-answer.md`).

## The margin and the launch ad cost

(Tester review of 0.4.21: heated eye masks at USD 28.99 on Amazon US scored "helps" at 49% after fees and read **Strong**, high confidence; after USD 1–2 of freight and USD 8–12 of ads a sale they leave USD 0–5. Owner's design, 13 Nov 2026: two signals, the margin before ads and the launch ad cost in dollars.)

**Score the margin at the offer the leaders sell.** The scored margin is for the offer buyers actually compare: the format most of the comparable top 10 sell, counting the listings with sales (a "bought in past month" badge, TikTok units). When most of them are N-packs or bundles, the margin is scored at that pack: the leaders' median pack price (or the user's, for the same pack) against N × the unit cost, after the fees at that price ("a 5-pack at USD 8.99: − 4.56 fees − 5 × 1.50 = −3.07, against"). A single-unit margin is scored only when single units are what sells (most badge-carrying leaders are singles; one single-unit listing with sales in `singleNewPrice` is not enough on its own) or when the user plans a single unit **and** single units are a real part of what sells: at least 3 of the top 10 with sales are single units priced near theirs (one single among multi-pack leaders is an exception buyers rarely meet, not the offer they compare); otherwise the single-unit figure is one labelled line beside the scored one ("a single spinner at USD 9.99 would leave about 37% (9.99 − 4.76 fees − 1.50), but 9 of the top 10 sell packs"). If the answer itself calls a figure unrealistic (a pack nobody sells at that price, a single unit against multi-pack leaders), that figure is never the scored one: score the realistic one and say why in the row.

**The Margin row** (`judgement.md`) is what a sale leaves before ads: what it pays out after fees, less two things:
- **The unit cost,** as before (the user's quote or landed cost, else the factory offers that are the idea).
- **Freight and duty,** on **any factory price not stated as landed** (Made-in-China, Alibaba, and the user's own FOB or EXW quote): **15 to 30% of the unit cost**, an assumption (a planning range for sea freight and duty on a small item from China), scored at 30% and said once with "a freight quote replaces it". None on a cost the user says is landed or DDP, an AliExpress `landedPrice`, a DHgate estimate, or a maker's own cost; an AliExpress price before shipping stays "plus shipping, still to add". An own-store seller's shipping estimate per order (`judgement-rates.md`) is separate, and stays.

What is left is **the break-even ad cost per sale** (the profit per unit after fees, unit cost and freight, before ads): measured fees and costs, no ad assumption. Beside it, **the healthy ad cost**: the break-even less 25% of the price, the most ads can cost while the sale keeps a healthy margin. Spoken rounded down to the dollar ("about USD 5", "about USD 12"); the cents stay in the cells.

**The Launch ads row** judges the break-even against **USD 8 to 12 of ads a sale**, the launch range, an assumption, the same on every channel (on Amazon, the sponsored-ads cost of a sale while a new listing has no organic rank; in another currency the same numbers in it, €8 to 12, £8 to 12): **favours** when the healthy ad cost is USD 12 or more (the sale keeps 25% of the price at the top of the range); **against** when the break-even is under USD 8 (it loses money at the bottom of the range); **neutral** between, where the ad cost decides it. It is in the table for every seller, with the standard assumptions (the facts layer); what the user's own figures change is in `judgement-for-you.md`, "Launch ads, for you".

**Framed fairly to Amazon.** Not every sale comes from an ad: as reviews and organic rank build, ads buy a smaller share of sales and the ad cost averaged over every sale falls. So the range is said as **the launch ad cost per sale**, for the months when most sales are bought with ads, never as what every sale costs for good; the before-ads margin is what the margin rises toward. The launch is what the first order has to survive, so the launch is judged.

**In the cells,** one line each. Margin: "USD 28.99 − 8.71 fees = 20.28; − 6.10 unit cost − 1.83 freight (30%, an assumption) = 12.35 before ads (43%)". Launch ads: "break-even USD 12.35 a sale; healthy up to USD 5.10; at USD 8 to 12 of launch ads (an assumption) a sale keeps 4.35 to 0.35 (15% to 1%)". With the USD 8 to 12 figures named as launch costs, never as a second margin score.

**The verdict, when Launch ads is neutral** (the cap in `judgement.md`, "The verdict rule"): the label is at most **Promising, with one fix**, the fix is the ad cost, and the break-even with the healthy ad cost is **the deciding number** on the first screen: "**For the market: Promising, with one fix**: it works if ads cost under about USD 5 a sale, and loses money over about USD 12." The verdict confidence drops one step (`judgement.md`, "Confidence"). **Against:** Not this version, the related version a price, pack or bundle where USD 8 of ads leaves profit, said with its arithmetic. **Favours:** no cap, and the break-even stays in its cell. Either way, the two figures live in the cells and, when they decide it, in the verdict sentence; never a note of their own under the table ("Where these parts sit in the answer"). **Both rows are worked whenever a unit cost was read,** also on a verdict settled early (on the referral fee alone before the leaders are read, said so): the break-even is part of the answer, never "not read".

**Worked across prices** (Amazon US, factory costs): the eye mask above, break-even 12.35 and healthy 5.10 → neutral → Promising, with one fix, verdict confidence medium. A USD 15 item with a USD 3 cost and about USD 6.50 of fees: 15 − 6.50 − 3 − 0.90 = 4.60, under 8 → against → Not this version. A USD 60 item with a USD 8 cost and about USD 15 of fees: 60 − 15 − 8 − 2.40 = 34.60; healthy 34.60 − 15 = 19.60, over 12 → favours.

**The arithmetic, when asked,** one step a line: the payout, the unit cost, the freight, the break-even, then "ad cost per sale = price per click ÷ share of clicks that buy: USD 0.80 a click with 1 in 10 buying is USD 8 a sale (both assumptions)". Every click price and conversion rate is labelled an assumption or the user's, never presented as measured.

**Dropship and cold traffic** keep their own sums (`judgement-lens-cold-traffic.md`), which already carry USD 8 and 12 of ads; no freight allowance on a landed cost.

## A yes or no asked for

"Just tell me yes or no", "yes or no?", "no essay", "short answer please": the shape the user asked for wins (`judgement-answer.md`, "Size the reply to the question"), in about 80 words and never past 120 (live runs of 0.4.7's first draft gave 190: three sentences of reasons, a "what must stay true", the profile offer):

**A verdict asked for is not this shape.** "Tell me it's a yes", "I need this to be a yes", "say yes": that is a verdict asked for, and it gets "When the user wants a verdict, or pushes back" below: the acknowledgement, the verdict with its counts, the table, the thresholds between it and Strong, and the best related version. The yes/no shape is only for a user who asks for brevity, never for one who asks for a particular answer.
1. **The plain answer, one sentence,** its first word set by the label (with a profile, the For you label): **Strong** or **a strong fit** → "Yes:"; **Promising, with one fix** or **a fit, with one fix** → "Yes, if <the fix>:"; **Not this version**, **a long shot** or **not this one** → "No, but <the best related version>:" (the search never dead-ends: `judgement.md`, rule 10). Then the one or two numbers that decide it.
2. **The verdict, one sentence:** its label, word for word (with a profile, the For you label first, then the market's), with its verdict confidence in a few words; when Launch ads is neutral, the deciding number once, in the plain answer ("Yes, if ads cost under about USD 5 a sale; it loses money over about USD 12:"), and the Next step's thresholds in a few words, all within the 120 words (and "some data missing" when the data is partial); the one fix or the best related version only when the label carries one, in a few words. No counts list (the counts wait for the table), no "what must stay true".
3. **Only the lines that always reach the user, one short sentence each, two at most:** a regulated product's line (`judgement-first-answer.md`, "A regulated product gets one line before ordering") first, then the one that matters most of an exclusion or mismatch, a caveat that changes the verdict and a cross-check that fires (the rest wait for the table). The regulated line is never dropped for brevity: it is part of the yes.
4. **One short offer line,** "Want the table and the details?", then the Next step line, last, in a few words: the move alone, with no "After that" items (its thresholds are already in the plain answer), so the reply stays within 120 words. The profile's offer waits for the next answer here (it is still the one offer for the chat).

No table, no For you block beyond its verdict line, no "What you got right" (unless it is the reason itself), no headings, no bullets. The same data gets the same label as in the full answer; only the shape is shorter. For example (79 words):

> **Yes:** the LED masks in Amazon's top 10 sell at least 6,500 a month, and searches are up 51% over 5 years.
> **For the market: Strong** (verdict confidence: low, and the data is partial: only 4 of the top 10 were LED masks).
> Before ordering, check the supplier model's FDA status, Amazon's approval for the category, and the skin claims a listing can't make.
> Want the table and the details?
> Next step: more factory prices for LED masks only.

## One peak, said the same way everywhere

(R3-19: the table said searches "peaked April 2026", and the text under it said "Searches peaked last December, and that's under 90 days away".) One demand statement per idea (`judgement-first-answer.md`) covers its peak too. An answer names **at most one peak per idea**, read from the Trends `interestOverTime` points, and every place that mentions it (the table's trend cell, the 90-day line, the timing line, the prose, a later turn) names the same month:
- **Read the peak from the series, year by year** (tester review of 0.4.21: a heated eye mask answer said searches peak in April, while the 5-year data peaks every December; a one-off spring 2026 surge made April 2026 the single highest week). The series is the row's `interestOverTime_timelineData` (weekly points, each with `date` and `value`, the term's figure first in `value`); read it from the row, never from the status message; when the row is too long to come back whole (cut short, or saved to a file), read that term's series with `apify-api-read`: `https://api.apify.com/v2/datasets/<id>/items?offset=<the term's position, from 0>&limit=1&fields=interestOverTime_timelineData&unwind=interestOverTime_timelineData&omit=time,formattedTime,formattedAxisTime,formattedValue,hasData,isPartial` (one `{date, value}` per week; `offset` and `limit` pick the term before its points are unwound). Unread, no peak month is named.
- **A 5-year series** (`validate`, or wherever one is read): for each **complete calendar year** in it, note the month holding that year's highest week; the month that tops the most years (at least 2) is the yearly peak. Only complete years count: a series read in November 2026 starts in November 2021, so 2021 and 2026 are part-years, and the years counted are 2022 to 2025. `summary.peakDate`, and the status message's "peak N on <date>", are only the single highest week; the latest 12 months alone are one year, never the pattern. A part-year can give a one-off high, never the yearly peak.
- **A 12-month series** (`find-products`): one year, so never "every year" or "the yearly peak": its highest month is said as **last year's highest month** ("highest last December"). A season the user names is checked in it as last year's level in that window against the year's average (`judgement-timing.md`, "A season the user names").
- **A yearly peak** (5 years: the same month or season is the highest of its complete year in at least 2 of the years read) is "the peak", said as "peaks every <month>". It is the one the seasonality cross-check and the timing line use ("the yearly peak, December, is under 90 days away").
- **No yearly peak:** the single highest point (`summary.peakDate`) is said as "highest in <month year>", and no 90-day line is written: a one-off high doesn't come round again.
- **Both, in different months** (a one-off high in April 2026, a yearly peak every December): the yearly peak is "the peak"; the one-off high, only when it matters, is a separate plain fact ("a one-off high in April 2026"), never called "the peak" or "peaked".
- **Check before sending:** the table and the text each name the same peak month, or one of them names none. Two different months each called the peak is a mistake to fix, not two facts. And the month named is the one that topped most complete years in the series ("peaks every December: the highest month in each of 2022 to 2025"); a month taken from `peakDate` or the last 12 months alone is the R3-19 mistake again.

## Labels in the user's language

(R3-17: a Spanish conversation got "For you: not this one · For the market: Not this version: here's one that could be (en claro: …)".) The labels are fixed strings (`judgement.md`, rule 7) in every language. **In an answer written in another language, the labels are the fixed translations below, word for word, every time,** never the English label, a mix, or the English with a gloss in brackets; the label-exactness rule (never a paraphrase, the explanation after the label, never inside it) applies to the translated set in the same way. Their meaning is said once in the user's language, in plain words, the first time they appear (`plain-words.md`, "Plain words for a newcomer, in a judgement").

- **The answer's language** is the user's: the language of their messages. A user who writes in English gets the English labels, whatever their marketplace.
- **The internal readings** (favours, against, neutral) are said in the user's language too, in the plain table's words (helps, hurts, neutral).

## The margin row, for a seller who won't sell on Amazon

(R3-21: a dropshipper's table showed "Margin if sold on Amazon", and a line under it said that row didn't apply to them; graders and users read it as their margin.) For a user who has said they'll sell somewhere other than Amazon (TikTok Shop, their own store, Etsy, eBay: in the question, the chat or the profile), **the Amazon-fee margin row is left out of the printed table,** and their own margin is said where their figures live:
- **Their per-sale line:** with a profile, in the For you block, where it already is (the fit check's money per sale: the price, their channel's fee from this run's data or its stated assumption, the unit cost, and for an own store or cold traffic the ad cost per sale as an assumption), worked as `judgement.md`, "The margin follows the user's channel" says. Without a profile (the channel said in the question), it is one plain line right under the table, labelled with their channel ("On TikTok Shop, a sale at USD 24.99 leaves about USD 9 after its 6% fee, the USD 6.10 cost and USD 8 of ads (an assumption)").
- **The market's Margin signal is unchanged:** it is still worked on Amazon's fees and scored, and the market verdict's counts include it, as for everyone. One clause, once, where the market's counts are given, says so, without the Amazon figure and as Amazon's, not theirs: "(these counts include the margin Amazon sellers would make; yours is below)". Amazon's margin is never one of the reasons the verdict sentence names for them ("searches are up and Amazon sellers keep a healthy margin" reads as their margin praised): the reasons named are the other rows.
- **No line saying an Amazon figure doesn't apply:** none is on screen. The Amazon figure comes only if they ask for it, labelled as Amazon's.
- **On Amazon, or not said:** the Margin row is printed as before. Outside the US, or with no Amazon fees read, the same rule holds for that marketplace's row.

**Why this choice, against the facts layer** (`judgement.md`: the same data gets the same table and the same market verdict, whoever asks). The facts layer's promise is that the facts are the same for everyone and no profile moves a number: the signals, thresholds, counts and market verdict are unchanged, and every row that is printed is the same row anyone would see. Leaving out one row that doesn't apply to this reader changes no number; the row stays in the scoring, and the clause above says it counts. The alternative, printing a row and then telling the reader to ignore it, presents a figure as theirs that isn't. Their own margin is not put into the facts table (that would make the table depend on who asks); it stays in the For you block, the fit layer, or its one labelled line.

## The phrase that reads live, taught once

(R3-20: on claude.ai an information question, "what does X go for on alibaba", goes to Claude's web search whatever the skill descriptions say, while "can you check …" loads MarketMage.) Once per conversation, one plain line teaches it: **"Ask me to check or look up anything and I'll read it live."**
- It goes in the welcome when there is one (the "What can you do?" menu, the guided session's opening: `first-use.md`). Otherwise it goes only where it reads naturally: as the last clause of the one offer line in a `suppliers` or `gaps` answer ("Want the offers side by side? And anytime, ask me to check or look up something and I'll read it live."), never as a line of its own and never after the Next step.
- **Not in a full check's answer** (`validate`, `find-products`, `market-scan`, and any yes or no): their end is the one offer line and the Next step, nothing more (`judgement-first-answer.md`, "The end of a first answer").
- Said once in the chat: never repeated, never in a later answer, never in a reply to a bare greeting (its one offer line stays as `first-use.md` sets it).

## When the user wants a verdict, or pushes back

**If the user asks for a particular verdict** ("tell me it's a yes", "I need this to work"), before or after the run, treat it as pushback in the answer itself: the acknowledgement line comes first, then the verdict with its counts, then the specific threshold or thresholds standing between this and Strong, and the question of whether they'd set one differently.

**If the user pushes back,** stay warm and stay exact. Acknowledge what they care about in one plain line ("I can see this one matters to you"), show the table again, and ask which threshold they'd change. Then apply their threshold openly ("at your threshold of 5,000 reviews, review depth reads neutral, and the verdict is Promising, with one fix") rather than changing the verdict. Never change a verdict because the user is upset, and never go cold or lecture because they pushed: go straight back to building (the ladder, the cheapest test).

## Wording that doesn't contradict itself

A credit and a fix about the same number are said as one fact: "priced like the leader, above most of the page" (never "What you got right: your USD 19.99 is where the top listing sells" beside "the one fix: USD 19.99 sits above the market's band").
