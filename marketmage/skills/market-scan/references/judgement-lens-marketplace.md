# Judgement: the Marketplace lens

Part of the judgement rules (`judgement.md` is the core). Read with `judgement-for-you.md` when the lens is Marketplace. Gate 2 ("Single units ship") reads as gate 2 of the cold-traffic lens: the single-unit check's `singleUnit.moq1OfferFound: true` (or an AliExpress `lowestMoqOffer` with `moq: 1`) passes; it fails **only** when AliExpress answered with offers and none sells single units (`singleUnit.offersRead` above 0 and `moq1OfferFound: false`). Every other reading is a note that never caps the verdict and counts as not failed: no matching listing (`offersRead: 0`) → "AliExpress showed no matching listing under this name; try another name", with one rerun under a synonym offered; no answer, in any form (`aliexpress: "no answer"` or `aliexpress: "not checked"`, a free row with `kind: "unavailable"` or `kind: "stopped"`, or no summary row for the idea at all) → "single units: not confirmed yet (AliExpress didn't answer in time; most consumer goods sell single units there)", with one rerun offered (about USD 0.04); not read yet → "single units: not read yet", with the single-unit check as the next step. The margin row reads the same way: with no `unitPriceMin` and no unit cost from the user it is `unknown`, never a cap. As in the cold-traffic lens, an unanswered or unread gate 2 never caps this lens's verdict.

**Still reading AliExpress.** The single-unit check usually returns within its wait (under a minute) and is then read like any read that is in, with none of what follows; only when it is still running after that wait (AliExpress is slow on this one: 5 to 20 minutes) does the first answer go without it (`running.md`, "Timing"). Until it lands, gate 2 and the margin row read exactly as not read yet: gate 2 is the note "single units: AliExpress is slow on this one (it can take 5 to 20 minutes)" and the margin is `unknown` ("AliExpress is slow on this one"), unless the user gave a unit cost; neither caps. While the check is still running, and only then, the verdict can't be "a strong fit": a count that reads Strong is "a fit, with one fix (provisional)", the fix being the AliExpress price, already being read ("Strong" is a claim the data has to back, so a fit the follow-up raises beats a strong it lowers). The For you verdict is marked "(provisional)" on them. Once the check has returned or missed, these rules apply unchanged, an `unknown` margin included. When the run lands, the short follow-up gives gate 2 and the margin after the marketplace's fees with the actor's numbers (the MOQ-1 price before shipping), counts the margin row, and gives the final For you verdict, saying plainly when the label changed ("now: a strong fit", "now: a fit, with one fix") or that it stands. A run that ends with "AliExpress didn't answer within 20 minutes" is the no-answer note above.

**Marketplace** (dropship or print on demand on Amazon, Etsy or eBay; the buyers search):

### Which marketplace is read

The lens reads the marketplace **they sell on**: the one named in the question, else `channels` (`amazon`, `etsy`, `ebay`). Amazon sellers get the Amazon rows already in the table. Etsy and eBay sellers get their own marketplace's read: `cleanpull/marketplace-search-scanner` with `keywords: ["<product>"]`, `sources: ["etsy"]` or `["ebay"]`, `maxResults: 24` and `listingRows: true`. Cost for one keyword on one marketplace: USD 0.015 provider search (charged once per marketplace per run) + USD 0.003 summary + 24 × USD 0.003 provider records + 24 × USD 0.0005 listing rows ≈ **USD 0.10**; on Apify's free plan from 2026-11-07, records and the search cost double, so ≈ **USD 0.19**. Cap: 1.5× the higher figure, about **USD 0.29** (`running.md`, item 6). It takes up to about 12 minutes, so it starts first, as its own run (`running.md`, "Timing"). When they sell on both, read both in that one run: two searches, two summaries, 48 records and 48 rows ≈ **USD 0.20** (USD 0.38 on the free plan from 2026-11-07; cap about USD 0.57). Count the one this product would sell on (the first named, if unclear); the other is information. Name the marketplace in the For you heading ("through the Marketplace lens, on Etsy").

What each read gives, and what it doesn't. Never present one marketplace's figure as another's:

| They sell on | Demand | Competition and price | Fees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Amazon** | the sales floor (`salesFloor`, top 10 organic) | sellers are brands read from titles; product reviews; sponsored results marked | the leaders' `fees` (referral + FBA), else Amazon's `marketplaceFees` (referral only) |
| **Etsy** | **no sales count**: Etsy shows none, so Demand level is `unknown` for them. Amazon's floor is one context line, labelled ("on Amazon, at least 3,200 a month"), not counted. Etsy's own clue, as information: the listings' `itemReviews` (each item's review count) and `listedAt` | `sellers` are shops; concentration from the listing rows (below); `priceP25`…`priceP75` (not `singleNewPrice`: Etsy states no condition, so that figure leaves every listing out); the summary's reviews are the **shop's** (`reviewScope: "shop"`), not the product's; ads not marked (`sponsored: null`) | Etsy's `marketplaceFees` (transaction, payment processing and listing fee; US sellers' rates) |
| **eBay** | **no monthly count**: eBay shows none, so Demand level is `unknown` for them. Amazon's floor is one context line, labelled ("on Amazon, at least 3,200 a month"), not counted. eBay's own clue, as information only: the listings' `quantitySold` (sold over that listing's life, not a month), summed over the listings that are the product | `sellers` are eBay sellers; concentration from the listing rows (below); the price band from `singleNewPrice` when used or multi-pack listings were left out, else `priceP25`…`priceP75`; the summary's reviews are the **seller's feedback** (`reviewScope: "seller"`), not the product's; ads not marked | eBay's `marketplaceFees` (final value and per-order fee; US sellers' rates). eBay is read on ebay.com (the US site): say so for a seller outside the US |

The Etsy or eBay figures go in the table as their own rows, labelled with the marketplace ("Concentration on Etsy"), facts only. They are not counted in the market verdict, which stays on the standard reads, so it is the same for everyone.

**Concentration on Etsy or eBay is counted from the listing rows.** Keep the listing rows that are the product (checked against the title), and take the top 3 sellers' share of those rows (shops on Etsy, sellers on eBay). It is scored on the top-3 share thresholds only (under 0.35 favours, over 0.55 against); there is no HHI here. It needs 10 or more such rows; under 10, the row is `unknown`, said as "sample too thin to score" with the count, and a rerun at `maxResults: 48`, still with `listingRows: true`, is offered with its cost (one keyword on one marketplace: USD 0.015 search + USD 0.003 summary + 48 × USD 0.003 records + 48 × USD 0.0005 rows ≈ USD 0.19; USD 0.35 on Apify's free plan from 2026-11-07; cap about USD 0.52). The summary's `top3SellerShare` and `sellerHhi` cover every organic result, not only the product, so they are never scored here. A read without listing rows (market-scan's default) leaves the row `unknown`: "not read: needs the listing rows".

### The rows, for you

On Amazon, every row is as in the table. On Etsy or eBay, the rows marked "(Etsy, eBay)" are **replaced** (read from their marketplace instead of Amazon) or **not counted**, and the rest stay **same**.

| Row | For you | Reads from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every market row | **same**, as in the table (search demand favours here: their buyers search) | the table |
| Demand level (Etsy, eBay) | **replaced** by their marketplace's demand, which is `unknown`: neither publishes sales. Amazon's floor is one context line, labelled ("on Amazon, at least 3,200 a month"), not counted and never a reason for "a long shot". Their marketplace's own clue (`itemReviews`, `quantitySold`) is information beside it, never scored and never turned into a monthly figure | their marketplace (none); Amazon `salesFloor` as context |
| Concentration (Etsy, eBay) | **replaced** by the top 3 sellers' share of their marketplace's listing rows that are the product, on the top-3 share thresholds only (no HHI), with 10 or more such rows; under 10, `unknown` ("sample too thin to score"); no listing rows read, `unknown` ("not read: needs the listing rows") | their marketplace's listing rows |
| Quality gap (Etsy, eBay) | **not counted**: it reads Amazon's ratings, and no Amazon competition figure counts for them | the table |
| Price band (Etsy, eBay) | **replaced** by their marketplace's band, as above | their marketplace's summary |
| Review depth (Etsy, eBay) | **not counted**: their marketplace's reviews are the shop's or the seller's, not the product's, so the thresholds don't apply. The median is said as information ("the shops on page 1 have a median of 1,450 shop reviews: established shops"). Amazon's stays in the table | the table |
| Paid pressure (Etsy, eBay) | **not counted**: Amazon's ads aren't on their marketplace, and the Etsy and eBay read doesn't mark ads; say so once | the table |
| Margin | **same**, after the marketplace's fees (`marketplaceFees`, or the leaders' `fees` on Amazon), with the MOQ-1 offer's price before shipping (`singleUnit.unitPriceMin`) as the unit cost (print on demand: the user's base cost if given, else `unknown`) | supplier rows, fees |
| MOQ-1 suppliers exist | **gate**, as gate 2 of the cold-traffic lens (print on demand passes: it is MOQ 1 by design) | supplier rows |

**On Etsy or eBay, this lens uses its own count**, as the cold-traffic lens does (`judgement-for-you.md`, "The "For you" verdict"). Count these rows (their marketplace's in place of Amazon's; `not counted` and `unknown` rows left out) with the market rule, and map it: Strong → "a strong fit"; Promising → "a fit, with one fix"; Not this version → "not this one", naming its rows. The market's own Not this version makes "For you: not this one" only when the rows that decided it are still counted for them. A failed gate makes "not this one" as in every lens. Demand level is `unknown` for them, so thin Amazon demand never makes "a long shot" here: Amazon's floor stays a context line. **One cap:** when Amazon's floor is under 1,000 units a month, an Etsy or eBay seller can't reach "a strong fit": the verdict is at most "a fit, with one fix", and the fix is a small demand test on their marketplace, named with what it costs them (for example one listing for 2 to 4 weeks: its listing fee, plus any ad spend they choose). Say why in one line: Amazon shows little demand, and Etsy and eBay don't publish theirs. When the count already reads Promising for another reason, the label's one fix is the one that matters most (an `against` row, or else the `unknown` read that would settle the most, as in `judgement.md`), and the demand test still appears on the first screen as one line: "before you spend: <test>, about USD <cost>".

**Not read yet:** until their marketplace's read is back (or if the user declines it), its rows are `unknown` for them, marked provisional, and the answer says so plainly ("Etsy isn't read yet: concentration and price there are unknown until it is"), with the read offered and its cost. Amazon's competition figures never stand in for Etsy's or eBay's, not even as a proxy.

### Worked examples

- **Amazon** (heated eye mask, dropship): every row as in the table: a sales floor of at least 14,000 units a month (favours), top-3 share 0.22 (favours), 4 ads in 48 (favours); the margin at USD 27.99 after Amazon's referral fee, with the AliExpress single-unit price of USD 6.20 before shipping as the unit cost. "For you, through the Marketplace lens, on Amazon [under $1k · dropship · first time]: a strong fit."
- **Amazon, while the single-unit check is still running** (the same heated eye mask): the same counts read Strong, but gate 2 is "single units: AliExpress is slow on this one (it can take 5 to 20 minutes)" and the margin is `unknown` ("AliExpress is slow on this one"), so the first answer gives "For you: a fit, with one fix (provisional)", the fix the AliExpress price, already being read. The follow-up, once the MOQ-1 offer at USD 6.20 lands: gate 2 passes, the margin at USD 27.99 after Amazon's referral fee is counted, "now: a strong fit". Had AliExpress missed (20 minutes), the rules above apply unchanged: gate 2 the no-answer note, the margin `unknown`, and the count decides.
- **Etsy** (personalised dog collar, print on demand; illustrative figures): Etsy's read gives 24 listings that are the product, across 20 shops; the top 3 shops hold 7 of them, a top-3 share of 0.29 (favours, under 0.35: "Concentration on Etsy: 0.29, no shop holds the page"), a price band of USD 16.00 to 28.00, a median of 1,450 shop reviews (information, not counted), and no ads marked (not counted). Demand level: `unknown` (Etsy shows no sales counts), with "on Amazon, at least 3,200 a month" as one context line, not counted, and the Etsy listings' median of 38 item reviews as information. The margin at USD 22.00 after Etsy's fees (about 9.5% + USD 0.45), with their base cost.
- **eBay** (cork yoga block, dropship): eBay's read gives 24 listings, 5 of them the product (checked against the title), from 5 sellers. Concentration on eBay: `unknown`, "sample too thin to score" (5 product rows, under 10), with a rerun at `maxResults: 48` offered and its cost. New single units sell at USD 19.11 to 35.25; a median of 1,077 seller feedback (information, not counted); 40 sold across the 2 listings that show a count (over each listing's life, not a month: information only). Demand level: `unknown`, with Amazon's floor as one labelled context line. The margin at USD 23.99 after eBay's fees (about 13.6% + USD 0.40).

Say once that Amazon and Etsy have their own dropshipping rules ("Before listing: read Amazon's dropshipping policy; it decides which suppliers fit."), as the one outside-knowledge step line (Tone), not as a gate.
