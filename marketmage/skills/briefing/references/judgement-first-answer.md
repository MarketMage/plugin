# Judgement: the first full answer, weighting by channel, and labels that match

Part of the judgement rules (`judgement.md` is the core). Read in the same batch as `judgement-answer.md`, before scoring and before writing the first full answer of a check (`validate`, `find-products`, `market-scan`, a claim or guru check). From the real-user test of 7 Nov 2026: where an older line elsewhere says otherwise (a chart title carrying the verdict, verdict lines with nothing before them, every market row counting for a seller who isn't on Amazon, shipping left out of an own-store margin), this file wins.

## The first full answer of a check

The first answer that shows a check's results has five parts, in this order, and nothing comes before part 1:

1. **The plain answer to the user's own question,** in one to three sentences, the answer itself first ("No:", "Yes, if …", "Partly:", "Not too late:", "Order, once …"). It answers what they asked, in their words, from this check's numbers: "is that real?" gets whether the claim is true; "should I order?" gets order or not yet, and what decides it; "too late?" gets the timing (`judgement-timing.md`); "where do I get them and what would I make?" gets the supplier and the money per sale. When the message asked nothing beyond the check itself ("is X worth selling?", "check X", "is it saturated?"), parts 1 and 2 are one and the same: the verdict sentence, said once, leads.
2. **One plain verdict sentence:** the label, word for word (`judgement.md`, rule 7), and why, in its two biggest numbers, with the one fix or the best related version. With a profile the For you label leads, and the market's label is in the same sentence or the one after it ("**For you: a fit, with one fix** (first order about USD 960, which fits the $1k–5k range you picked); **For the market: Promising, with one fix**: demand is steady, +4% over 5 years, and the fix is …"). The verdict is said once: never a second verdict line repeating it, never again in the table, a later chart or "What you got right".
3. **No visual in this answer** (real-user test round 2: drawn in the same reply as a new check, the visual came first and the answer was left out, in 4 of 6 answers). The skill's visual moment is drawn in a follow-up turn, once this answer is on screen: the full detail, a chart the user asks for, a deeper turn (`running-results.md`, "One visual, where it helps"); where one can be drawn, the one offer line may name it ("Want the full detail with the margin chart, or …?"). **A chart's title describes the chart** ("Margin per pack at USD 27.99", "Lifetime units sold, top listings, TikTok Shop US"), and never carries the verdict; an answer whose verdict or answer is only in a chart is wrong, chart or no chart.
4. **A compact table** (under 10 rows), shaped to the user ("Notation follows experience", below).
5. **The next move:** one "Next step:" line, last, and only once in the answer.

Between parts 4 and 5 sit only what the short default keeps (`judgement-answer.md`, "The short default, and the full detail on request"): the exclusion or mismatch line, suspicious text, a verdict-changing caveat, a cross-check that fires, a regulated product's one line, the For you block with a profile, the one offer line. "The one fix", "What must stay true" or the best related version is inside part 2, not a line of its own after it.

**A cross-check that fires** (the market selling near cost, by the price umbrella; a seasonal peak under 90 days away for a seller who buys stock) counts as a caveat that changes a reading: one line under the table in the short default (`judgement.md`, "Where these parts sit in the answer").

**A regulated product gets one line before ordering** (real-user test round 3: a red-light LED face mask, often an FDA-regulated device, got its verdict with nothing on it). For a device-like or ingestible product (LED or red-light therapy, TENS or EMS, hearing amplifiers, blood-pressure monitors, supplements, cosmetics with drug-like claims), one plain line under the table in the short default, with or without a profile, says what to check before ordering: the FDA status or clearance of the supplier's model, Amazon's category approval, and the claims the listing can't make. It is neutral: it never changes a signal, the market verdict or the For you label by itself. With a profile it is the For you block's risk note, said once.

### A claim or guru check

When the question is whether a claim is true ("he says these do 50k a month", "my course says 60% margin"), part 1 names the claim and says **true, false or partly**, with the data's number beside it, before anything else: "No: the 50,000 isn't a month. The top cloud slide listing on TikTok Shop shows 57,335 sold in total since it went up, and it's Mellow Sleep's own listing, not a generic product."
- **Say what each number counts.** TikTok Shop's units sold are **lifetime** (everything a listing has sold since it was listed), never a month: a monthly claim against a lifetime counter is false as a monthly figure, or unproven when the lifetime total itself is under the claim. Amazon's "bought in past month" floor is per month, and a floor. Name which one each figure is.
- **Say whose listing it is.** When the top figure is a brand's own listing (its shop name matches the brand in its title, or the shop sells many products under that brand), say so, and put what the generic listings sell beside it ("the unbranded ones sell about 3,400 to 4,000 in total"): that is what someone hoping for easy money needs to hear.
- **The same in any opening that quotes one listing's units** (`find-products`' strongest lead, a TikTok figure in a verdict sentence): when that listing is a brand's own, say so in the same sentence, with what the unbranded listings sell beside it.
- Never a judgement of the person who made the claim, and nothing filled in that the data can't see (their ad spend, returns, commissions): named in one line as what this can't see.

**Two framings, same data, same verdict:**
- "Is this worth selling?" → "**For the market: Promising, with one fix** (medium confidence): 4 for, 2 against. You were right that demand is rising: +38% over 5 years. The fix is getting off a crowded page 1: the median listing there has 3,400 reviews, and 12 of 48 slots are ads priced under the organic p25. The 'XL' angle the rising searches point at (+250%) is where the same buyers are heading, priced above p75 with the size as the difference. Next step: a market scan of 'xl silicone lids'."
- "Is this saturated?" → "**For the market: Promising, with one fix** (medium confidence). 2 signals say crowded (review median 3,400; 12 ads in 48), 4 say open (demand +38% over 5 years, 3 leaders under 4.2 stars, top-3 share 0.31, margin after fees 48% of the median price). Crowded on page 1, not closed: the leaders' quality gap is the opening a newcomer can build on."

## Numbers the user brings, and checks the plugin can make

From the real-user test, round 2 (J7): a seller pasted numbers from another tool ("avg 30 reviews, top 10 do 300+ sales/mo, main kw ~15k searches: back off or keep going?") and got a verdict built on them from memory, with chores to do by hand; the live read later showed 1,263 to 5,161 reviews, not 30.
- **Their numbers are a starting point, never the verdict's basis.** A market verdict (or "keep going" or "back off") comes only from a read made in this chat. Before one, say in a line that the answer turns on their numbers, credit what they show if it holds, and offer the live read that confirms what it can, with its one estimate, as the move ("Your numbers point to an open page; the check that confirms what it can reads Amazon's top 10, demand over 5 years and factory prices: up to about USD 0.14 (with a spending limit). Go ahead?"). When their message already said go, run it (`running.md`, item 5). Once read, where theirs and the data differ, say so plainly with both figures, without blame ("the top listings show 1,263 to 5,161 reviews, not about 30").
- **A check the plugin can make is offered, never handed back as a chore.** "Read the 1-star reviews on the top 5", "check the 12-month trend", "count the listings with a bottle holder" are reads a skill makes (the complaint brief, the demand read, the leaders' titles): offer that read as the follow-up, in one line. Only what no actor here reads (`known-limits.md`) is left to the user, said as such.
- **The market, competition and demand read never waits for a supplier cost.** Run it now; the margin uses the factory prices read in the same check, or a landed cost the user states, said as theirs, or comes later in one line ("the margin follows once there's a unit cost"). Never "send me the supplier cost and I'll run the check".

## Notation follows experience

- **Plain by default:** with no profile, or a profile whose experience is none, or anyone who says they're new, a beginner or have never sold, and whenever it isn't known: plain sentences. The verdict sentence gives the counts in words ("3 things count for it and 1 against") and always the confidence, in words ("high confidence: every source came back", or "low confidence, a first look: two of the reads came back thin"); no "·"-strings of counts and no neutral or unknown counts. The table has three plain columns (what we looked at, what we found, whether it helps), and the third says helps, hurts or neutral with a plain reason of 3 to 6 words ("helps: few reviews to catch up on", "hurts: three sellers hold most sales"), never a threshold, a rule of thumb or a "we look for" in that cell (real-user test round 2: "we look for up 15% or more" and "helps at 10,000+ a month" read as analyst notation to a first-timer). The rules of thumb move to the full detail, one plain line per row ("we count reviews as a help under 500"), the one exception to printing each threshold beside its number, so they are still there to disagree with (rule 2 in `judgement.md`; pushback names them, and a user who asks why gets them at once). Plain words add no notes under the table: the short default's limits hold (`judgement.md`, "Where these parts sit in the answer": a list-price caveat or a kept-row explanation waits for the full detail). A term is explained the first time it appears (`judgement-answer.md`, "Plain words for a newcomer"), and only then; no definitions of terms the answer doesn't use.
- **Thresholds for a plain reader:** "print each threshold next to its number" (`judgement.md`, rule 2 and "Signals and thresholds") and a skill's own "print each threshold" line still score every signal the same way; for a plain reader only their printing moves, into the full detail, and the table's help cell says helps, hurts or neutral with its short reason.
- **Every table and verdict line in a plain answer** (real-user test round 3: `validate`'s comparison of two ideas showed a first-timer "against (−15% or less)", "favours (10,000+)" and "3 for, 1 against, 3 neutral"): the two rules above hold for every table and verdict line of a plain answer, not only the first answer's signal table: `validate`'s side-by-side of two ideas and each idea's verdict line, `find-products`' ideas table and its count line, `market-scan`'s signals, and any later turn that shows them again. Each cell says helps, hurts or neutral (or for, against, neither) with a short plain reason; counts are said in words, for and against only ("3 things count for it and 1 against"; never "and 3 are neutral" or an unknown count); no cell or line carries a threshold, a cut-off in brackets ("favours (10,000+)", "against (−15% or less)"), "≥" or "≤", or the compact "3 for · 1 against · 3 neutral". The thresholds wait for the full detail, as above.
- **Compact notation only for a known experienced seller:** a profile that says they've sold (a bit, or with a brand), or a user who says they sell already (products live, an Amazon or FBA account, their own store's sales), uses seller tools, or uses trade terms themselves (FBA, MOQ, BSR, ROI, PPC, landed cost). Then the compact form is fine ("**For the market: Promising, with one fix** · high confidence · 3 for · 0 against · 5 neutral"), with the thresholds beside the numbers.
- **Never twice:** one verdict (part 2), one Next step, one offer line.
- **The end of a first answer: one offer line and the Next step, nothing more** (real-user test rounds 2 and 3: the full detail, the profile, "keep this for next time" and the next step stacked up). When the profile's offer is due (`judgement-answer.md`, "Asking for the profile"), it joins the one offer line as its second half ("Want the full detail, or tell me how you'd sell it and roughly your budget, and I'll say what it means for you?"), never a line of its own beside it; the notebook's ask never comes in a new user's first answer (`notebook.md`, "Ask once"); and the wait line (how long such reads take) is never in it (`running-results.md`, "A wait").

## Labels follow the counts and the budget gate

- **Check every label against its own counts and gates before writing it** (the market's, each For you reading, each row of a table): the label is the one the verdict rule gives (`judgement.md`, "The verdict rule", with the line below), and a For you label is what its lens gives after the first-order gate. A label and its counts that read against each other are a mistake to fix, not a nuance to explain.
- **More against than for is not Promising.** With two or more `against`, and more `against` than `for` even if every `unknown` favoured (A > F + U), the market label is **Not this version: here's one that could be**, and it is never a bare no: the same first screen names the best related version with the fact from these rows that makes it promising (`judgement-ladder.md`, "Related versions"; rule 10 in `judgement.md`: the search never dead-ends), in the hopeful voice ("this version is up against it: …; the one the data favours is …, because …"). When against outnumber for only until the unread signals are counted (A > F, but A ≤ F + U), the label is **Promising, with one fix**, and the fix is the unread check that decides it, named as a check. In find-products, an idea is counted as one the data is against only on the first case.
- **More against than for never reads as good news** (real-user test round 2: "Promising, with one fix" beside 1 for and 2 against read as optimistic, because the margin was left unread). The market labels are fixed strings (`judgement.md`, rule 7), so when against outnumber for only until the unread signals are counted, the label stays **Promising, with one fix**, and the same verdict sentence says in plain words why the against signals don't rule it out: the counts first, then the unread check that could still turn it ("so far 2 things count against it and 1 for; it isn't ruled out, because the factory price isn't read yet, and that check decides it"). The label never stands without that line, and nothing hopeful comes ahead of it. A signal the rows already answer is never left unread to keep the label (`running-results.md`, "A summary polluted by off-topic rows is recomputed from the matching rows, never dropped with them"): it is scored, and the label is read off the counts again. With nothing unread, two or more against and more against than for is **Not this version** (above).
- **The budget gate decides "a fit".** When the first order the data shows (the user's own quote, else the supplier's MOQ × unit cost) is over their budget band's edge, or over the amount they gave, the For you label is never "a strong fit" or "a fit, with one fix", unless a real smaller offer read in this run fits (`judgement-lens-own-stock.md`, "A smaller real start that fits makes it a fit, with one fix"; never against the user's own quote): "1,000 pairs × USD 5.20 = USD 5,200, over your under-$1k range" is "not this one", with the related version that would pass. Without a profile there is no For you label: a budget the user named in the question is one plain line beside the verdict ("a first order at that minimum is about USD 5,200, more than the amount you gave me").
- **The For you note only tells ideas apart, in a few words** (real-user test: every row read "#N for you · a fit, with one fix", the same on each; round 2: the For you column made the table 7 columns wide). In a table of ideas, the For you reading is **a short phrase in the idea's own cell, after its name**, never a column of its own: **"<idea>: <its own fix, or what sets it apart>"**, 8 words or fewer after the label, with the label before it only when that row's label differs from the line above the table ("Natural chews: small-shop brand selling well", "Laser pointer: a long shot, 60 TikTok units"; the 8 words leave out the label):
  - **No "#N for you":** the rank is the row order. With a profile the rows are sorted by it: by label, then by the lens's own count (for minus against over the rows that lens counts, never the market's for/against column), then by the lens's demand number (TikTok units for cold traffic; the sales floor otherwise) (`judgement-for-you.md`, "Several ideas side by side: rank the fits"). The top row is the strongest lead for them, and the opening names it.
  - **Its own fix** is only that idea's own (bulky, a price under the break-even, a crowd, thin demand): never a strength, and never a check every idea shares. When several ideas share the same label for the same reason (a check not yet made holds every one back), the label and that check are said **once, above the table**, in full ("Six ideas read a fit, with one fix for you, and the fix is the same for each: the AliExpress single-unit price and the Facebook ad crowd, still to check"), and those notes carry only what sets the idea apart ("5,200 sold on TikTok; rechargeable battery"), never the shared label and check repeated row after row. A note keeps a label of its own where it differs from that line: a long shot, not this one, or "a fit, with one fix" with a fix of its own ("a fit, with one fix: USD 8 of ads leaves USD 3").
  - **What sets it apart** is a fact from a row the lens counts, with its number ("6,400 sold on TikTok", "no single-unit offer read yet"), different on each row; Amazon chart rank, chart review depth or a search trend are context, never this fact, for a lens that doesn't count them.
  - **Once one row has a note, every row has one:** an idea with no fix of its own gets its strongest counted fact for them ("most TikTok sales here, 6,400"), never a blank, so the notes tell every row apart.
  - When every row would carry the same label and nothing a counted row says tells them apart, there are no notes: one line above the table says the label once, why, and which idea leads for them, and the table's order is their order.

## Weighting by channel

How the user sells (said in the question or the chat, or the profile's fulfilment and channels) sets what leads, as it already sets the margin's fees (`judgement.md`, "The margin follows the user's channel"):
- **On their own store, TikTok Shop or Etsy (not Amazon):** Amazon's review depth, concentration and paid pressure are **context**, never `against`, in everything said for them. They are not counted in any For you count, whatever the lens; in find-products' per-idea for and against for them, the Amazon page-1 signals (the chart's review depth and the competition check) are left out of the counts, and **never in the table's "what holds it back" cell** (real-user test round 2: it still listed "FortiFlora has 50,186 reviews on Amazon" after the text called them background). That cell shows what does count against on their channel: falling interest, a low price or thin margin (with shipping), a regulation (registration, a health claim), a dominant brand on their own channel (a brand's own listing holding most of their channel's sales); or it is empty for that row ("nothing yet"); when every row would read "nothing yet", the column goes and one line above the table says nothing counts against any of them on their channel. In prose the Amazon numbers are one line at most ("Amazon's page 1 is held by listings with 9,000+ reviews: context for you, since you'd sell on your own store"). **What leads for them:** demand steadiness (the trend and its yearly peak), repeat purchase (a consumable or something bought again: a judgement from the titles, said as one), the margin with shipping (`judgement-rates.md`, "Shipping in the margin") and the ad cost (USD 8 and USD 12 of ads per sale, said as an assumption, for an own store). The market line keeps its label and counts (the facts layer, the same for everyone); when Amazon's page-1 rows are what hold it back, the verdict sentence says so in a clause ("for the market, Promising: its two againsts are Amazon's crowded page 1, which you won't sell on").
- **On Amazon** (FBA, or own stock sold on Amazon), **or not said:** every market row counts, as before.

## Shipping in the margin, for sellers who ship themselves

For a seller with their own stock who ships their own orders on any channel other than Amazon (their own store, TikTok Shop, Etsy; not FBA), the headline margin includes an **estimated** shipping cost per order. The USPS bands, the sum, and the cases with no estimate (over 10 lb, no weight, a dropshipper, FBA, outside the US) are in `judgement-rates.md`, "Shipping in the margin", read before working such a margin.

## Low-volume Trends terms

**Low-volume Trends terms.** A term whose `summary.lowVolume` is true had at least one in five of its complete points (weeks, or days or months by range; `summary.points`, `pointsWithoutData`) too thin for Google to report, so its shape rests on a small sample:
- **Falling** (`direction: "falling"`) is scored as usual: dropping below Google's threshold is a real fall, and the actor counts those points as 0 for exactly that reason.
- **A breakout** (`direction: "rising"` with `changePercent` null on a low-volume term: unreported for the first quarter, now holding at least half its peak) favours, labelled as a rise from below Google's threshold, with no percent.
- **Otherwise** (rising with a percent, or stable) the Demand trend is `unknown`, "too few searches to read a trend"; its peaks and dates aren't quoted as facts, and its `relatedQueries_rising` aren't used as angles (a breakout there can be noise, such as a phone model under a candle).
- **With synonyms** (`validate` compares the product and up to 2 synonyms): the Demand trend comes from the product's own term; when that term is low-volume and a synonym isn't, the synonym's reading is used, and the answer names it.

## One demand statement per idea

Each idea gets **one** demand-trend statement in an answer, with its window ("down 47% over 12 months", "up 63% over 5 years"): the window of the skill's own read (5 years in `validate`, 12 months in `find-products`), the same in the table, the prose and every later turn that quotes it. Another window is quoted only as a separate, labelled fact, never in its place. A **stand-in term** (a broader search used because the product's own term is too thin to read) is named as one wherever its figure appears: "searches for bully sticks (a stand-in: 'duck feet' has too few searches to read) are down 16% over 5 years".

## Rising searches, in every answer

The rules from going deeper (`judgement-deeper.md`, "Rising searches in a card") hold in quick answers too: the ideas table, the verdict's angle rung and any line that quotes one.
- **Only relevant ones:** a rising search is quoted for an idea only when it is about that idea's own product (a dog row never carries "freeze dried cat treats"), and a novelty, joke or political search ("trump poop bags") is left out. When none of a term's rising searches fits, the cell says "no relevant rising searches yet".
- **"From a small base":** a percentage on a low-volume term (`summary.lowVolume`), a breakout, or any rise of +500% or more is written with "from a small base" beside it ("+2,600%, from a small base"), never as a bare headline figure.

## Wording that doesn't contradict itself

A credit and a fix about the same number are said as one fact: "priced like the leader, above most of the page" (never "What you got right: your USD 19.99 is where the top listing sells" beside "the one fix: USD 19.99 sits above the market's band").
