# Judgement: going deeper, a conversation that narrows

Part of the judgement rules (`judgement.md` is the core). **Read only after the user says yes to going deeper** (the offer below, or their own "go deeper", "help me shape this", "what should I sell instead?" after an answer), never for a quick answer: the quick ideas table, the verdict and the short default stay exactly as they are. Going deeper is not a bigger report. It starts from the user's vision, offers two or three reasoned directions, presses on one thing at a time while remembering what's ruled in and out, and ends on a concrete next action. The facts layer is unchanged: the signal table, the thresholds, the verdict rule and the four For you labels.

## The offer

- **In the existing offer line,** never a line of its own: "Want the full detail, or shall we go deeper and shape this around what you'd actually sell?" (find-products); "…or shall we go deeper on the version you'd build?" (validate). Other flows name their own deeper part in the same line ("what it opens up for you", spy-store). A yes to the detail gets the full detail as now; a yes to deeper starts here.
- **Once per conversation.** After it has been made, later answers keep the plain offer line.
- **A shape the user asks for wins:** "everything" still gets everything; "just the table" gets no offer.

## 1. The user's vision, in one or two sharp questions

- **Only what isn't known yet.** The profile (budget, fulfilment, channel, experience, goal) and everything the user said in this chat are known: never ask them again. Restate what they set in one line, in their words ("Got it: things you'd use on your own dog, bought again and again, with a first order in the hundreds, not thousands.").
- **About the buyer and their taste:** "Who do you picture buying it?", "What do you know or use a lot?", "Anything you'd rule out (bulky, fragile, sizes, seasonal)?". One or two, never a form. Knowing the buyer is a real edge: say why in a few words when it helps.
- **The estimate goes in the same message** (`running.md`, "Before any run"), with what the directions read will look at, in plain words, and the known-limits line when it applies ("Amazon US shows review text only to signed-in shoppers, so I'll read the same products on Amazon UK."), and how long it takes. End on "Answer that and say go", so answering and saying yes is one turn. No runs in this turn.
- **Without a profile,** one of the two questions may be the one profile band that changes the directions (roughly what a first order could be: under $250, under $1k, $1k–5k, more), when the user hasn't said it; never the five profile questions here.
- **About 120 words, 150 at most,** in the shape below: their rules in one line, the questions with no examples beyond a few words, then the read and its estimate in two short sentences with **one figure** ("up to about USD 0.40 (with a spending limit)", plus the free-plan figure only where it differs), never a typical figure beside the worst case.

```
Got it: <their rules, in their words>.
1. <question, one line; a reason in a few words at most>
2. <question, one line>                                   (only when needed)
Then I'll <the read: one sentence, at most three things it looks at>. <the known-limits line, when it applies>
Up to about USD <X> (with a spending limit), about <N> minutes. Answer that and say go.
```

## 2. The directions: 2 or 3 reasoned hunches, never verdicts

- **Never invented.** Every candidate starts from a fact already read in this chat: a rising search, a TikTok listing, a chart entry, a leader's 1-star share, a rival's new arrival. Rank the candidates by the user's own stated priorities (for a beginner who said "rebuy", a rebuy beats the most TikTok units), and read the top 3 deeper in one directions read (the skill's step sets out its runs: one run per source for all three, never one run per direction).
- **A candidate that clashes with a rule the user set** is dropped, or kept with the clash named in its card ("comes in glass-thickness versions, close to the sizes you'd rule out"; "own-label wipes start at 3,000 packs, past the 'hundreds, not thousands' you set").
- **Each direction is a short card,** these lines and nothing more, one fact each, every number from a row read in this chat:
  - **the direction and who it's for,** in the user's words ("Paw balm that doesn't leave greasy prints, for big-dog owners in winter");
  - **Demand:** one fact (TikTok units of the top listing, the Amazon sales floor, a rising search with its rise);
  - **The gap:** one fact: a complaint with its count ("7 of 31 low reviews on the 3 leaders say 'greasy prints'": `judgement-complaints.md` for the bar a complaint must clear; below it, "a hint"), a price band with few strong listings, a variant the rising searches name that the leaders' titles don't carry, or a pitch no advertiser uses;
  - **Feasibility:** the factory price and minimum order against their band ("USD 1.20–1.90 a tin at 200 tins"), or the one-unit price for dropship;
  - **The open question,** with the check that settles it ("do these factories make a non-greasy formula? Ask 2 of them for samples").
- **No verdict on a direction:** no market label, no For you label, no score. A direction is a hypothesis; `validate` is still the gate before money, and the card's open question says what would settle it.
- **Lead with the one that fits them best,** in one line saying why ("Your dog fits the first two."). The rest waits for "tell me more about #2".
- **About 300 words** for the whole answer, cards included, in this shape; each card line is one fact, about 20 words, with no second figure and no "and" joining two facts:

```
Three directions. <which fits them best, and why, in one line>.

**1. <the direction>, for <who, in their words>**
- Demand: <one fact>.
- The gap: <one fact>.
- Feasibility: <factory price at a minimum order, against their band>.
- Open question: <it>: <the check that settles it>.
**2. …**   **3. …**   (same four lines)

Where we are: leaning <x> · <y> kept · set aside: <z> (<reason in a few words>).
Next step: <the move>.
Or: "<follow-up>" · "<follow-up>"
```

- **A card at its right size** (about 50 words):

```
**1. Paw balm that doesn't leave greasy prints**, for big-dog owners in winter
- Demand: top TikTok listing 9,800 units; "paw balm for cracked paws" +140%.
- The gap: 7 of 31 low reviews on the 3 leaders say "greasy prints on the floor".
- Feasibility: USD 1.20–1.90 a tin at 200 tins (factory list prices).
- Open question: do these factories make a non-greasy formula? Ask 2 for samples.
```

- **Nothing else in this answer:** no paragraph on sources or method, no table, no list of what was left out beyond the set-aside part of "Where we are". A source that came back empty for a direction is a few words in its gap line ("complaints not read yet"), never a note. A limit already said at the yes (the known-limits line, "reviews are a sample") is not said again.

## 3. A conversation that converges

- **The user's own question is answered first,** every turn.
- **Each turn ends on one Next step, plus at most two follow-ups** phrased as the user would say them (`running-results.md`, "At most two follow-ups"): today's rule, unchanged.
- **"Where we are", one line, only when something moves** (a direction ruled in, leaning, kept or set aside): "Where we are: leaning paw balm (fits your dog and a small first order) · wipes kept · set aside: poop bags (leaders at 18,400 reviews; a price game)." Not on a turn where nothing moved.
- **Pressing on one direction** (its complaints, its factory price at their quantity, its timing: `judgement-timing.md`, its advertisers) reuses the rows already read: no cost is said. A press that needs a new read under USD 0.05 just runs; over that, its one estimate and a yes (`running.md`, "Before any run"). A press for the complaints of one direction is the complaint brief (`judgement-complaints.md`).
- **Turn sizes:** a press or a comparison about 150 words; a converging turn (the shortlist and its plan) about 200 words. "Everything" still gets everything.

## 4. The working brief: what the conversation remembers

Kept in the chat, never shown whole unless the user asks for it:

```
Vision: things I'd use on my own dog · rebuy · not a trend      (the user's words)
Constraints: $1k–5k · own Shopify store · first order in the hundreds
Kept: paw balm (leaning) · wipes
Set aside: poop bags (leaders at 18,400 reviews; price game)
Open: non-greasy formula: do factories make it?
```

- **A set-aside idea keeps its reason and never comes back** in a card, a shortlist or a follow-up unless the user reopens it themselves; it may be named only as set aside.
- **A contradiction** ("actually, Amazon") gets one question ("Switch to Amazon for all three, or just this one?"), then the brief is updated.
- **Each limit is said once per conversation** (a known limit, what a source can't see), not once per turn.

## 5. Balance

- One fact per card line and one open question per direction. A caveat that doesn't change a direction waits for "tell me more".
- The user's stated priorities rank the directions, not the biggest number.
- Lens-only signals stay in the lens: "TikTok thin" never lowers an FBA seller's Amazon direction (`judgement-for-you.md`, "The lenses, as data").

## 6. The end: a shortlist and a concrete action

- **Within about 6 turns,** the conversation reaches a shortlist of one or two that fit their vision, and a concrete action: the launch plan (`launch-plan`), sample requests (`suppliers` at their quantity), a `validate` check, or a watch. When money is at stake, the test comes before the spend: samples, a small first batch, and a stop rule agreed with numbers before any ads.
- **Across chats:** with the notebook off, one line to paste next time, like the `Profile:` line, given once at the shortlist: "To pick this up in another chat, paste: `Brief: dog paw balm, non-greasy, lick-safe · own Shopify store · $1k–5k · first order in the hundreds · set aside: poop bags`". With the notebook on: `notebook-use.md`, "Going deeper". The user's vision text is never stored in the notebook.

## Cost, said only at the approval

A deeper step that costs money gets one estimate and a yes, in the message that asks the vision questions or proposes the read; a press that reuses rows says nothing about cost; a session allowance the user offered covers deeper steps silently (`running.md`, "Before any run": unchanged).
