# Supplier prices

What it costs to source a product, from public supplier pages. Read `references/running.md` first (costs, caps, tools, timing) and `references/judgement.md` (the core: facts apart from advice), with its parts only as each step needs them: `references/judgement-answer.md` (the tone, the answer structure, and asking for the profile) before the answer, or with the first batch when the profile is to be offered, `references/judgement-ladder.md` when a related version or a watch is offered, `references/judgement-profile.md` (the seller profile) when there is one (asking for one is in `references/judgement-answer.md`), and with a profile, once the lens is chosen, `references/judgement-for-you.md` and the chosen lens's file only (`references/judgement-lens-cold-traffic.md` with `references/judgement-lens-cold-traffic-count.md`, `references/judgement-lens-marketplace.md`, `references/judgement-lens-own-stock.md`, `references/judgement-lens-maker.md` or `references/judgement-lens-general.md`; plus `references/judgement-lens-goals.md` when the goal is side income or brand); `references/judgement-abroad.md` (margins outside the US or across currencies, EU risk notes) before working such a margin. Prices are in `references/costs.md`. If the user asks to be remembered or keeps a notebook, follow `references/notebook.md`.

`references/running.md` is the core; its parts are read only at their step (its first lines say when): `references/running-results.md` once results are in, before using them; `references/running-runs.md` also before a status message stands in for rows; `references/running-runs.md` before a data-provider read, when a run doesn't succeed, or when this chat already holds a run; `references/running-abroad.md` outside the US; `references/running-asks.md` when the user asks who runs this, offers an allowance, pastes a secret or asks for something MarketMage won't do.
- `references/known-limits.md`: routes established not to work, each with what to use instead: read in the same batch as `references/running.md`, before choosing any source (a store, a marketplace, a country, a keyword); never try a route it lists. `references/plain-words.md` (the words the user sees, for every answer) is read in that same batch.

**Read once, together:** a reference already read in this conversation (by this skill or another MarketMage skill: their copies are the same text) is not read again. Read the references a step needs in one batch of parallel reads, never one after another.

**Reading a run's record** (`RUN_SUMMARY`, `DIGEST.md`): `get-key-value-store-record` with `keyValueStoreId` set to the run's `defaultKeyValueStoreId` (from `call-actor` or `get-actor-run`) and `recordKey` set to the key; never without `keyValueStoreId` (`references/running.md`, "Running").

## 1. Ask only for

- **The product ideas,** a few words each ("silicone baking mat", "dog harness"), up to 50.
- **Where to look:** Made-in-China (factories, the default), and if they want, AliExpress (dropship and small quantities) and Alibaba (wholesale). Nothing else is needed: no accounts or keys.
- **Quotes the user already has** ("got 3 quotes: USD 4.20, 7.80 and 11, same specs, which one do I trust?"; real-user test round 3: no read was offered, and the next question went to a web search): answer what they asked from what they gave first (what makes quotes comparable: the same spec, quantity and terms; the checks before trusting one), then offer this skill's read of the factory listings for the same product as the follow-up that settles whether a quote is out of line, with its one estimate (`references/judgement-first-answer.md`, "A check the plugin can make is offered, never handed back as a chore"). "What does it go for on Alibaba or Made-in-China?" is this read: never a web search for it.
- **What each source gives,** when you describe them before a run (say only this, never from memory):

  | Source | Unit price | MOQ | Price breaks | Supplier | Shipping |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Made-in-China search | yes | yes | from the top offers' own pages (`offerPagesPerSource`) | name, years, badge (Diamond, Gold), audited or verified, rating, from those pages | no ("contact the supplier") |
  | Alibaba search | price, or a price range that spans its breaks | **yes** (`moq`, `unit`) | no: give the offer's page | name, years, rating, country | no |
  | Alibaba offer pages (`alibabaUrls`) | yes | yes | **yes** (`priceTiers`) | also Gold Supplier, on-time rate, lead time, specs | no (signed-in buyers only) |
  | AliExpress search | per piece | 1 (single pieces) | no | store name, where the card shows it | no |
  | DHgate offer pages | per piece (lots converted) | yes | yes | yes | **yes, to the US** (landed cost) |

  An Alibaba idea read through the data service's dataset instead of its search page (rare; the run's warnings say so) has no MOQ.
- **Optional:**
  - DHgate offer pages (`dhgate.com/product/…`) or Alibaba product pages (`alibaba.com/product-detail/…`) they found, to compare;
  - their order quantity (`quantity`), to price at the right break;
  - the ship-to country (`shipToCountry`, US by default).

AliExpress and Alibaba searches come through CleanPull's marketplace data service, a third-party web data provider that CleanPull holds the account for. DHgate is **not searched** (its robots.txt disallows search pages): only DHgate pages the user gives are read. 1688.com needs a sign-in and isn't read.

## 2. Run

- **Run** `cleanpull/supplier-price-finder` with `products`, `sources` (always pass it: `["made-in-china"]`, or with `"aliexpress"` and `"alibaba"` as asked), `maxOffersPerSource` (10 by default), `offerPagesPerSource` (3 by default: the top offers' own pages, for price breaks and supplier years), and `dhgateUrls`, `alibabaUrls`, `quantity`, `shipToCountry` as given.
- **Cost (paid plans):** USD 0.005 per product idea priced (free when no offer is found) + USD 0.002 per offer delivered.
  - One idea with 10 offers ≈ **USD 0.025**; each DHgate or Alibaba page is one more offer (USD 0.002).
  - A page read through residential addresses adds USD 0.007 (rare: in tests every source answered without it).
  - **AliExpress or Alibaba:** + USD 0.02 per source per run (one provider search covers all the run's ideas there) + USD 0.005 per offer (USD 0.002 offer + USD 0.003 provider record). One idea with 10 AliExpress offers ≈ USD 0.07 more; results filtered out by title cost nothing.
  - **Example:** 3 ideas on Made-in-China ≈ USD 0.08; the same with AliExpress too ≈ USD 0.25: over USD 0.05, so give the one estimate (`references/running.md`, "Before any run") and get a yes. One idea on Made-in-China alone (about USD 0.025) just runs, with no cost said.
  - **The estimate is the worst case:** `maxOffersPerSource` bounds the offers, so per idea at most USD 0.005 + offers × USD 0.002 on Made-in-China, and USD 0.02 + offers × USD 0.005 per AliExpress or Alibaba source, plus USD 0.007 for each page that needed residential addresses (rare). For the AliExpress example, "This costs about USD 0.30, occasionally a little more if I need to dig deeper to answer you."
- **The cap** (`maxTotalChargeUsd`) is the spending limit, never said (`references/costs.md`, "Estimate and spending limit"; its share of it when other runs share the yes). It must leave **USD 0.02 per idea per AliExpress or Alibaba source** above the high end: the actor reserves a provider search per idea and source before it starts one, and stops early under a tight cap (a cap near the estimate stopped a read after 1 of 7 ideas); when max(1.5 × the high end, the high end + USD 0.15) leaves less, the cap is raised to leave it. Example: 3 ideas on AliExpress, about USD 0.30; cap USD 0.45.
- **Timing:** Made-in-China takes seconds to a minute; **AliExpress and Alibaba searches usually take 1 to 4 minutes** (read from each site's search page), and up to about 12 minutes when a page doesn't answer and the run falls back to the data service's dataset. Say so, start with `waitSecs: 45`, and follow the run.
- **The single-unit check** (dropship: "can I buy one, and at what price?"; the check `find-products` offers on its top 3): `products`, `singleUnitCheck: true` and `sources: ["aliexpress"]` (always say it: without `sources` the actor's default adds a paid Made-in-China read) (AliExpress alone, its top 3 results per idea; `sources: ["made-in-china", "aliexpress"]` only when they want factory MOQ-1 offers counted too, and then the cap is at least the high end plus USD 0.07 per idea, since the actor reserves the factory pages' worst case before it starts and reads nothing under it). For a cold-traffic dropship seller who sells through their own store (the 2.9% + USD 0.30 card fee), add `marginCheck: {}`, or `marginCheck: {sellPrice: <price>}` when they gave a planned price in USD (and, when they gave them before the run, their own ad cost per sale as `adCosts: [<theirs>]` and card fee as `feePercent` and `fixedFee`): each summary then carries a `margin` object (the break-even price at USD 8 of ads, the cost-free floor and, with a price, the profit at USD 8 and 12 of ads), read as `references/judgement-lens-cold-traffic.md` says ("The single-unit check's `margin`, taken as-is"). Not for TikTok Shop sales: that fee comes from the TikTok read, so that sum is worked out as before (a seller using both gets `marginCheck` for the own-store sum only). **Usually under a minute** (it reads AliExpress's search page; 3 ideas in about 30 seconds), and 5 to 20 minutes only when AliExpress is slow (the search-page read failed and the data service visits each product page), 4 ideas at once: say so (in the estimate, when it needs a yes: "the AliExpress price usually takes under a minute"), and never set its run timeout under 22 minutes (leave `callOptions.timeout` out). On its own, start it with `waitSecs: 45`, and once more with `get-actor-run` at `waitSecs: 15` if it is still RUNNING; finished by then (the normal case), answer with it directly. Still running after that, say "AliExpress is slow on this one (it can take 5 to 20 minutes)" and follow it as `references/running-runs.md`, "Timing" sets out. A check already running for the same idea in this conversation is followed, not started again (the same section). Beside other reads in the same answer (a Made-in-China read as its own run, or another skill's reads), start it first with `waitSecs: 0`, and when the others are in, give it its wait (`get-actor-run` at `waitSecs: 45`, then 15: `references/running-runs.md`, "Timing"); finished by then, answer once with it read directly, no provisional mark and no follow-up. Only if it is still running after that wait, answer with the others, the parts that read from it marked "AliExpress is slow on this one (it can take 5 to 20 minutes)", and complete them in a short follow-up when it lands ("The single-unit check, in that pattern") (the first answer is not the end of the turn: keep following the run in the same turn and write the follow-up when it lands). Cost: USD 0.02 provider search per run + per idea USD 0.005 summary + 3 offers × USD 0.005 ≈ **USD 0.04 for one idea, USD 0.08 for 3** (3 ideas: about USD 0.08, occasionally a little more if I need to dig deeper to answer you); the cap is its spending limit, USD 0.23, never said, which leaves the USD 0.02 per idea the actor reserves. Read each summary's `singleUnit` (`moq1OfferFound`, `unitPriceMin` with `currency`, before shipping, `offersRead`, `sampleUrl`) and the offers' `sellsSingleUnit`. `offersRead: 0` means AliExpress showed no matching listing: say "AliExpress showed no matching listing under this name; try another name" and offer one rerun under a synonym (never a no). An idea AliExpress didn't answer, in any form (a free `unreadable` row with `kind: "unavailable"` ("AliExpress didn't answer within 20 minutes"), a free `stopped` row, `aliexpress: "no answer"` or `"not checked"` on the summary, or no summary row): nothing is charged for it, and it is never a no: say "single units: not confirmed yet (AliExpress didn't answer in time; most consumer goods sell single units there)" and offer one rerun of those ideas.

## 3. Read the results

- **`summary` rows** (one per idea, read in full): `unitPriceMin`, `unitPriceMedian`, `unitPriceMax`, `unitPriceP25`, `unitPriceP75`, `moqMin`, `moqMedian`, `landedUnitCostMin/Median/Max` and `offersWithShipping`, `cheapestOffer`, `lowestMoqOffer`, `cheapestLandedOffer`, `sources` (each source's status: one that found nothing is said as "Alibaba returned no offers this time", with no word on what it did or didn't charge: `references/running-results.md`, "The last line"), `notes`.
- **Offers:** `fields: "recordType,product,source,title,url,unitPriceMin,unitPriceMax,moq,unitPriceAtQuantity,priceTiers,shippingCost,shippingDaysMin,shippingDaysMax,landedUnitCostMin,supplierName,supplierYears,supplierLevel,supplierVerified,supplierRating,category,specs,variantOptions,unit,lotSize"`, `limit` about 40. `category`, `specs` (the supplier's own attribute table: material, size, capacity, model…) and `variantOptions` (the colours and sizes a buyer picks from) are passed through as the supplier wrote them, where the page or search record shows them.

## 4. Explain it like an expert

**First, the answer's rules (required):** if `references/judgement-answer.md`, `references/judgement-answer-checks.md` and `references/running-results.md` haven't been read in this chat, read them now, in one batch, before writing a word of the answer: an answer written without them is wrong.

- **The cost range first, from the summary row:** "Factory price USD 0.50 to 17 a unit, median USD 5.60, at a median MOQ of 300." The range quoted first comes from the offers that are the product and comparable (the checks in the next bullet, made first): when any were set aside, it is recomputed from the rest and said as recomputed ("USD 2.20–5.20 a pair, from 3 matching offers; 7 were playground slides"), and the summary's own range is quoted only when every offer is comparable (`references/running-results.md`, "A summary polluted by off-topic rows"). The summary covers every offer found (`offers`), not only the rows you read; then pick out a few offers as examples.
- **Compare like with like** (`references/running-results.md`, "Read the data like an analyst"): offers are compared at an equal MOQ (or price break, `priceTiers`) and the same spec: read `category`, `specs` and `variantOptions` with the title for size, material, thickness and type, and `unit` and `lotSize` for the pack (a lot of 10 is priced per piece). Specs are as the supplier filled them in, unchecked: when an offer has none, judge by the title and say the spec wasn't stated. An offer for a different spec, or priced per set or per lot without a per-piece price, is set aside or converted, and the answer says so ("2 offers were for a 3-piece set; priced per piece"). The summary's range covers every offer found: recompute it from the comparable ones when any were set aside.
- **MOQ against cash:** the cheapest unit price often comes with the highest MOQ. Show the trade-off: unit price × MOQ is the first order's cash.
- **Supplier reality per direction** (after a deeper conversation or a complaint brief: `references/judgement-deeper.md`, "In other flows"): for each kept direction, the first order's cash at its lowest minimum against their band, and which offers list the fix in their titles or specs (else "ask for it as a custom part"); the fix leads the questions to send.
- **Landed cost** is shown only where a page quotes shipping (DHgate pages, to the US): unit price plus that shipping spread over the units. It's an estimate: it excludes import duties, taxes, payment fees and customs brokerage, and it never guesses freight. Made-in-China says "contact the supplier" for freight, Alibaba shows shipping only to signed-in buyers, and AliExpress and Alibaba search results carry no shipping or price breaks: say these have no landed cost. **Alibaba search results do carry the MOQ** (`moq`, `unit`; except an idea read through the dataset fallback) and often a price range across the breaks: use both. For the breaks themselves, read the offers' own pages (`alibabaUrls`, USD 0.002 a page plus USD 0.005 for the idea's summary).
- **A brand name in a factory listing:** when an offer's title or `specs` names a well-known brand (Apple, AirPods, Stanley, Nike, Dyson…) on a product that brand didn't make, or says "replica", "1:1", "AAA" or "mirror quality", add one neutral line under the table: "<n> offers carry <brand>'s name: check the supplier is authorised to sell it before ordering; unauthorised branded goods are counterfeits, which Amazon, Meta and TikTok ban." Keep those offers out of the cheapest and the trust ranking's top picks, and point to the unbranded offers of the same spec. A listing that names the brand only as what it fits or works with ("for iPhone 15", "fits Stanley 40 oz", "compatible with <brand>", "replacement for <brand>": a case, a strap, a topper, a part) is an accessory, fine, and needs no line.
- **Prices** are list prices for a signed-out visitor, in US dollars only (for a seller who sells in another currency, never converted at a remembered rate: at their rate or the dated reference rate, `references/judgement-rates.md`, with their landed cost invited once, `references/running-abroad.md`, "Country"). Negotiated, sample and member prices aren't included, and "negotiable" offers have no price. DHgate lots are priced per piece.
- **Thin coverage: fewer than 6 comparable offers** (the same threshold as `references/running-results.md`, "Coverage"). Not the single-unit check: it reads AliExpress's top 3 offers per idea by design, so 3 offers there is a complete read, never thin coverage, and no broader search is offered for it. The actor keeps only offers whose title names every word of the product idea (`RUN_SUMMARY.filteredOut`, and each source's `filteredOut` in the summary row: free, not charged), and the comparison then sets aside other specs. When fewer than 6 comparable offers are left across the sources, say so plainly with the counts ("4 offers to compare: the title filter left out 16 of 20, and AliExpress kept 1 of 10"), and offer a broader search, with no price on the offer (its estimate comes at the yes, when it needs one): the same sources with a shorter idea that drops a qualifier word ("silicone baking mat" instead of "half sheet silicone baking mat"), then the spec (size, thickness) read from each offer's title and `specs`, as above. Its cost from step 2: per AliExpress or Alibaba source USD 0.02 + up to 10 offers × USD 0.005, plus USD 0.005 for the idea; Made-in-China USD 0.005 + offers × USD 0.002 (for AliExpress and Alibaba together: 2 × (0.02 + 10 × 0.005) + 0.005 ≈ **USD 0.145 at most**; said as "about USD 0.15, occasionally a little more if I need to dig deeper to answer you", with a cap of **USD 0.30**, never said, which leaves the 2 sources × USD 0.02 reserved for the idea). Never build a cheapest offer or a verdict on one or two offers (`references/running-results.md`, "Coverage").
- **The visual moment, just above the trust-ranking table** (`references/running-results.md`, "Explaining results": "One visual, where it helps"; only where you can draw one, and otherwise nothing about it is said; never in the first answer to this skill's new reads, only in a follow-up turn: the full detail or list, a chart the user asks for, a deeper turn): **unit price against MOQ**, one dot per offer in that table (`unitPriceMin`, or `unitPriceAtQuantity` when a `quantity` was set, against `moq`), the MOQ axis on a log scale with its ticks labelled, each dot labelled with its `supplierName` and trust score; the user's order quantity as a marked line when they gave one. An offer with no MOQ or no price is named under it, not drawn. Caption: the sources and "list prices in US dollars, read <date>".
- **Who to contact first: a trust ranking.** Score each offer from its own fields and show the rule, so the user can disagree with it:
  - `supplierYears`: 1 point for 3+ years, 2 for 8+;
  - `supplierVerified` (audited or verified): 2 points;
  - `supplierLevel` Diamond or Gold: 1 point;
  - `supplierRating` 4.5 or more: 1 point (AliExpress store ratings count the same way).

  Show the top 5 by score, with unit price and MOQ next to it, and name the trade-off plainly: "the cheapest offer (USD 0.48) is from a 1-year supplier with no audit, score 0; the median offer (USD 0.61) from a 12-year audited one, score 5." The score is a first filter from public listing data, not a check of the business: suggest ordering samples before committing.
- **Price at the quantity they'll buy:** offer reruns with `quantity` at 100, 500 and 1,000 units, or read the `priceTiers` of the offers already found, to show the price breaks as a small table.
- **Margin** (if they gave a selling price and a marketplace): after that marketplace's published fees (`references/judgement.md`, "Margin after fees"): this run reads no fees, so take the rate from `marketplaceFees` in a market scan of this conversation, or say the margin is before fees and score it on the before-fee thresholds (cost at most 30% of the price favours, over 45% against). Freight, duty and ads are not in it unless a landed cost was shown. `validate` adds Amazon's FBA fee per product and combines this with demand and competition.
- **For you:** only with a profile (`references/judgement-profile.md`, "The seller profile and lenses"): compare the first order's cash (unit price × MOQ) with their budget band, and when the band is under it, lead with the way in: MOQ-1 offers (`lowestMoqOffer`, AliExpress) or a smaller first order. The facts above are the same for everyone.
- **When the margin is against,** say so once with its number, then the version that could work from these rows: a cheaper comparable offer at a higher quantity, a different spec, or a selling price above the market's p75 with a difference (`validate` checks that).
- When the answer shows only some of the offers, one plain line with the link to all of them ("All 30 supplier offers: <link>") (`references/plain-words.md`); then end on the next move, last, with no price: the supplier to ask for a sample first, a `quantity` rerun, or `validate` with this unit cost.
