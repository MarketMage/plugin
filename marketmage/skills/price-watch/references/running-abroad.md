# Running: outside the US

The part of `running.md` read before the first run whenever the marketplace, the user's country or a currency isn't the US one. The rest of the "Country" rules, and every other rule, are in `running.md`.

## In euros (or another currency)

- **In euros (or another currency), when the user works in one:** Apify bills in US dollars, and every estimate stays in US dollars. When the user works in EUR (they price in euros, sell on an EU marketplace, or ask "how much is that in euros?"), add the euro figure beside the dollar one **only at a rate they gave in this chat**, labelled as approximate at their rate: "about USD 0.14 (≈ €0.13 at your 0.92)". Without their rate, ask once, in the same line, whether they'd like euro figures and at what rate, and keep the dollars until they answer ("Apify bills in US dollars; tell me the rate you use and I'll add euros"). The rate they gave for a supplier cost ("Country" below) serves here too, said as theirs. Never a rate from memory, and never a euro figure without its "≈" and "at your rate". The cap is always set in US dollars. The same holds for pounds or any other currency.

## Country, outside the US

Once the marketplace is known (`running.md`, "Country"), without asking again:
- Amazon actors get that `marketplace` (or `amazonMarketplace`, `amazonMarketplaces`, `rankMarketplace`);
- Google Trends gets the same country as `geo`;
- TikTok Shop is skipped (it covers the US shop): say so once;
- every Amazon product read outside the US adds the `local-market` event (USD 0.003 a product, also on watch checks): put it in the estimate and in `maxRunCostUsd`;
- prices are compared in their own currencies, never converted.

- **a cost in another currency** (USD supplier prices or a USD quote against a GBP or EUR selling price, an RMB quote): never subtracted across currencies, and never converted at a rate from memory. Ask once, in one line, for **their rate or a landed cost in their selling currency** ("Your supplier prices are in US dollars and you sell in euros: what rate do you use, or what does one unit cost you landed in euros?"), in the message with the estimate, or, when the run went ahead without one, as one line in the answer. Use what they give, labelled as theirs, with its date if they give one ("at your rate of 0.92 € per USD, 3 Nov"). Until then, the price side and the cost side are shown apart, each in its own currency, and the margin is "not worked out yet: needs your rate" (`judgement-abroad.md`, "Mixed currencies").
- **US-only reads, said before the yes:** eBay (ebay.com), Etsy, Walmart, Target, Best Buy, Home Depot and Lowe's, and barcode store checks (`cleanpull/barcode-price-checker`: Amazon US, Best Buy and Google Shopping US) read the US sites. A user outside the US who asks for any of them hears it before the run (in the estimate, when a yes is needed): "these read the US sites (US prices and sellers, in US dollars)", with what that can still tell them (a US price reference, a market to compare) and what reads their own market (their Amazon marketplace, a store's own page). Never charge for a US-only read as if it were their market.
- **Search terms in the site's language:** reads of a non-English market (Amazon, Google Trends `geo`, the marketplace scan, the ad libraries) use the term shoppers there type ("faltbare Trinkflasche" on amazon.de and for Trends in Germany), and supplier reads (Made-in-China, AliExpress, Alibaba, DHgate) the English term, since those sites search in English. Say once which term went where. A term the user gave is used as given for its own language's reads.

- **Selling from another country than the marketplace's** ("I'm in Lagos and want to sell on Amazon US", "a seller in Shenzhen", "I live in Kenya"): the reads are the marketplace's, as above. With the judgement skills, the one optional question "Where are you selling from?" and the cross-border first-steps note follow `judgement-profile.md` ("Where they sell from, when it differs from the marketplace"); in any skill, never assume the user lives where the marketplace is, and never call a channel open to them that may accept sellers only from some countries (TikTok Shop US, Shopify Payments): name it as theirs to check.
- **Wherever the user is based:** MarketMage works for sellers based outside the US and the EU (Nigeria, China, Brazil, India, anywhere): the actors read the marketplaces, not the user's location, and every run bills their own Apify account in US dollars with the same caps. Say so plainly when they ask whether it works from their country, or when they say where they're based and wonder ("does this work from Lagos?": "Yes: it reads the marketplaces you'd sell on, from anywhere.").
