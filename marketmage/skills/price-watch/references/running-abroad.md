# Running: outside the US

The part of `running.md` read before the first run whenever the marketplace, the user's country or a currency isn't the US one. The rest of the "Country" rules, and every other rule, are in `running.md`.

## In euros (or another currency)

- **In euros (or another currency), when the user works in one:** Apify bills in US dollars, and every estimate stays in US dollars. When the user works in EUR (they price in euros, sell on an EU marketplace, or ask "how much is that in euros?"), add the euro figure beside the dollar one **only at a rate they gave in this chat**, labelled as approximate at their rate, beside the range (the ceiling may stay in dollars): "Usually USD 0.09–0.14 (≈ €0.08–0.13 at your 0.92); it can't go over USD 0.50". Without their rate, ask once, in the same line, whether they'd like euro figures and at what rate, and keep the dollars until they answer ("Apify bills in US dollars; tell me the rate you use and I'll add euros"). The rate they gave for a supplier cost ("Country" below) serves here too, said as theirs. Never a rate from memory, and never a euro figure without its "≈" and "at your rate". The ceiling and the cap are always set in US dollars. The same holds for pounds or any other currency.

## Country, outside the US

Once the marketplace is known (`running.md`, "Country"), without asking again:
- Amazon actors get that `marketplace` (or `amazonMarketplace`, `amazonMarketplaces`, `rankMarketplace`);
- Google Trends gets the same country as `geo`;
- TikTok Shop is skipped (it covers the US shop): say so once;
- every Amazon product read outside the US adds the `local-market` event (USD 0.003 a product, also on watch checks): put it in the estimate and in `maxRunCostUsd`;
- prices are compared in their own currencies, never converted.

- **a cost in another currency** (USD supplier prices or a USD quote against a GBP or EUR selling price, an RMB quote): never subtracted across currencies, and never converted at a rate from memory: at their rate when they give one, else at the dated reference rate (`judgement-rates.md`, "Reference exchange rates"), labelled with its date. Invite once, in one line, **their landed cost in their selling currency** to refine it ("Your supplier prices are in US dollars; I've used about USD 1.34 to £1, the rate on 6 Nov. If you know what one unit costs you landed in pounds, I'll use that"), in the message with the estimate, or, when the run went ahead without it, as one line in the answer. Use what they give, labelled as theirs, with its date if they give one ("at your rate of 0.92 € per USD, 3 Nov"). The margin is never held back for a rate: only a currency the reference doesn't list waits for theirs, its two sides shown apart until then (`judgement-abroad.md`, "Mixed currencies").
- **US-only reads, said before the yes:** eBay (ebay.com), Etsy, Walmart, Target, Best Buy, Home Depot and Lowe's, and barcode store checks (`cleanpull/barcode-price-checker`: Amazon US, Best Buy and Google Shopping US) read the US sites. A user outside the US who asks for any of them hears it before the run (in the estimate, when a yes is needed): "these read the US sites (US prices and sellers, in US dollars)", with what that can still tell them (a US price reference, a market to compare) and what reads their own market (their Amazon marketplace, a store's own page). Never charge for a US-only read as if it were their market.
- **Search terms in the site's language:** reads of a non-English market (Amazon, Google Trends `geo`, the marketplace scan, the ad libraries) use the term shoppers there type ("faltbare Trinkflasche" on amazon.de and for Trends in Germany), and supplier reads (Made-in-China, AliExpress, Alibaba, DHgate) the English term, since those sites search in English. Say once which term went where. A term the user gave is used as given for its own language's reads. With the user's own product known (their listing read, or named), the term is theirs, translated for each market; an example of another product ("faltbare Trinkflasche") is never put to them in its place.

- **Selling from another country than the marketplace's** ("I'm in Lagos and want to sell on Amazon US", "a seller in Shenzhen", "I live in Kenya"): the reads are the marketplace's, as above. With the judgement skills, the one optional question "Where are you selling from?" and the cross-border first-steps note follow `judgement-profile.md` ("Where they sell from, when it differs from the marketplace"); in any skill, never assume the user lives where the marketplace is, and never call a channel open to them that may accept sellers only from some countries (TikTok Shop US, Shopify Payments): name it as theirs to check.
- **Wherever the user is based:** MarketMage works for sellers based outside the US and the EU (Nigeria, China, Brazil, India, anywhere): the actors read the marketplaces, not the user's location, and every run bills their own Apify account in US dollars with the same caps. Say so plainly when they ask whether it works from their country, or when they say where they're based and wonder ("does this work from Lagos?": "Yes: it reads the marketplaces you'd sell on, from anywhere.").
