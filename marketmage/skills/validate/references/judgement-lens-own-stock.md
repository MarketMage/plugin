Part of the judgement rules (`judgement.md` is the core). Read with `judgement-for-you.md` when the lens is Own stock / FBA.

**Demand, for a seller on Amazon.** For FBA, or own stock sold on Amazon, Demand level for you reads from the Amazon sales floor alone; TikTok units are one context line, never `against` for you, never the reason the For you verdict or its confidence moves, and never the opening's number when the sales floor was read (as the deeper layer already holds: `judgement-deeper.md`, "Balance").

**With an exact amount given,** the gate's line never names the band's edge as their budget: it reads "Against the amount you gave me" below (`judgement-profile.md`, "Privacy, unchanged").

**The user's quote decides the gate.** When the user gives their own supplier quote (an MOQ and a unit price), the gate reads that quote: its MOQ × its unit price replaces the supplier summary's `moqMedian` × `unitPriceMedian`. A quote over the band's edge fails the gate, so "For you: not this one", said with the arithmetic as bands ("500 × USD 9 = USD 4,500, over your under-$1k range"). The offers read are then related versions that would pass, each a real offer row named with its own MOQ and price (`lowestMoqOffer`, or an offer row: "the 50-unit offer at USD 4.80, about USD 240"), never the fix that turns this verdict into "a fit, with one fix". Without a quote, the medians decide; they are a summary, never called an offer.

**Against the amount you gave me.** When the user named an exact amount in this chat (`judgement-profile.md`, "Privacy, unchanged": kept privately, never repeated), the gate reads that amount instead of the band's edge, and leaves room for the rest of a launch, as `launch-plan` plans it. The first order's goods (MOQ × unit or landed cost, from the user's quote or the medians as above):
- **at or under about half the amount** (a rule of thumb, said as such) passes: "fits the amount you gave me, with room for freight, samples, fees and launch ads";
- **over half, up to the whole amount** passes, with one line of information (never a cap): "fits the amount you gave me, with little left for freight, samples and ads; the 50-unit offer at USD 4.80, about USD 240, keeps room";
- **over the whole amount** fails, said with the arithmetic and without the figure: "200 × USD 4.80 = USD 960, more than the amount you gave me", with the way in as for a band (a real lower-MOQ offer row, a smaller first order); unless, without the user's own quote, a real offer row read in this run gives a start that fits (below, "A smaller real start that fits makes it a fit, with one fix"): then it doesn't fail.

**A smaller real start that fits makes it a fit, with one fix.** Without the user's own quote, the first order at the medians (`moqMedian` × `unitPriceMedian`) is a summary of the market, not an order anyone has to place. When it is over the amount they gave (or the band's edge), a real offer row read in this run can still give a start that fits, sized the way `launch-plan` sizes a first order: **the goods** are about half the amount (a rule of thumb, rounded down to USD 100s), or, with a band only, the band's goods figure (under $250: USD 100; under $1k: USD 400; $1k–5k: USD 1,200; $5k–25k: USD 4,000; $25k+: USD 10,000); **the units** are the goods ÷ that offer's own price, rounded down to a multiple of 10. That start passes when the units are at least the offer's MOQ and at least 20 (10 with an under-$250 budget): a first batch big enough to sell through and learn from. An offer whose MOQ alone costs more than the goods but no more than the whole amount (or the band's edge) also passes, at its MOQ, with the "little left for freight, samples and ads" room line. Either way the gate passes through that offer: "For you: a fit, with one fix", and the fix is that start, named as the offer row with its arithmetic ("start with the 50-unit offer at USD 4.80: 50 × USD 4.80 = USD 240, which fits the amount you gave me, with room for freight, samples and ads"; from a 1-unit offer at USD 6.10, "about 40 units × USD 6.10 = USD 244"). The medians' first order is still said, as one line of information ("a typical factory first order, 200 × USD 4.80 = USD 960, is more than the amount you gave me"), never as the reason for "not this one". This holds most plainly on a near-tie (a first order a few percent over), and for any gap such a start covers. A token start never counts: when the goods buy fewer units than that (a USD 250 item against about USD 600 buys 1), no offer fits, and the gate fails as above, with the way in. It never applies to the user's own quote: their quote is their plan, and it decides the gate as above (related versions, never the fix).

The amount itself is never said, nor worked back from ("half your budget is USD 300" repeats it). **With a band only,** the gate reads the band's edge as above (under $250: USD 250), and a first order that passes but is above `launch-plan`'s goods figure for the band (under $250: USD 100; under $1k: USD 400; $1k–5k: USD 1,200; $5k–25k: USD 4,000; $25k+: USD 10,000) gets the same room line ("fits if you're toward the top of your under-$1k range, with little left for freight, samples and ads"; under $250: "fits if you're toward the top of your under-$250 range, with little left for freight, samples and ads"), so the fit check and the launch plan never disagree. The room line is information, never a cap.
