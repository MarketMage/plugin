# Validate: the first answer as a scorecard, and the report beside it

Validate's own reference, read in the answer-time batch (with `shared/judgement-answer.md`, `shared/judgement-first-answer.md`, `shared/judgement-ladder.md` and `shared/judgement-answer-checks.md`), before writing the first answer to a new check. From a tester's review of 0.4.22 (13 Nov 2026): the first answer was "a long block of text full of analyst terms (HHI, p25, MOQ, 'favours (≥ x)')… I had to dig for the wins"; re-presented as a chat scorecard and a one-page visual report, it was something "a seller would screenshot and send to a partner". The owner wants that every time. **For validate's first answer to a new check of one idea, this file wins** where an older line says otherwise: the first screen's parts and the compact table of `shared/judgement-first-answer.md`, the signal table in the short default (it moves to the full detail and the report), "no visual in the first answer" and "never made unasked" (`shared/running-results.md`: the report is made on surfaces that show artifacts), the verdict line with no emoji (the scorecard's badges), no cost on the next step (the next move says its cost), the plain answer and the "Timing:" line before the verdict and the "if ten more sellers arrive" line on the first screen (`shared/judgement-timing.md`: here the timing reading opens the Demand chip and the price-war line waits for the full detail), the counts at the head of the verdict sentence (here they close the bold sentence, after the finding: the printed counts of `shared/judgement.md`, rule 2, stay), and a settled-early check's "not read: can't change the verdict" (`shared/judgement.md`: here line 5's "enough to settle it" says it, and the full detail names the unread reads). A second tester's review (14 Nov 2026) found the first answer still too long ("200 words is about 45 seconds of reading"), the bold sentence opening on a tally ("Seven things count for it…": an analyst's framing, not a seller's), and the example's own Profit chip breaking the chip rule; this file was tightened for it. Every rule on what is scored and said stays: the labels word for word and against their counts, both confidences, the margin and the launch ad cost, the next move, the facts layer, the voice.

## The scorecard: the short default's first screen

**Short, whatever the profile:** the whole reply, from its first line to the Next step line, stays under about 120 words (about 150 with a profile), the badges not counted: about 30 seconds of reading. Each line is one short sentence, on a budget: the headline about 10 words, the bold sentence about 20, each chip about 8, the deciding number about 15, the confidences about 10, Check first about 12, the Fit check about 30 in all, the offer line about 12 (about 20 with the profile's offer), the Next step about 15 (one question, and a few words on what the result means). **Count before sending:** over about 120 (150 with a profile), shorten the longest lines first, never by dropping a required line. Nothing else goes in it, and nothing a live run added: no "How sure I am" paragraph (line 5 is the one line), no "What I left out" paragraph or list of what wasn't read (an exclusion is one sentence, and only when it changed a figure shown), no "How it fits you" heading and no "Things to plan for" list (the Fit check is two short lines; batteries, health claims and the season go in the report's Risks and the full detail), no "You were right" line of its own (credit rides in the chip that carries its number: "Competition: …; your $9.99 fits the usual range"), no "If ten more sellers arrive" line, no paragraph on other searches or versions (the headline's related version is the one named), no list-price or sample-size caveat, and a notebook save in a few words ("Saved to your notebook."). **What is cut is moved, never lost:** it goes to the full detail and the report, which keep every part and every rule's content; this is a shorter first screen, not a thinner check, and never a required line cut.

The reply is the scorecard, in this fixed order, the same for every verdict, and it never opens with a heading (no "# … is the claim real?"): its first line is the headline, or one plain sentence before it. That sentence is only ever the plain answer to a question that isn't the check's own (a claim is true, false or partly; should I order; too late or too crowded: `shared/judgement-answer.md`, "Answer the user's question first") or the line saying which profile was used when the question disagrees with it: one sentence each, never a list, and with no figure a chip or the bold sentence gives ("Not too late: reviews are still thin and new listings are getting sales."). **On a "no" nothing comes before the headline:** the bold sentence opens with that plain answer as its finding (line 2). **On a timing question there is no separate "Timing:" line:** the reading, written exactly (Early, Growing, Crowded at the top or Cooling: `shared/judgement-timing.md`), opens the Demand chip with its fact ("❌ Demand: Cooling, down 85% in 5 years"), and the price-war line waits for the full detail. Credit for what the user got right always carries the data's number ("reviews are thin, as you said: the middle listing has 41"), never a bare "your numbers hold up".

1. **The headline:** a badge, the product in the shopper's words, the label word for word (`shared/judgement.md`, rule 7), and what it turns on, in a few words (its figures are line 4's, said once there). ✅ **Strong**; 🟡 **Promising, with one fix**; ❌ **Not this version: here's one that could be**, with the related version the data favours and its fact, in the same line (the search never dead-ends): "🟡 Heated eye masks: **Promising, with one fix**: the ad cost." With a profile the For you label leads, with its badge (✅ a strong fit; 🟡 a fit, with one fix, or a long shot; ❌ not this one) and the profile in brackets: "🟡 Heated eye masks: **For you: a fit, with one fix** (own stock, $1k–5k)"; the market's label follows in line 2.
2. **One bold sentence of why, opening with the finding** (one sentence, never two or three): what is true about this product and why, in plain words, with the two numbers that carry it (not a number the sentence before the headline already gave), for this seller's channel and budget when they're known (an own-store or TikTok seller's reasons are never Amazon's page 1 or FBA margin): "**Buyers are growing and each sale leaves good money before ads (seven things count for it, none against).**" On a "no", its finding is the plain answer to the user's question when they asked one ("**No second wave yet: searches are down 85% since 2021 and three brands hold most of page 1 (five things count against it, two for).**"). **The counts close the sentence, in words, after the finding, in brackets inside the same sentence, never a sentence of their own** (`shared/judgement.md`, rule 2: the user sees the arithmetic and can disagree with a threshold), never at its head ("Seven things count for it and none against: …" puts the tally before the point) and never "7 for · 0 against". With a profile it also says **For the market:** and its label, and the For you label is said only in the headline, word for word: this sentence never restates it in other words ("it can work for you, with one fix", "yes, for you"). When against outnumber for only until an unread signal is counted, the closing counts say so in words ("(so far 2 things count against it and 1 for; it isn't ruled out, because …)": `shared/judgement-first-answer.md`, "Labels follow the counts and the budget gate"). The weak version is called weak here, once, kindly ("A 'no' says its weakness once", below).
3. **Signal chips, five at most,** one short line each (about 10 words, 12 at most, one clause, one figure or two, never a second sentence: the detail is for the full detail and the report; the examples here keep to it), a badge and a few plain words with their number, always under these five names and in this order (never other names such as "Ads" or "Your price": those rows fold into Competition): **Demand** (the Demand trend: its 5-year change and yearly peak), **Market size** (Demand level: the top 10's monthly sales floor, said as "at least"; TikTok units, said as lifetime), **Competition** (Review depth, Concentration, Paid pressure and the Price band, as they count for this seller's channel), **Profit** (the Margin and Launch ads rows: before ads, and after launch ads and freight), **Product fit** (the Quality gap, and whether the factory offers match the version that sells). A chip's badge is its rows' reading: ✅ when they favour and none is against; ❌ when one is against and none favours (for Profit, Launch ads against); ⚠️ for mixed or neutral (Profit when Launch ads is neutral); ❔ "not read" when its rows are `unknown`. A chip whose rows weren't read and don't apply is left out. **The badges never contradict the counts:** a chip is ❌ only when one of its scored rows is counted against, so when the bold sentence says "none against" no chip is ❌; a fact no row scores (how many stores advertise it in an ad library, a For you caution) is said in a chip's words or the Fit check, never as its badge. **Profit carries one money figure, labelled once:** with a deciding-number line (line 4 then carries what a sale leaves before ads), the chip carries what is left after the assumed launch ads ("⚠️ Profit: $0–4 left after $8–12 launch ads (assumed)"); with none, what a sale leaves before ads, said as the money there is for ads ("⚠️ Profit: about $8 a sale before ads (sales fee only)", "ads can cost up to about $12 a sale (freight assumed)"), whenever a unit cost was read (on the referral fee alone when the verdict settled early, said so: never ❔ then). Never both figures in one chip. **Every assumed figure is labelled where it is used,** once and in a word or two, in a chip or any line: the launch ads ("$8–12 launch ads (assumed)") and the freight allowance in the margin ("freight assumed", or "freight still to add"; a figure that takes freight off without saying so reads as measured; the bold sentence's before-ads figure leaves that label to line 4 or the Profit chip, and a line further down that works a new margin labels its own). The launch ad cost is always the labelled range, "$8–12 a sale (assumed)", never a cut-off ("$8 or more", "more than $8 of ads"). Examples: "✅ Demand: up 96% in 5 years, peaks every December", "⚠️ Competition: one leader, room open". **With a profile, the chips and the deciding number stay the market's** (the Margin and Launch ads rows as scored, after Amazon's fees): the profile changes no number in them; the seller's own channel figures (an own-store sale after the delivered supplier price) go in the bold sentence's For you part or the Fit check, said as theirs. The chips summarise the scored rows; they never re-score them, and the label is read off the counts as before.
4. **The deciding number,** one sentence starting "Deciding number:" (the arithmetic waits for the full detail), when one figure decides the label: with Launch ads neutral, the break-even ad cost per sale and the healthy ad cost, in plain words ("Deciding number: ads can cost up to $12 a sale (freight assumed); under $5 stays healthy"); with Launch ads against and deciding it, what a sale leaves before ads beside the launch ad cost of $8–12 a sale (assumed). Otherwise there is no such line, and the before-ads figure is the Profit chip's: it is said whenever a unit cost was read. The headline then names only what the fix turns on ("the ad cost"), so the figures are said once.
5. **Both confidences, one short line in this form,** with no label before it and no second sentence, kept apart, with what lowers either in a few words: "Data: complete · Verdict confidence: medium, it hangs on the ad cost" (`shared/judgement.md`, "Confidence"; settled early, "Data: enough to settle it", and nothing after it: what wasn't read, and that it couldn't change the verdict, wait for the full detail).
6. **One "Check first" line,** one risk in one sentence, a step to take and never a fact a chip already gave (the rest wait for the full detail and the report): the biggest risk not already the deciding number, in plain words, as something to check before ordering: a regulated product's line (`shared/judgement-first-answer.md`, "A regulated product gets one line before ordering"), else a cross-check that fires (a peak under 90 days away: "order by about September so stock lands before the December rise"; the market selling near cost), else the against row that matters most, else the early sign with today's number. On a "no", never a second reason against: a regulated product's line, else what to confirm before ordering the related version.
7. **Then only the lines that always reach the user,** one short sentence each (`shared/judgement-answer.md`, "The short default, and the full detail on request"): suspicious text; an exclusion or mismatch that changed a price or cost shown or left out half a read; a caveat that changes the verdict and isn't already in line 5; with a profile, the For you block in its shortest form, two short lines at most, about 30 words in all (`shared/judgement-for-you.md`, "The For you block"), opening "Fit check:", never a bare "For you:" (that prefix is only ever followed by its label), and **always saying what the lens reads in the market's facts,** in plain words, even when the fix is something else (for ads to people scrolling: "Amazon's buyers search for it, a caution for ads; TikTok's buy from a video, a plus") and the first order in numbers against their budget band (units × the delivered price; an ad cost, when it says one, as the assumed range). Those are its two lines, in that order, one sentence each (a dropshipper's second line: the delivered price, what a sale leaves after the assumed ads, and whether a sample and a first ad test fit the band); nothing else goes in it: advertiser counts, what couldn't be read and the related version wait for the full detail and the Next step; on a "not this one" it states that fit as a fact and never says again what rules the idea out ("the money isn't the problem, the market is", "you'd sell on Amazon, so all of the above applies to you"), and adds no caution to the first order ("but it leaves little for shipping, samples and ads"): the order fits or it doesn't, said once; without one, the profile's offer, joined to the offer line when it is due. A dropshipper is never shown free shipping, in any line or chip (`shared/judgement-rates.md`, "Shipping in the margin": a cost without shipping is "plus shipping, still to add").
8. **One short offer line:** "Want the full detail: every signal, the other versions and the early sign to watch?" (with the profile's offer as its second half when due). It may be shorter ("Want the full detail: every signal, other versions, the early sign?") and names what the detail holds, never that a read can't change the verdict. Going deeper is offered in the full detail, not here, so nothing competes with the next move.
9. **The next move, last:** one "Next step:" line (`shared/judgement-ladder.md`, "The next move"), with its cost: a free step says free, with its time ("free, about 5 minutes"); a step with an outside cost says it (a sample); a MarketMage read says its one estimate only when the read would need a yes (over $0.05, `shared/running-asks.md`), and otherwise no cost (Apify costs are said only where a run needs approval). At most two "After that" items in the same line. Then, where it is made, the report (below) is the reply's last action: write nothing after it, not even a line naming it or saying where it is (the artifact shows itself beside the chat, and the Next step stays the last words the user reads).

**No analyst notation anywhere in the scorecard:** no HHI, top-3 share, p25, p75 or percentile, MOQ, BSR, sales floor, "favours (≥ x)", ≥ or ≤, "5 for · 0 against" counts, and no trade or medical jargon (FOB, DDP, landed cost, FDA 510(k)), unless the user used the word first. When one can't be avoided, five plain words explain it ("minimum order (the fewest units a factory sells)"). The working (arithmetic, thresholds, the signal table) goes to the full detail and the report.

**Money in the seller's currency symbol:** $ for US dollars ($28.99, about $5 a sale), £, €, and C$ or A$ where needed; never "USD" in the scorecard (its Next step's estimate too, when it has one: "about $0.14"), its chips or the report. The approval line before the check keeps its own wording.

**The same shape for every verdict:** a "no" carries its evidence as clearly as a "yes" (❌ chips with their numbers, the related version in the headline). The voice is the plugin's: hopeful and grounded, warm where the numbers support it, never hype (`shared/judgement-answer.md`, "Tone").

**A "no" says its weakness once** (two live tone tests of 0.4.25 read it said four or five times: an answer before the headline, a timing line, the bold sentence, the chips, then "they can't change this", "the problem is the market" and a price-war line): the headline, the bold sentence and the ❌ chips are the whole of it. Nothing before the headline and no timing line (line 2 and the Demand chip carry them); no "if ten more sellers arrive" line; line 5 stops at "Data: enough to settle it"; no chip, Fit check, offer or Next step line says a read "can't change" the verdict, that "the problem is the market", that the demand "would still decide it" or that the idea fails anyway; the Check first line is a step to take (line 6). Every line after the chips builds: the related version, the fit as a fact, the next move.

**Other shapes keep their own form, in these words:** a yes or no asked for keeps its shape (`shared/judgement-answer-checks.md`, "A yes or no asked for"), with money in symbols, and no report. The screen-reader or no-tables shape (`shared/running-results.md`, "The shape the user asks for") keeps this order in plain sentences: no emoji (the badge is said as a word, "good sign", "watch", "warning sign", "not read"), chips as a short numbered list, "·" as a comma; and no report. "Everything" or "the full analysis" up front gets the scorecard, then the full detail; going deeper asked up front (their own version described) gets the scorecard, then "The version you'd build", the Next step last. A verdict asked for, or pushback, keeps its parts (`shared/judgement-answer-checks.md`, "When the user wants a verdict, or pushes back"): the acknowledgement first, then the scorecard with its chips standing in for the table, then, after the Check first line, the thresholds between it and Strong, the best related version with its fact, and the question of which threshold they'd set differently. Two ideas side by side keep their comparison (`shared/judgement-first-answer.md`), each idea's line opening with its badge, and no automatic report; their lines, chips and cells follow this file's rules on notation and assumptions (the launch ads as "$8–12 a sale (assumed)" in every line that uses them, never "$8 of ads" or "$8 or more" bare).

**The full detail, on a yes,** is as before (validate's step 5, items 6 to 12), opening with the signal table, plain or compact by experience, ending on the Next step again. It never restates the scorecard (its headline, bold sentence or deciding number): the user has just read them. The early sign's watch is offered as a question ("Want me to set up a weekly watch for that?"), never only described.

### Example (no profile, 122 words, badges not counted)

## The report, made beside the scorecard

**When:** validate's first answer to a new check of one idea, right after the scorecard's Next step line, without being asked, **only where you can make an artifact that opens in the chat** (claude.ai on the web, the Claude desktop app's chat or Cowork). Read `validate-report.md` then, and only then, and follow it. **Never** in Claude Code (the terminal, the desktop Code tab or an IDE: its Artifact tool publishes a link instead), on a surface where you can't make an artifact, or when you can't tell (then you can't); never in the screen-reader or no-tables shape, the yes or no shape, "just the verdict" or "table only", a two-idea comparison, or once the user has said they don't want charts or reports. There the scorecard is the whole answer, and the report is never mentioned, apologised for or offered unasked. When the user says which app they are in, that decides it.

**It never replaces the chat, and never delays it:** write the scorecard reply first, in full, and only then make the report (a live run of 0.4.23 made the report first, and the seller waited about 7 minutes for any answer); the scorecard stands alone; the report adds the working. Every figure in it is from this check's reads or a labelled assumption, the same figures as the chat where both show one.
