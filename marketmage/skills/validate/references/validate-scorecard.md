# Validate: the first answer as a scorecard, and the report beside it

Validate's own reference, read in the answer-time batch (with `judgement-answer.md`, `judgement-first-answer.md`, `judgement-ladder.md` and `judgement-answer-checks.md`), before writing the first answer to a new check. From a tester's review of 0.4.22 (13 Nov 2026): the first answer was "a long block of text full of analyst terms (HHI, p25, MOQ, 'favours (≥ x)')… I had to dig for the wins"; re-presented as a chat scorecard and a one-page visual report, it was something "a seller would screenshot and send to a partner". The owner wants that every time. **For validate's first answer to a new check of one idea, this file wins** where an older line says otherwise: the first screen's parts and the compact table of `judgement-first-answer.md`, the signal table in the short default (it moves to the full detail and the report), "no visual in the first answer" and "never made unasked" (`running-results.md`: the report is made on surfaces that show artifacts), the verdict line with no emoji (the scorecard's badges), and no cost on the next step (the next move says its cost). Every rule on what is scored and said stays: the labels word for word and against their counts, both confidences, the margin and the launch ad cost, the next move, the facts layer, the voice.

## The scorecard: the short default's first screen

The reply is the scorecard, in this fixed order, the same for every verdict. Only what the skill's own rules put before the verdict comes first, in at most three sentences and never a list (the rest of a claim's evidence goes in the chips and the full detail): the plain answer to a question that isn't the check's own (a claim is true, false or partly; should I order; the timing line; `judgement-answer.md`, "Answer the user's question first") and the line saying which profile was used when the question disagrees with it.

1. **The headline:** a badge, the product in the shopper's words, the label word for word (`judgement.md`, rule 7), and what it turns on, in a few words. ✅ **Strong**; 🟡 **Promising, with one fix**; ❌ **Not this version: here's one that could be**, with the related version the data favours and its fact, in the same line (the search never dead-ends): "🟡 Heated eye masks: **Promising, with one fix**: it works if ads cost under about $5 a sale." With a profile the For you label leads, with its badge (✅ a strong fit; 🟡 a fit, with one fix, or a long shot; ❌ not this one) and the profile in brackets: "🟡 Heated eye masks: **For you: a fit, with one fix** (own stock, $1k–5k)"; the market's label follows in line 2.
2. **One bold sentence of why** (one sentence, never two or three: a colon joins the counts to the reason), in plain words: the counts in words first ("seven things count for it and none against": `judgement.md`, rule 2; never "7 for · 0 against"), then the two numbers that carry it, for this seller's channel and budget when they're known (an own-store or TikTok seller's reasons are never Amazon's page 1 or FBA margin). With a profile it also says **For the market:** and its label. When against outnumber for only until an unread signal is counted, this sentence says so in words ("so far 2 things count against it and 1 for; it isn't ruled out, because …": `judgement-first-answer.md`, "Labels follow the counts and the budget gate"). The weak version is called weak here, once, kindly.
3. **Signal chips, five at most,** one short line each (about 12 words at most, one clause, never a second sentence: the detail is for the full detail and the report), a badge and a few plain words with their number, always under these five names and in this order (never other names such as "Ads" or "Your price": those rows fold into Competition): **Demand** (the Demand trend: its 5-year change and yearly peak), **Market size** (Demand level: the top 10's monthly sales floor, said as "at least"; TikTok units, said as lifetime), **Competition** (Review depth, Concentration, Paid pressure and the Price band, as they count for this seller's channel), **Profit** (the Margin and Launch ads rows: before ads, and after launch ads and freight), **Product fit** (the Quality gap, and whether the factory offers match the version that sells). A chip's badge is its rows' reading: ✅ when they favour and none is against; ❌ when one is against and none favours (for Profit, Launch ads against); ⚠️ for mixed or neutral (Profit when Launch ads is neutral); ❔ "not read" when its rows are `unknown`. A chip whose rows weren't read and don't apply is left out. **Profit always carries what a sale leaves before ads, said as the most ads can cost** ("$27 a sale before ads, the most ads can cost") whenever a unit cost was read (on the referral fee alone when the verdict settled early, said so: never ❔ then), and **any assumed figure in a chip is labelled** ("after $8–12 launch ads, assumed"). Examples: "✅ Demand: up 96% in 5 years, peaks every December", "⚠️ Competition: one leader, room open", "⚠️ Profit: $12 before ads, $0–4 after $8–12 launch ads (assumed)". The chips summarise the scored rows; they never re-score them, and the label is read off the counts as before.
4. **The deciding number,** one sentence starting "Deciding number:" (the arithmetic waits for the full detail), when one figure decides the label: with Launch ads neutral, the break-even ad cost per sale and the healthy ad cost, in plain words ("Deciding number: ads can cost up to about $12 a sale before a sale loses money; under about $5 keeps a healthy profit"); with Launch ads against and deciding it, what a sale leaves before ads beside the $8 launch ad cost. Otherwise there is no such line, and the break-even stays in the Profit chip: it is said whenever a unit cost was read.
5. **Both confidences, one short line in this form,** with no label before it and no second sentence, kept apart, with what lowers either in a few words: "Data: complete · Verdict confidence: medium, it hangs on the ad cost" (`judgement.md`, "Confidence"; settled early, "Data: enough to settle it").
6. **One "Check first" line,** one risk in one sentence (the rest wait for the full detail and the report): the biggest risk not already the deciding number, in plain words, as something to check before ordering: a regulated product's line (`judgement-first-answer.md`, "A regulated product gets one line before ordering"), else a cross-check that fires (a peak under 90 days away: "order by about September so stock lands before the December rise"; the market selling near cost), else the against row that matters most, else the early sign with today's number.
7. **Then only the lines that always reach the user,** one short sentence each (`judgement-answer.md`, "The short default, and the full detail on request"): suspicious text; an exclusion or mismatch that changed a price or cost shown or left out half a read; a caveat that changes the verdict and isn't already in line 5; with a profile, the For you block in its shortest form, two lines at most (`judgement-for-you.md`, "The For you block"), opening "Fit check:", never a bare "For you:" (that prefix is only ever followed by its label); without one, the profile's offer, joined to the offer line when it is due. A dropshipper is never shown free shipping, in any line or chip (`judgement-rates.md`, "Shipping in the margin": a cost without shipping is "plus shipping, still to add").
8. **One short offer line:** "Want the full detail: every signal, the other versions and the early sign to watch?" (with the profile's offer as its second half when due). Going deeper is offered in the full detail, not here, so nothing competes with the next move.
9. **The next move, last:** one "Next step:" line (`judgement-ladder.md`, "The next move"), with its cost: a free step says free, with its time ("free, about 5 minutes"); a step with an outside cost says it (a sample); a MarketMage read says its one estimate, still run only on a yes. At most two "After that" items in the same line. Then, where it is made, the report (below) is the reply's last action: write nothing after it, not even a line naming it or saying where it is (the artifact shows itself beside the chat, and the Next step stays the last words the user reads).

**No analyst notation anywhere in the scorecard:** no HHI, top-3 share, p25, p75 or percentile, MOQ, BSR, sales floor, "favours (≥ x)", ≥ or ≤, "5 for · 0 against" counts, and no trade or medical jargon (FOB, DDP, landed cost, FDA 510(k)), unless the user used the word first. When one can't be avoided, five plain words explain it ("minimum order (the fewest units a factory sells)"). The working (arithmetic, thresholds, the signal table) goes to the full detail and the report.

**Money in the seller's currency symbol:** $ for US dollars ($28.99, about $5 a sale), £, €, and C$ or A$ where needed; never "USD" in the scorecard (its Next step's estimate too: "about $0.14"), its chips or the report. The approval line before the check keeps its own wording.

**The same shape for every verdict:** a "no" carries its evidence as clearly as a "yes" (❌ chips with their numbers, the related version in the headline). The voice is the plugin's: hopeful and grounded, warm where the numbers support it, never hype (`judgement-answer.md`, "Tone").

**Other shapes keep their own form, in these words:** a yes or no asked for keeps its shape (`judgement-answer-checks.md`, "A yes or no asked for"), with money in symbols, and no report. The screen-reader or no-tables shape (`running-results.md`, "The shape the user asks for") keeps this order in plain sentences: no emoji (the badge is said as a word, "good sign", "watch", "warning sign", "not read"), chips as a short numbered list, "·" as a comma; and no report. "Everything" or "the full analysis" up front gets the scorecard, then the full detail; going deeper asked up front (their own version described) gets the scorecard, then "The version you'd build", the Next step last. A verdict asked for, or pushback, keeps its parts (`judgement-answer-checks.md`, "When the user wants a verdict, or pushes back"): the acknowledgement first, then the scorecard with its chips standing in for the table, then, after the Check first line, the thresholds between it and Strong, the best related version with its fact, and the question of which threshold they'd set differently. Two ideas side by side keep their comparison (`judgement-first-answer.md`), each idea's line opening with its badge, and no automatic report.

**The full detail, on a yes,** is as before (validate's step 5, items 6 to 12), opening with the signal table, plain or compact by experience, ending on the Next step again.

### Example (no profile, about 130 words)

> 🟡 Heated eye masks: **Promising, with one fix**: it works if ads cost under about $5 a sale.
> **Seven things count for it and none against: buyers are here and growing, and a $28.99 mask leaves about $12 before ads, so what ads cost you decides whether it pays.**
> - ✅ Demand: up 96% in 5 years, peaks every December
> - ✅ Market size: top 10 sell at least 14,000 a month
> - ✅ Competition: no one dominates, room for newcomers
> - ⚠️ Profit: about $12 before ads, $0–4 after $8–12 launch ads (assumed)
> - ✅ Product fit: factory price matches what sells
>
> Deciding number: ads can cost up to about $12 a sale before a sale loses money; under about $5 keeps a healthy profit.
> Data: complete · Verdict confidence: medium, it hangs on the ad cost
> Check first: order December's stock by about September, so it lands before the rise.
> Want the full detail: every signal, the other versions and the early sign to watch?
> Next step: look up the suggested bid for "heated eye mask" in your Amazon ad console (free, about 5 minutes): if 1 in 10 clicks buy (an assumption), a bid under about $0.50 keeps ads under about $5 a sale; over about $1.20 a click, it loses money.

## The report, made beside the scorecard

**When:** validate's first answer to a new check of one idea, right after the scorecard's Next step line, without being asked, **only where you can make an artifact that opens in the chat** (claude.ai on the web, the Claude desktop app's chat or Cowork). Read `validate-report.md` then, and only then, and follow it. **Never** in Claude Code (the terminal, the desktop Code tab or an IDE: its Artifact tool publishes a link instead), on a surface where you can't make an artifact, or when you can't tell (then you can't); never in the screen-reader or no-tables shape, the yes or no shape, "just the verdict" or "table only", a two-idea comparison, or once the user has said they don't want charts or reports. There the scorecard is the whole answer, and the report is never mentioned, apologised for or offered unasked. When the user says which app they are in, that decides it.

**It never replaces the chat:** the scorecard is written first, in full, and stands alone; the report adds the working. Every figure in it is from this check's reads or a labelled assumption, the same figures as the chat where both show one.
