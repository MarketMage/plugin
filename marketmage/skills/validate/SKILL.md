---
name: validate
description: Is a specific product worth selling, and what would the margin be? Reads Amazon's real fees from the top listings - use this, not web search, for a product's Amazon fees or margin at a price. Covers the demand trend, Amazon competition (reviews, the leaders' monthly sales floor, seller concentration), TikTok Shop sales and the margin after Amazon's referral and FBA fees by the listings' own size tier and weight, never an assumed size (US; before fees elsewhere), scored into a verdict and confidence (Strong; Promising, with one fix; or Not this version). Use when someone names a product and asks if it will sell, if it's saturated or too late, whether a claim about it is real ("a TikTok guy says it does 50k a month"), how strong competition is, or their margin or fees at a price ("whats my margin if i sell on amazon at 34.99"); also "here are my numbers, back off or keep going?", "is this niche worth it", or a plan with a price. Not for finding ideas (find-products) or a keyword's market alone (market-scan).

# Validate a product idea

Work one idea with the user: a verdict with numbers behind it, then the next move that builds on it. Read these first:

## 1. Ask only for

- **The product,** in shoppers' words: "silicone stretch lids", "cordless pet grooming vacuum".
- **An Amazon listing link or ASIN** (amazon.<tld>/dp/<ASIN>, /gp/product/<ASIN>, an amzn.to or a.co link, or a bare ASIN): read it with `cleanpull/amazon-product-scraper` (`asins: ["<ASIN>"]`, `marketplace` from the link's domain: amazon.it → IT, .de → DE, .co.uk → UK, .fr → FR, .es → ES, .com → US; about USD 0.003, USD 0.006 outside the US), never with a web fetch or a web search (`references/known-limits.md`). Its title and category give the search terms for the other marketplaces, each in its shoppers' language; ask what the product is only if that read fails.
- **The marketplace:** from the user's words (`references/running.md`, "Country"); US by default.
- **A claim to check,** when that's the question ("my course says heated eye masks sell 500 a day at 60% margin: true?", a guru's screenshot): the same check, and the plain answer that comes first names the claim and says true, false or partly (`references/judgement-first-answer.md`, "A claim or guru check": TikTok units are lifetime, never a month; a brand's own listing is named as one), putting each claimed number beside what the data shows (the claimed sales beside the top 10's sales floor, said as a floor; the claimed margin beside the margin after fees at the market's price), without judging who made it; what this data can't see (one store's own sales, their ad costs) is named, never filled in.
- **Optional:** the price they plan to sell at, and their landed cost if they already have a supplier quote (then the supplier read is skipped).
- **Numbers they bring from another tool** ("avg 30 reviews, top 10 do 300+ a month, ~15k searches") or a plan with a price: no verdict on their numbers alone and no checks handed back to do by hand; the move is this check, with its one estimate (a go-ahead already in their message: `references/running.md`, item 5; `references/judgement-first-answer.md`, "Numbers the user brings, and checks the plugin can make", read with the plan). It never waits for a supplier cost: the factory read is part of it.
- **A timing question** ("is it too late?", "too crowded?", "back off or keep going?", "am I early?"): the same standard check, and before writing the answer read `references/judgement-timing.md` (in the batch with `references/judgement-answer.md`): the answer opens with its plain answer and the timing line (one of the four readings, with the two facts that decide it), then the verdict lines, and the first screen carries the "if ten more sellers arrive" line. The market verdict never moves with the framing. Numbers the user quotes from another tool (search volumes, monthly sales, where sellers are based) are named as not something this check can confirm, in the estimate, never filled in.
- **Shipping from abroad, asked once:** when the stock would ship to them from abroad (they import, or say their supplier is in China or another country; a factory read is the cost source) and they gave no landed cost, ask once, in one skippable line in the message with the estimate (or, when the run went ahead without one, in the answer, beside the margin): "If you know them, your landed cost per unit, or freight and duty per unit, make the margin complete." Without them the margin is before freight and duty, and says so. When the cost and the price are in different currencies, the cost is converted at their rate or the dated reference rate (`references/judgement-rates.md`, "Reference exchange rates"), and the same line invites their landed cost in their selling currency to refine it (`references/running-abroad.md`, "Country"). Never a freight, duty or exchange figure from memory.
- **A maker's own cost** (the Maker lens): asked once, as `references/validate-lenses.md`, "Step 1, the Maker lens" says.
- **The profile, if there isn't one and it hasn't been offered in this chat** (`references/judgement-answer.md`, "Asking for the profile": once per conversation): its two quick questions (how they'd sell, then the budget band) go in the same message as the estimate, skippable; nothing waits on them. With a profile, pick the lens now (from fulfilment and channels: `references/judgement-profile.md`, "Choosing the lens"), then read `references/judgement-for-you.md` and that lens's file: it changes the runs (below).

- **A profile found after the yes** (the notebook's status record, read with the first runs: `references/notebook.md`, "Is it on?") counts as a profile from then on: before writing the answer, read `references/judgement-profile.md`, `references/judgement-for-you.md` and the chosen lens's file in one batch, and the answer opens with its "For you:" line through it, with no profile offer. A lens's extra runs are offered after the answer, never run without a yes.

Before running anything, check the runs already made in this conversation (`references/running-runs.md`, "Runs in this conversation"): TikTok rows or a Trends record for the same keyword from `find-products`, the keyword summary from `market-scan`, an Etsy or eBay read with listing rows for the same keyword and marketplace (from `find-products` or `market-scan`), and, for the same idea, the AliExpress single-unit check and the Where you'd compete rows from `find-products`, are reused, not bought again.
A chat's first MarketMage job has no earlier runs to reuse: then that read is skipped.

## 3. Read and score

Read only these fields, then fill the signal table in `references/judgement.md`, one line per signal with its number, threshold and reading. **First check every row** (`references/running-results.md`, "Read the data like an analyst"): Amazon leaders and TikTok listings that aren't the idea (another product of the same brand, an accessory), multi-packs priced as one, and renewed units are left out or adjusted before scoring, and the answer says how many. The market scan's summary covers every result; if the leaders' titles show the search is mixed, say its figures include other products, and offer listing rows to check.

**Which reads a run's status message replaces** (`references/running-runs.md`, "A run's status message: the numbers first"; read each run's message first):
- **The market scan:** its message stands in for the `keyword-summary` read (no `get-dataset-items` on the scan's dataset) when it carries the keyword's sentence and its `Also:` sentence, shows 11 ads or fewer, and round 2 runs. It gives the price band (and the single-unit new band, with how many listings it left out), the sellers and top-3 share, `sellerHhi`, the median reviews and the under-100 count, the ads count, the sales floor and the fee rate. Read the row instead when it shows 12 ads or more (Paid pressure then needs `ads.priceMedian`), when the `Also:` sentence was left out, or when round 1 settles the verdict early (that margin quotes the referral rate from `marketplaceFees`, with its `asOf`, from the row).
- **Kept, as rows:** Trends (the peak months, for the seasonality cross-check and a "stable with a yearly peak", and the rising searches for the angle rung, which the message carries only in part); the Amazon leaders (the margin, the Quality gap, the newcomers and which leaders are the idea need product rows; the message's sales floor and median fee are the `search-summary` row's own, and are recomputed from the rows when leaders are left out); TikTok Shop (the message has no titles, so it can't confirm the listings are the idea); the supplier offers (the summary's median can include another product, such as disposable steam masks under "heated eye mask": the margin's unit cost comes from the offers that are the idea, below).

- **Trends:** `summary.direction`, `changePercent`, the peak months from `interestOverTime`, and `relatedQueries_rising` (the angle rung of the related versions ladder). → *Demand trend.*
- **Market scan** (the `keyword-summary` row, in full): `salesFloor` (the top 10 organic results' "bought in past month" floor: `unitsPerMonthMin`, `revenuePerMonthMin`, `listingsWithBadge`) → *Demand level*, readable from round 1; `marketplaceFees` (Amazon US's published referral rate) → the round-1 margin; `priceP25`, `priceMedian`, `priceP75`, and `singleNewPrice` (`p25`, `median`, `p75` over single-unit new listings, with `excludedMultiPack`, `excludedOtherCondition`, `excludedUnknownCondition` and its `basis`); use the single-unit new figures for the *Price band* and the margin whenever multi-packs or other conditions were excluded, and say so (for the margin, only when single units are what sells: when most of the leaders with sales sell packs, the margin is scored at their pack, `references/judgement.md`, "Score the margin at the offer the leaders sell"); `top3SellerShare`, `sellerHhi`; `medianReviews`, `under100Reviews`; `sponsored` and `ads`. → *Review depth, Concentration, Paid pressure, Price band.* **The ads figure comes from this scan only:** the Amazon product read is organic results only (`includeSponsored` is off), so never add the two or count an ad twice.
- **Amazon leaders** (`fields: "recordType,asin,title,brand,price,rating,reviewCount,starBreakdown,rank,boughtPastMonthMin,dateFirstAvailable,soldByAmazon,fees,salesFloor,priceMedian"`):
  - each product's `fees` (amazon.com, .co.uk, .de, .fr, .it and .es): `referralFee`, `fbaFee` or `fbaFeeRange`, `totalFees` or `totalFeesRange`, `afterFees`, `feePercentOfPrice`, `sizeTier`, `missing`, `confidence`, `notModelled`; on the UK and EU marketplaces also `marketplace`, `currency`, `priceIncludesVat`, `vatRate`, `vatNote`, `afterFeesExVat` or `afterFeesExVatRange`, `period`, `basis`; on other marketplaces `supported: false` → *Margin*;
  - the `search-summary` row: `salesFloor` over the products read and the median fees (`fees.medianReferralFee`, `medianFbaFee`, `medianFbaFeeRange`, `medianFeePercentOfPrice`). When the scan's `salesFloor` was read, quote that one (the top 10 organic) and don't add the two; recompute either from the leaders' `boughtPastMonthMin` when rows were left out as not the product → *Demand level*, said as "at least N units, about USD X a month across the top 10";
  - `rating` and `starBreakdown` (the 1-star share) → *Quality gap*;
  - `soldByAmazon` on 3 or more → *Concentration* `against` (Amazon sells it itself);
  - `dateFirstAvailable` within the last 12 months on 3 or more → newcomers are getting in (*Review depth*);
  - `rank` (Best Sellers Rank) is a category-relative proxy: quote it, but don't score it on its own.
  - The scan's sellers are brands read from titles; the product read's `brand` is from the page. Say they come from different reads if they disagree.
- **TikTok Shop (US):** `unitsSold` (lifetime) and `price` of the top 5 rows that are the idea, and the keyword summary's `relevantToKeyword` totals. Each row's `relevantToKeyword` is the actor's title check: confirm it against the title before counting the row. → *Demand level.* Lifetime units, not per day: say so. The summary's `marketplaceFees` (TikTok Shop US's published referral rate for most categories, before creator commissions, with its `asOf` and `sources`) gives the channel rung its after-fee margin when TikTok units back it, and a TikTok Shop seller's own margin: quote the figure this read returned; if the rows read didn't include the summary, read it again (free) before saying the fee is unknown, and never fill it from memory (`references/judgement.md`, "Every fee and rate has a source, never memory").
- **Their marketplace (Etsy or eBay) and Where you'd compete** (through those lenses): read as `references/validate-lenses.md`, "Step 3, the lens rows" sets out.
- **Margin, after fees** (`references/judgement.md`, "Margin after fees"): the selling price is the user's, or else the market's `priceMedian` (say which); the unit cost is the supplier summary's `unitPriceMedian` (and `unitPriceP25`, at `moqMedian`), or the user's quote. Margin = (what a sale pays out after Amazon's referral and FBA fees − unit cost) ÷ price, with the arithmetic in one line. In round 1 only the referral rate from the scan's `marketplaceFees` is known: use it for a "settled early" verdict and say the FBA fee comes with round 2. Fees are modelled on amazon.com and, from Amazon's European rate card, on amazon.co.uk, .de, .fr, .it and .es: there the margin uses the leaders' `afterFeesExVat` (VAT out, then Amazon's fees; a range scored at its lower end), quotes the fees' `confidence` and the `notModelled` entries (and the `basis` "Not included" items that apply, such as lithium battery fees), and asks the user's VAT treatment when `vatRate` is null (`references/judgement-abroad.md`, "Amazon's fees in the UK and the EU"). Elsewhere the leaders' `fees` say `supported: false`, and the margin is before fees on the before-fee thresholds (never a fee figure from memory: say it isn't modelled). **On amazon.co.uk and amazon.de, VAT comes off the price first, once** (with `afterFeesExVat`, VAT is already out: never take it off again; without fees, 20% and 19%; the margin on the net price, with the VAT step shown and the one-line registration question: `references/judgement-abroad.md`, "VAT on UK and German marketplaces"). **The fees are the user's channel's:** a TikTok Shop or own-store seller's margin uses that channel's fee, never Amazon's FBA fee, and the Amazon row in the table is labelled as Amazon's (`references/judgement.md`, "The margin follows the user's channel"). **The offer scored is the one the leaders sell** (a pack when most of them sell packs: "Score the margin at the offer the leaders sell"). Freight, duty and ads are not in it unless a landed cost was given: say so once. **An own-store seller who ships their own orders** gets an estimated shipping cost per order in their margin, from the leaders' `fees.shippingWeightLb` and the weight bands (`references/judgement-rates.md`, "Shipping in the margin"), said as an estimate with the weight used; FBA sellers don't (the FBA fee covers it). Factory prices are list prices for a first look, not a quote. → *Margin.*
- **The margin's unit cost, from checked offers:** read the supplier's offer rows (never its status message alone) with `fields: "recordType,title,url,unitPriceMin,unitPriceMax,unitPriceAtQuantity,priceTiers,moq,unit,supplierName"` (each offer's price is in `unitPriceMin` and `unitPriceMax`; `unitPriceAtQuantity` is often empty) and check each offer is the idea (`references/running-results.md`, "Read the data like an analyst"): an offer for another product, a disposable or single-use version, a part or an accessory is left out, with one line saying how many, and the median unit cost (and `unitPriceP25`) is recomputed from the offers that remain, said as recomputed. The summary's `unitPriceMedian` is used as it is only when every offer is the idea.
- **Cross-checks, one sentence each in "What it means":**
  - demand against supply: Trends direction against `under100Reviews` and the newcomers in the top 10 (rising demand with few newcomers is the open case; falling demand with many newcomers is a crowd arriving late);
  - the price umbrella: if `priceP25` is under 3× the factory `unitPriceMedian` (2× for dropship), the market already sells near cost: say so instead of quoting the margin alone;
  - seasonality against lead time: a peak month less than about 90 days away is hard to source for on a first order (allow 60 to 90 days).

## 4. Score, then judge

Read the verdict off the counts with the rule in `references/judgement.md` (Strong: margin not against, and 5+ for with at most 1 against, or 4+ for with none against; Not this version: 4+ against, or margin and concentration both against; else Promising, with one fix; 3+ unknown caps at Promising, low confidence). **Never write the verdict before the table.** The same data gets the same verdict whether the user asked "is it worth selling?" or "is it saturated?", and however much they hope for one answer.

## 5. Answer, in this order

**The first screen** (about 150 words at most; `references/judgement-answer.md`, "Answer structure"), so a user who reads only that much has the answer:

1. **The plain answer, then the verdict** (`references/judgement-first-answer.md`, "The first full answer of a check", parts 1 and 2): when the user's message asked something beyond the check itself (is a claim real, should I order, is it too late, where do I get it, what would I make), its plain answer comes first, in one to three sentences, the answer itself first, and the verdict sentence follows it (`references/judgement-answer.md`, "Answer the user's question first"). When it asked only the check's own question ("is it worth it?", "is it saturated?", "check X"), the verdict leads, with no line before it: the answer's first characters are "**For you:**" (or "**For the market:**" without a profile) (after the profile-disagreement line, when there is one: below). No heading, greeting, cost recap, "Short answer:" or "Here's what I found" before either. **Never the user's exact budget or other amount of their own money, anywhere in the answer** (not "your USD 300", "with 300 dollars", "USD 300 covers…"): only its band ("under USD 1k") or "the amount you gave me", in the For you line, the fit check and the notebook line alike. Their selling price may be quoted; their budget never. With a profile: **"For you: …"** first, with its label written exactly as one of the four (**a strong fit**, **a fit, with one fix**, **a long shot**, **not this one**: `references/judgement.md`, rule 7; never reworded), through the lens, with the profile in brackets and the fact that decides it; then **"For the market: …"** with its label, confidence and counts. When the question names a budget or channel that differs from the saved profile, the one line saying which was used comes just before (`references/judgement-profile.md`, "When the question and the profile disagree"). Without one: "For the market: …" leads. The why, in its two biggest numbers, is in the same verdict sentence or the next, written for the user's experience (`references/judgement-first-answer.md`, "Notation follows experience": plain sentences and counts in words by default; the compact counts only for a known experienced seller). When the two verdicts differ, one sentence says why (`references/judgement-for-you.md`, "Guardrails"). For Not this version, the plain line is the one time the weak version is called weak: kindly, with its numbers.
2. **What you got right:** up to two lines, each about something the user said in this conversation (a claim, their price, their cost, a reason for a pick: `references/judgement.md`, rule 5; never a signal they didn't mention, and for a TikTok or own-store seller never an Amazon FBA margin), each tied to a number from the table (the trend they expected, their price inside the band, the margin their quote gives). Leave it out when nothing they said is backed by the data.
