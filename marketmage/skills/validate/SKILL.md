# Validate a product idea

Work one idea with the user: a verdict with numbers behind it, then the next move that builds on it. Read these first:
- `references/judgement.md`: the core: the rules, the signal table, the verdict rule and the confidence. **Follow it, and each part below, exactly.** Its other parts are read only at the step that needs them: `references/judgement-tone.md` and `references/judgement-answer.md` before writing the answer; `references/judgement-ladder.md` (the related versions ladder, the watches) once the verdict is known; `references/judgement-profile.md` (the profile, asking for it once, choosing the lens) when there is a profile or it is to be offered; with a profile, once the lens is chosen, `references/judgement-for-you.md` and the chosen lens's file only (`references/judgement-lens-cold-traffic.md`, `references/judgement-lens-marketplace.md`, `references/judgement-lens-own-stock.md`, `references/judgement-lens-maker.md` or `references/judgement-lens-general.md`; plus `references/judgement-lens-goals.md` when the goal is side income or brand); `references/judgement-saturation.md` (Where you'd compete) only through the cold-traffic lens, when that read is made or offered. Never read a lens file for a lens the user isn't on. **When the first message already gives the profile** (or the chat holds one), read the core and every part this job will use (tone, answer, ladder, profile, for-you, the lens file, the goals file when the goal is set, and saturation through the cold-traffic lens) in that one first batch, with `running.md` and `costs.md`.
- `references/running.md`: costs, caps, tools, secrets, and the runs already made in this conversation.
- `references/costs.md`: the prices.

**Read once, together:** a reference already read in this conversation (by this skill or another MarketMage skill: their copies are the same text) is not read again. Read the references a step needs in one batch of parallel reads, never one after another.

**Reading a run's record** (`RUN_SUMMARY`, `DIGEST.md`): `get-key-value-store-record` with `keyValueStoreId` set to the run's `defaultKeyValueStoreId` (from `call-actor` or `get-actor-run`) and `recordKey` set to the key; never without `keyValueStoreId` (`references/running.md`, "Running").

## 1. Ask only for

- **The product,** in shoppers' words: "silicone stretch lids", "cordless pet grooming vacuum".
- **The marketplace:** from the user's words (`references/running.md`, "Country"); US by default.
- **A claim to check,** when that's the question ("my course says heated eye masks sell 500 a day at 60% margin: true?", a guru's screenshot): the same check, and the plain answer that comes first puts each claimed number beside what the data shows (the claimed sales beside the top 10's sales floor, said as a floor; the claimed margin beside the margin after fees at the market's price), without judging who made it; what this data can't see (one store's own sales, their ad costs) is named, never filled in.
- **Optional:** the price they plan to sell at, and their landed cost if they already have a supplier quote (then the supplier read is skipped).
- **Shipping from abroad, asked once:** when the stock would ship to them from abroad (they import, or say their supplier is in China or another country; a factory read is the cost source) and they gave no landed cost, ask once, in one skippable line in the message with the cost line (or, when the run went ahead without one, in the answer, beside the margin): "If you know them, your landed cost per unit, or freight and duty per unit, make the margin complete." Without them the margin is before freight and duty, and says so. When the cost and the price are in different currencies, the same line asks for their rate or a landed cost in their selling currency (`references/running.md`, "Country"). Never a freight, duty or exchange figure from memory.
- **A maker's own cost, asked once** (the Maker lens: they make what they sell, `references/judgement-lens-maker.md`): in the message with the cost line, one skippable line for their materials and packaging, the time one takes them, and, if they'd like their time counted, their hourly rate (that file, "Their unit cost: materials plus time, asked once"). Never a factory price in its place.
- **The profile, if there isn't one and it hasn't been offered in this chat** (`references/judgement-profile.md`, "Asking for the profile": once per conversation): its five quick bands go in the same message as the cost line, skippable; nothing waits on them. With a profile, pick the lens now (from fulfilment and channels: `references/judgement-profile.md`, "Choosing the lens"), then read `references/judgement-for-you.md` and that lens's file: it changes the runs (below).

Before running anything, check the runs already made in this conversation (`references/running.md`, "Runs in this conversation"): TikTok rows or a Trends record for the same keyword from `find-products`, the keyword summary from `market-scan`, an Etsy or eBay read with listing rows for the same keyword and marketplace (from `find-products` or `market-scan`), and, for the same idea, the AliExpress single-unit check and the Where you'd compete rows from `find-products`, are reused, not bought again.

## 3. Read and score

Read only these fields, then fill the signal table in `references/judgement.md`, one line per signal with its number, threshold and reading. **First check every row** (`references/running.md`, "Read the data like an analyst"): Amazon leaders and TikTok listings that aren't the idea (another product of the same brand, an accessory), multi-packs priced as one, and renewed units are left out or adjusted before scoring, and the answer says how many. The market scan's summary covers every result; if the leaders' titles show the search is mixed, say its figures include other products, and offer listing rows (about USD 0.024) to check.

- **Trends:** `summary.direction`, `changePercent`, the peak months from `interestOverTime`, and `relatedQueries_rising` (the angle rung of the related versions ladder). → *Demand trend.*
- **Market scan** (the `keyword-summary` row, in full): `salesFloor` (the top 10 organic results' "bought in past month" floor: `unitsPerMonthMin`, `revenuePerMonthMin`, `listingsWithBadge`) → *Demand level*, readable from round 1; `marketplaceFees` (Amazon US's published referral rate) → the round-1 margin; `priceP25`, `priceMedian`, `priceP75`, and `singleNewPrice` (`p25`, `median`, `p75` over single-unit new listings, with `excludedMultiPack`, `excludedOtherCondition`, `excludedUnknownCondition` and its `basis`); use the single-unit new figures for the *Price band* and the margin whenever multi-packs or other conditions were excluded, and say so (for the margin, only when single units are what sells: when most of the leaders with sales sell packs, the margin is scored at their pack, `references/judgement.md`, "Score the margin at the offer the leaders sell"); `top3SellerShare`, `sellerHhi`; `medianReviews`, `under100Reviews`; `sponsored` and `ads`. → *Review depth, Concentration, Paid pressure, Price band.* **The ads figure comes from this scan only:** the Amazon product read is organic results only (`includeSponsored` is off), so never add the two or count an ad twice.
- **Amazon leaders** (`fields: "recordType,asin,title,brand,price,rating,reviewCount,starBreakdown,rank,boughtPastMonthMin,dateFirstAvailable,soldByAmazon,fees,salesFloor,priceMedian"`):
  - each product's `fees` (amazon.com, .co.uk, .de, .fr, .it and .es): `referralFee`, `fbaFee` or `fbaFeeRange`, `totalFees` or `totalFeesRange`, `afterFees`, `feePercentOfPrice`, `sizeTier`, `missing`, `confidence`, `notModelled`; on the UK and EU marketplaces also `marketplace`, `currency`, `priceIncludesVat`, `vatRate`, `vatNote`, `afterFeesExVat` or `afterFeesExVatRange`, `period`, `basis`; on other marketplaces `supported: false` → *Margin*;
  - the `search-summary` row: `salesFloor` over the products read and the median fees (`fees.medianReferralFee`, `medianFbaFee`, `medianFbaFeeRange`, `medianFeePercentOfPrice`). When the scan's `salesFloor` was read, quote that one (the top 10 organic) and don't add the two; recompute either from the leaders' `boughtPastMonthMin` when rows were left out as not the product → *Demand level*, said as "at least N units, about USD X a month across the top 10";
  - `rating` and `starBreakdown` (the 1-star share) → *Quality gap*;
  - `soldByAmazon` on 3 or more → *Concentration* `against` (Amazon sells it itself);
  - `dateFirstAvailable` within the last 12 months on 3 or more → newcomers are getting in (*Review depth*);
  - `rank` (Best Sellers Rank) is a category-relative proxy: quote it, but don't score it on its own.
  - The scan's sellers are brands read from titles; the product read's `brand` is from the page. Say they come from different reads if they disagree.
- **TikTok Shop (US):** `unitsSold` (lifetime) and `price` of the top 5 rows that are the idea, and the keyword summary's `relevantToKeyword` totals. Each row's `relevantToKeyword` is the actor's title check: confirm it against the title before counting the row. → *Demand level.* Lifetime units, not per day: say so. The summary's `marketplaceFees` (TikTok Shop US's published referral rate for most categories, before creator commissions, with its `asOf` and `sources`) gives the channel rung its after-fee margin when TikTok units back it, and a TikTok Shop seller's own margin: quote the figure this read returned; if the rows read didn't include the summary, read it again (free) before saying the fee is unknown, and never fill it from memory (`references/judgement.md`, "Every fee and rate has a source, never memory").
- **Their marketplace** (Marketplace lens on Etsy or eBay): its `keyword-summary` row (`source` etsy or ebay): `sellers`, `priceP25`, `priceMedian`, `priceP75` (on eBay, `singleNewPrice` when it left listings out; never on Etsy, which states no condition), `medianReviews` with `reviewScope` (the shop's or the seller's, not the product's), `marketplaceFees`; and the listing rows with `fields: "recordType,source,position,title,price,seller,reviewCount,itemReviews,quantitySold,listedAt,condition,packCount,url"`, each checked against the title as above. → the For you rows of `references/judgement-lens-marketplace.md` (Concentration on their marketplace: the top 3 sellers' share of the listing rows that are the product, with 10 or more such rows, else `unknown`; never the summary's `top3SellerShare` or `sellerHhi`, which cover every organic result; Price band; Margin after its fees; Demand level `unknown`, with Amazon's floor as a labelled context line and `itemReviews` or `quantitySold` as information). Label every figure with its marketplace; never call Amazon's floor Etsy or eBay demand.
- **Where you'd compete** (cold-traffic lens; the `advertiser` rows: `advertiser`, `daysRunning`, `earliestFirstShown`, `adsKept`, `landingDomain`, `sampleAdUrl`): leave out an advertiser whose name and landing domain plainly sell something else (the keyword in an unrelated ad) and say how many; then count the active advertisers, the proven ones (`daysRunning` 35 or more) and the newcomers (14 or fewer), and say "at least" when the read filled its 30 ads. Label the counts with the library and the market country wherever they appear ("14 active advertisers on Meta's US ads"): the coverage line, said once. From the TikTok rows already read: distinct `shopName` among the relevant top 20, and the top 3 shops' share of their units. → the table's *Where you'd compete* row (counts only, not scored for the market), read through the lens (`references/judgement-saturation.md`, "Where you'd compete"). The wave read, when made: its `waveSummary` row and its product rows, each match confirmed against the idea, said as "N of M stores advertising it added it in the last 30 days" with the dates (different products matched by the term said apart; unreadable and not-Shopify stores counted apart; `partial` said); a store wave is not the late wave. One line in the fit check, read with the Where you'd compete reading, never counted, never a gate ("The wave read").
- **Margin, after fees** (`references/judgement.md`, "Margin after fees"): the selling price is the user's, or else the market's `priceMedian` (say which); the unit cost is the supplier summary's `unitPriceMedian` (and `unitPriceP25`, at `moqMedian`), or the user's quote. Margin = (what a sale pays out after Amazon's referral and FBA fees − unit cost) ÷ price, with the arithmetic in one line. In round 1 only the referral rate from the scan's `marketplaceFees` is known: use it for a "settled early" verdict and say the FBA fee comes with round 2. Fees are modelled on amazon.com and, from Amazon's European rate card, on amazon.co.uk, .de, .fr, .it and .es: there the margin uses the leaders' `afterFeesExVat` (VAT out, then Amazon's fees; a range scored at its lower end), quotes the fees' `confidence` and the `notModelled` entries (and the `basis` "Not included" items that apply, such as lithium battery fees), and asks the user's VAT treatment when `vatRate` is null (`references/judgement.md`, "Amazon's fees in the UK and the EU"). Elsewhere the leaders' `fees` say `supported: false`, and the margin is before fees on the before-fee thresholds (never a fee figure from memory: say it isn't modelled). **On amazon.co.uk and amazon.de, VAT comes off the price first, once** (with `afterFeesExVat`, VAT is already out: never take it off again; without fees, 20% and 19%; the margin on the net price, with the VAT step shown and the one-line registration question: `references/judgement.md`, "VAT on UK and German marketplaces"). **The fees are the user's channel's:** a TikTok Shop or own-store seller's margin uses that channel's fee, never Amazon's FBA fee, and the Amazon row in the table is labelled as Amazon's (`references/judgement.md`, "The margin follows the user's channel"). **The offer scored is the one the leaders sell** (a pack when most of them sell packs: "Score the margin at the offer the leaders sell"). Freight, duty and ads are not in it unless a landed cost was given: say so once. Factory prices are list prices for a first look, not a quote. → *Margin.*
- **Cross-checks, one sentence each in "What it means":**
  - demand against supply: Trends direction against `under100Reviews` and the newcomers in the top 10 (rising demand with few newcomers is the open case; falling demand with many newcomers is a crowd arriving late);
  - the price umbrella: if `priceP25` is under 3× the factory `unitPriceMedian` (2× for dropship), the market already sells near cost: say so instead of quoting the margin alone;
  - seasonality against lead time: a peak month less than about 90 days away is hard to source for on a first order (allow 60 to 90 days).

## 4. Score, then judge

Read the verdict off the counts with the rule in `references/judgement.md` (Strong: margin not against, and 5+ for with at most 1 against, or 4+ for with none against; Not this version: 4+ against, or margin and concentration both against; else Promising, with one fix; 3+ unknown caps at Promising, low confidence). **Never write the verdict before the table.** The same data gets the same verdict whether the user asked "is it worth selling?" or "is it saturated?", and however much they hope for one answer.

## 5. Answer, in this order

**The first screen** (about 150 words at most; `references/judgement-answer.md`, "Answer structure"), so a user who reads only that much has the answer:

1. **Verdicts, as the very first lines:** the answer's first characters are "**For you:**" (or "**For the market:**" without a profile), except when the user's message asked a direct question that the check itself doesn't answer (a question the check answers, such as "is it worth it?" or "is it saturated?", is answered by the verdict lines themselves, which still lead with no line before them): then its plain answer comes first, in one to three sentences, and the verdict lines follow it (`references/judgement-tone.md`, "Answer the user's question first"). Otherwise no heading, greeting, cost recap, "Short answer:", "Here's what I found" or any other line before them. **Never the user's exact budget or other amount of their own money, anywhere in the answer** (not "your USD 300", "with 300 dollars", "USD 300 covers…"): only its band ("under USD 1k") or "the amount you gave me", in the For you line, the fit check and the notebook line alike. Their selling price may be quoted; their budget never. With a profile: **"For you: …"** first, with its label written exactly as one of the four (**a strong fit**, **a fit, with one fix**, **a long shot**, **not this one**: `references/judgement.md`, rule 7; never reworded), through the lens, with the profile in brackets and the fact that decides it; then **"For the market: …"** with its label, confidence and counts. When the question names a budget or channel that differs from the saved profile, the one line saying which was used comes just before (`references/judgement-profile.md`, "When the question and the profile disagree"). Without one: "For the market: …" leads. Then one plain line on why, in its two biggest numbers. When the two verdicts differ, one sentence says why (`references/judgement-for-you.md`, "Guardrails"). For Not this version, the plain line is the one time the weak version is called weak: kindly, with its numbers.
2. **What you got right:** up to two lines, each about something the user said in this conversation (a claim, their price, their cost, a reason for a pick: `references/judgement.md`, rule 5; never a signal they didn't mention, and for a TikTok or own-store seller never an Amazon FBA margin), each tied to a number from the table (the trend they expected, their price inside the band, the margin their quote gives). Leave it out when nothing they said is backed by the data.
3. **The move in one line:** Promising, the one fix; Not this version, the best related version with the fact that makes it promising; Strong, what would need to stay true; **For you: a long shot**, this line instead, on the first screen: "What would have to be true: <one line>. The cheapest test: <the move>, about USD <cost>." When the market reads Promising, its one fix still gets one line after it ("The market's one fix: <it>"), so neither label is left without its move. The long-shot verdict line itself names both thin demand numbers read (the top TikTok listing's units and the Amazon floor), so it's clear demand is thin in both, not only on one.
4. **Without a profile,** one friendly line offering it (5 quick questions, or skip) only when it wasn't offered earlier in this chat, the cost line included (`references/judgement-profile.md`, "Asking for the profile"); never a re-offer.
5. **Next step:** the one move, costed: the best related version's check (for example `validate` on it, cheaper when it reuses these rows, or a `market-scan` summary, about USD 0.017), the fix's test (the leaders' complaints, about USD 0.03; `suppliers` at the user's order quantity, about USD 0.025), or, after a Strong verdict, the watch. After Not this version (or For you: not this one), it may be the free stop instead, "Next step: nothing to buy; keep the USD <X>." (X: their quote's first order or the first order at the medians; for a maker, the batch they'd make), with the best related version kept in "Build on it" with its cost (`references/judgement-tone.md`, "A free 'stop here' is a next move too").

**Then the detail, under it:**

6. **The signal table** (under 10 rows), the same for everyone (its Margin row labelled with the fees it uses, "after Amazon's fees" or "before fees", and on amazon.co.uk or amazon.de with VAT taken off); through the cold-traffic lens it carries the Where you'd compete counts as a row of facts, with no market reading.
7. **What it means (for any seller):** two sentences, with the cross-checks.
8. **For you, through the lens:** only with a profile (`references/judgement-for-you.md`, "The For you block"): the lens lines (each row that counts differently for them; each gate with the fact read in this run that settles it, "unclear" when that fact wasn't read; notes as information, said once and capping only when three or more point the same way; risk notes in one neutral line), then the **fit check**: first order against the band's upper edge, or, when they named an exact amount in this chat, against that amount privately, said as "fits" or "doesn't fit the amount you gave me" (`references/judgement-lens-own-stock.md`, "Against the amount you gave me") (units × unit or landed cost; AliExpress prices labelled "before shipping"), fulfilment against the product (the leaders' weight and size tier from their `fees`, labelled as the leaders', else "weight not read"; never invented freight), ad cost against the band (ads in 48, or for cold traffic the margin at USD 8 and USD 12 of ads per sale, stated as an assumption, with the Where you'd compete counts and their reading for this profile, thresholds beside the numbers, then the wave read's line when it was made), and the skills needed. Outside knowledge beyond the lens's gates and risk notes never goes here.
9. **Build on it:**
   - **Strong:** what would need to stay true, in one line (already on the first screen: don't repeat it here);
   - **Promising, with one fix:** the one fix's test, with the cost, then 1 or 2 related versions;
   - **Not this version:** 2 or 3 related versions in the user's direction, in the ladder's order (angle from `relatedQueries_rising`, segment above `priceP75` with a real difference, bundle, marketplace, channel), each with the fact from these rows that makes it promising, and the best one offered for a check now.
10. **The early sign and its watch:** the biggest risk, phrased as "the early sign would be …" with today's number, and the watch that catches it, with its cost (`references/judgement-ladder.md`, "Watches"). Offer only; never create a watch without a yes.
11. **One line on the opposite of the user's framing**, from the data (not a policy or rule already named) ("Asked 'is it saturated': the opening a newcomer still has is …").
12. **What this can't see,** in one line, only for the sources read (`references/judgement.md`, rule 9).
13. **What this research cost,** from the runs' charged events.
14. **The notebook** (`references/notebook.md`): if the user keeps one, note the idea (the market verdict, counts, key numbers; its `note` starts `stage: validating · ` and carries the For you label: `references/notebook-use.md`, "Journey stages") and any related version checked, and save a new or changed profile (`upsertProfile`, bands only); say "Noted in your notebook" in one line. If they haven't been asked yet in this chat, ask once, in one line. After Strong or Promising, the launch plan (`launch-plan`) is one of the follow-ups, with the cost of its first paid step.
15. **Next step, last:** step 5's move again, in one short line, so the answer ends on it.

Voice: hopeful, curious, plain-spoken (`references/judgement-tone.md`, "Tone"). Warm when the numbers support it; kind and brief when they don't; never doom, never empty praise, never a compliment about the person.
