# Price check

Answer "what does it cost, where, right now" in one run. Read `references/running.md` first (costs, caps, tools, secrets). Prices are in `references/costs.md`.

**Read once, together:** a reference already read in this conversation (by this skill or another MarketMage skill: their copies are the same text) is not read again. Read the references a step needs in one batch of parallel reads, never one after another.

**Reading a run's record** (`RUN_SUMMARY`, `DIGEST.md`): `get-key-value-store-record` with `keyValueStoreId` set to the run's `defaultKeyValueStoreId` (from `call-actor` or `get-actor-run`) and `recordKey` set to the key; never without `keyValueStoreId` (`references/running.md`, "Running").

## 1. Ask only for

The products, in the form the user has them:

| What they have | Actor | Input | Cost on paid plans |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barcodes (UPC, EAN, GTIN) | `cleanpull/barcode-price-checker` | `products: ["<barcode>", …]`; defaults search Amazon, Best Buy and Google Shopping (Walmart, Target, and other stores by name) | per barcode: USD 0.003 + 3 Google searches × USD 0.004 ≈ **USD 0.015**. A barcode no store knows still costs its searches: one web search for its name, then the Shopping searches, up to 4 × USD 0.004 ≈ **USD 0.016** with no product row |
| Product page URLs (any shop) | `cleanpull/any-store-product-monitor` | `productUrls: [...]`, `includeVariants: true` | per page **USD 0.0015** (protected stores USD 0.004). Walmart, Target, Best Buy, eBay and Etsy pages: + USD 0.003 each and USD 0.015 per marketplace per run, through CleanPull's data service (seconds to a few minutes) |
| Amazon ASINs or URLs | `cleanpull/amazon-product-scraper` | `asins: [...]`, `marketplace`; `includeOffers: true` for every seller | per product **USD 0.003**, + USD 0.001 with offers |

- **Exact matches only, cheaper:** with barcodes, `sources: ["amazon", "bestbuy"]` skips the Google Shopping searches: USD 0.003 a barcode found, so 10 barcodes ≈ USD 0.03 instead of 0.15. Offer it when the user needs stores that match the barcode exactly, not every store's price.
- **Optional:** the user's own price, to show where they stand. For barcodes it goes in `myPrices: {"<barcode>": price}`.
- **Target and Best Buy by barcode, exactly** (optional, `serviceStores: ["target", "bestbuy"]`): each store's own record must state the same barcode. USD 0.02 per store per run + USD 0.003 per barcode it sells, and the run takes **3 to 5 minutes**: offer it when the user needs those two stores for sure, and say the wait first.
- **A user outside the US** (`references/running.md`, "Country"): the barcode check's stores are US stores (Amazon US, Best Buy, Google Shopping US) and Walmart, Target, Best Buy, eBay and Etsy pages are the US sites: say "these read the US sites (US prices and sellers, in US dollars)" in the cost line, before the run, even under USD 0.05. Their own Amazon marketplace is read by barcode with `cleanpull/amazon-product-scraper` (`barcodes: [...]`, `marketplace`), USD 0.003 a barcode plus USD 0.003 local-market outside the US: offer it as the read of their own market.
- **eBay links from Europe and the UK:** an ebay.de, ebay.co.uk or ebay.es item link is read as the same item on ebay.com (Any Store Product Monitor 0.2.5), at the eBay page price above; its price keeps the listing's own currency, never converted. eBay sold prices aren't available: only the current listing price.
- **Before the run:** state the estimate. Run straight away if it is under USD 0.05; above that, ask first.
- **Say the wait first, even under USD 0.05:** Walmart, Target, Best Buy, eBay and Etsy pages take from a few seconds to a few minutes, and Target and Best Buy by barcode 3 to 5 minutes (`references/running.md`, "Timing"). Say so before starting, so the user isn't left waiting without warning.
- **Many new barcodes:** say that a barcode no store knows still costs about USD 0.016, so 50 new EANs cost about USD 0.80 even if few are found.

## 2. Read the results

- **Barcode records:**
  - `lowestNewPrice` and `lowestNewPriceStore` (confirmed-new offers only), and `lowestPrice`, `lowestPriceStore` and `lowestPriceCondition` (every offer kept, whatever its condition); `highestPrice`, `priceSpreadPercent`; `conditionCounts` (offers by condition);
  - **The headline is `lowestNewPrice`** whenever `lowestPriceCondition` isn't `new`: "lowest new price USD 279.99 at Best Buy; a renewed unit is USD 184.99 at StackSocial".
  - **Except for a seller of renewed, used or refurbished stock** (they say they sell renewed, refurbished, used or open-box): their condition leads. Pass their condition with new in the barcode run's `conditions` (for example `["new", "renewed"]` for a refurbished seller, `["new", "used"]` or `["new", "open-box"]`; the default reads new only), then headline that condition's cheapest offer and where, and its range (lowest to highest, with how many offers), with the lowest new price as the reference line under it ("renewed from USD 184.99 at StackSocial, 3 renewed offers up to USD 219; new from USD 279.99 at Best Buy"). Offers in another used-type condition stay on their own line, and `unknown` is still never counted as theirs or as new.
  - `storeCount` and `offerCount`;
  - `offers`: read them all, with `store`, `seller`, `price`, `condition` (`new`, `renewed`, `used`, `open-box` or `unknown`), `conditionBasis` (`label`, `title`, `listing`, `unlabelled`, `store-listing` or `none`: where the condition came from), `title`, `offerType` and the link (`url`): with `urlSource: "store"` it is the store page, with `"google"` it is Google's listing of the offer (Google gave no store link), so call it "store page" or "Google's listing" to match; a Google Shopping offer also carries `googleUrl`, Google's listing, as a second link. `isInternationalVersion: true` (the title says International, Global Version or Intl) is a unit made for another country, where warranty and fittings may differ: say so beside that offer, as a clue for the user. A Best Buy offer's `offerCount` is 1 (each Best Buy SKU has one seller); its `familyOfferCount` counts the other SKUs Best Buy groups with it (marketplace, refurbished), not sellers of this one. And, for listings set aside (`setAside`, with `includeSetAside`), `flag`, the actor's reason (for example "condition renewed", "store outside the US", "other variant (…)", "bundle or accessory (…)"): mention it when you say what was left out.
  - **Verify the clues against the titles** (`references/running.md`, "Read the data like an analyst"): the condition fields are the actor's reading; a title it missed (a bundle, a case, another model) still decides. `unknown` is never new: give those offers as "condition not stated; check before relying on it", never as the lowest new price. An offer with `conditionBasis: "unlabelled"` (Google, no label and no condition word) is new by Google's convention.
  - Runs from before these fields (no `lowestNewPrice`): read `condition` and the titles as above and work out the lowest new price yourself;
  - with `myPrices`, how many offers undercut it.
  - `sources` says which source found nothing.
  - **An Amazon offer is the listing's featured (Buy Box) price, not its cheapest:** an offer with `store: "Amazon"`, `offerType: "listing"` and `offerCount` above 1 or `null` (`null` means the count isn't known, never one seller) means other sellers on that listing (`storeId`, the ASIN) may charge less, new or used. Before calling any price "the cheapest" when that Amazon offer is the lowest new one (or within about 20% of it), **read the listing's offers:** `cleanpull/amazon-product-scraper` with `asins: [<storeId>]`, the same `marketplace` and `includeOffers: true` (with `maxOffers: 30` when the count is `null`), about **USD 0.004** an ASIN. Run it straight away, without asking, while the barcode run and it stay under USD 0.05 in total (up to about 8 such ASINs); for more, give the cost and ask. Then the answer uses each offer's `price`, `condition` (`New`, `Used - Like New`, `Refurbished`…), `seller` and `buyBox`, with the same rules as the barcode offers (a used or refurbished offer goes in its own line, never as the lowest new price).
  - **When the offers aren't read** (the user said no, or too many ASINs), label that price **"Amazon's Buy Box price"**, never "the cheapest", and add in one line that the listing has `offerCount` sellers whose prices weren't read, with the read's cost: "Read the other 2 sellers' prices? (about USD 0.004)"; when `offerCount` is `null`, say the number of sellers isn't shown: "Read the other sellers' prices on this listing? (about USD 0.004)".
- **Store pages:** `price`, `currency`, `inStock`, `onSale` (compare-at price) and variant prices.
  - A page that couldn't be read has no row. Its reason is in `RUN_SUMMARY.failures`, for example a page that publishes no product data or a store that couldn't be read. Say so. Marketplace pages (Walmart, Target, Best Buy, eBay, Etsy) have no variants.
- **Amazon:** `price` is the Buy Box price; `buyBoxSeller`, `rank`, `offerCount` and `lowestOfferPrice` (the lowest of all sellers' offers, **any condition**: never the lowest new price on its own) and, with offers (`includeOffers: true`), each seller's `price`, `condition`, `seller`, `buyBox` and FBA. When the user asks for the cheapest price and `offerCount` is above 1, include the offers in the run (USD 0.001 more an ASIN), or read them as above before naming the cheapest.

## Reselling: one flip line per UPC

**When it applies:** the user gives a buy cost ("clearance 14.97", a receipt list with costs) or says flip, resell, arbitrage or "sell it on Amazon". They want what a unit pays them on Amazon, not who sells it cheapest.

- **The read:** `cleanpull/amazon-product-scraper` with `barcodes: [...]` (or `asins` when they have them), their `marketplace` (US unless they say), and `includeOffers: true`: about **USD 0.004 a UPC** (product USD 0.003 + offers USD 0.001; outside the US + USD 0.003 local-market), so 40 UPCs ≈ USD 0.16 (ask first above USD 0.05). It goes straight to the Amazon listing, without the Google Shopping searches. Offer the full barcode check (every store's price, about USD 0.015 a UPC) only as a follow-up, when they also want where else to buy it.
- **Read** each product row's `price` (the Buy Box price), `buyBoxSeller`, `fees` (`afterFees` or `afterFeesRange`, `referralFee`, `fbaFee` or `fbaFeeRange`, `sizeTier`, `missing`, `confidence`, `notModelled`; on amazon.co.uk, .de, .fr, .it and .es also `currency`, `vatRate`, `afterFeesExVat` or `afterFeesExVatRange`; `supported: false` on other marketplaces), `boughtPastMonth` and `boughtPastMonthMin`, `soldByAmazon`, `offerCount`, `rank` and `rankCategory`, `barcodeVerified`, and the `offers` (`seller`, `price`, `condition`, `fulfilledByAmazon`, `soldByAmazon`, `buyBox`). Check the title against what they hold (another pack size, an "International Version", a bundle: say so, and don't work the line on it). `barcodeVerified: null` means the page shows no barcode (matched by Amazon's search only): flag it as an unconfirmed match. A page naming another code isn't delivered (the run's `RUN_SUMMARY` lists it): mention it with the UPCs not found.
- **One line per UPC**, in a small table when there are several (sorted by ROI, best first), each figure from the row:
  - **Buy Box** price and who has it;
  - **pays out after Amazon's fees:** `fees.afterFees` (the price less the referral and FBA fees, from Amazon's published US schedule), with the fee split in a few words; a range when only `afterFeesRange` exists (work the profit and ROI on the lower end, and say it's a range). On amazon.co.uk, .de, .fr, .it and .es, what a sale pays out is `fees.afterFeesExVat` (the price without VAT, at the row's `vatRate`, less Amazon's fees; work the profit on the lower end of `afterFeesExVatRange`), never `afterFees`, which still has the VAT in it; the VAT step shown ("£24.99 includes £4.17 VAT (20%), so £20.82"), assuming they're VAT-registered and saying so in one line, as a question if they haven't said. When `vatRate` is null (a category that often takes a reduced rate), ask which VAT rate their product takes and whether they're registered, and until then work it at the standard rate, labelled. Beside the figure, the fees' `confidence` and any `notModelled` entry in a few words. On other marketplaces outside the US, `fees` says `supported: false`: "Amazon's fees for <marketplace> aren't in this data; Amazon's fee calculator gives them", and the profit is before fees, labelled. Never a fee from memory;
  - **profit and ROI on their cost:** profit = what it pays out − their buy cost; ROI = profit ÷ their buy cost, as a percentage ("pays out USD 24.12; − your USD 14.97 = USD 9.15 profit, 61% ROI"). Before inbound shipping, prep and any sales tax they paid: say so once, under the table. Their cost in another currency than the price: never subtracted across currencies (`references/running.md`, "Country");
  - **sold last month:** `boughtPastMonth` as Amazon shows it ("1K+ bought in past month"), said as a floor, or "no badge shown" (never a forecast), and the Best Sellers Rank with its category beside it as a proxy;
  - **Amazon on the listing:** yes when `soldByAmazon` is true or an offer's seller is Amazon (`soldByAmazon` on the offer), else no;
  - **offers:** `offerCount` sellers, and how many of the offers read are FBA (`fulfilledByAmazon`), with the lowest new FBA offer's price when it is below the Buy Box.
- **Match confidence, per line:** **high** when `barcodeVerified` is true and the title is the item they hold; **medium** when the page shows no barcode (`barcodeVerified: null`, matched by Amazon's search) or the title leaves a doubt. **A title that says "International Version" (or "Intl Version", "Import", "Global Version", "No Warranty" beside a version word) is at most medium, whatever `barcodeVerified`, a barcode store's `confidence` or any other field says** (a confidence of 1 included): the listing may be a different region's unit, with another warranty, plug or model number, and its Buy Box is not the domestic one's. Name it in the line ("match: medium, an 'International Version' listing"), and, as above, don't work the profit on it until they say their unit is that version; when they confirm, the line is worked and stays labelled medium. Offer the domestic listing as the read that settles it (the same barcode's other Amazon listing, or a search by the model name, about USD 0.004). The same cap holds for an Amazon offer in a barcode check's results (`cleanpull/barcode-price-checker`).
- **Gated or hazmat, as a neutral note** (one line under the table, only for the lines it fits; never a verdict, never "skip"): from the title and `rankCategory`, name when the category or brand is one Amazon often asks new sellers to be approved for, or the product is often classed as dangerous goods (hazmat) for FBA, with Amazon's own pages as the source:
  - **approval:** some categories and some brands need approval before a seller can list them (Amazon's reseller guide names, for example, Collectible Coins, Entertainment Collectibles, Fine Art and Sports Collectibles, https://sell.amazon.com/blog/reselling); "if the product requires approval, you'll see an Apply to sell button" when adding it in Seller Central, and a brand may need a brand application (https://sell.amazon.com/blog/sell-branded-products-on-amazon). Whether their account is approved isn't in this data: "check with Add a Product in Seller Central";
  - **hazmat:** Amazon's dangerous-goods guide lists, among common examples, aerosols (cooking sprays, whipped cream, deodorant sprays, pressurised shaving cream), perfumes, products with lithium batteries, hand sanitiser, nail polish, essential oils, strong glues, pool chemicals and flea and tick treatments (https://sell.amazon.com/blog/amazon-hazmat-dangerous-goods, 4 Jan 2025); such listings go through Amazon's dangerous-goods review before FBA accepts them.
  Say it as "often gated" or "often hazmat for FBA: check before buying", never as a fact about their account, and only when the title or category points to it.
- **A clearance price target, one line per UPC** (when they asked about a clearance or sale price, or are deciding whether to wait): the most they could pay and still meet their return: **their ROI target** when they gave one (target buy price = what it pays out ÷ (1 + their target): "for your 50% ROI: buy at USD 11.17 or less"), else the **break-even buy price** (what it pays out after fees, before shipping and prep: "breaks even at USD 16.76") with one line asking their target. When the store's page is known (a Walmart, Target or Home Depot product URL they give, or from the barcode check), offer a **price watch on that page** that alerts when the price falls to the target: `price-watch` with `alertPriceDropPercent` set to the drop from today's price to the target, rounded down to a whole percent, so it fires at or just above the target, never after it ("today USD 14.97; an alert at a 25% drop fires at USD 11.23 or less, just above your USD 11.17"), with its cost from that skill.
- **No buy or skip call is made for them:** the line gives the numbers a reseller decides on, and one plain line under the table names what stands out (the best ROI, a listing Amazon sells itself, one with no sales badge, one whose fees are a range). The user's own rule (a minimum ROI, a minimum profit) is applied when they give one, labelled as theirs.

## 3. Answer

- **The visual moment, just above the table** (`references/running.md`, "Explaining results": "One visual, where it helps"; only where you can draw one, and otherwise nothing about it is said): **the price spread**, one dot per offer in the table (`offers`: `store` and `price`, after the title checks) on one price line in the offers' currency, each dot labelled with its store and price; the `lowestNewPrice` dot called out by name; renewed, used or open-box offers drawn hollow and labelled with their `condition`, `unknown` ones labelled "condition not stated"; the user's price (`myPrices`) or buy cost as a marked vertical line, labelled "your price" or "your cost". With 2 to 5 barcodes, one row per product in the same visual; over 5, or for the reselling lines (one listing each), no visual. Caption: the sources read and the check time ("Google Shopping, Best Buy and Amazon US, read 6 Nov").
- **A small table:** product, lowest **new** price and where (for a seller of renewed, used or refurbished stock, their condition's lowest price and where first, then the lowest new price as the reference column), highest, the spread, and the user's position if given. Renewed, used or open-box offers go in their own line or column, never as the lowest price. The headline says "cheapest" only after every seller on the matched Amazon listings was read; otherwise it names the Buy Box price as such.
- **One plain line on what was set aside**, for example "the USD 184.99 StackSocial offer is a renewed unit; the lowest new price is USD 279.99 at Best Buy" or "2 offers were for the case, not the headphones, and were left out".
- **Then one line** on what stands out: a seller far below the rest, a store out of stock, the Buy Box not with the cheapest seller.
- **Offer the next step:** `price-watch` to be told when these move, or `map-watch` for barcodes with a MAP.
